From Agreement to Alert: A Temperature Timeline

How global temperatures have escalated since the 2015 Paris Agreement

2015
Paris Agreement Signed
195 countries agreed to limit warming to "well below 2°C" and pursue 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels. Long-term warming at that point was roughly 0.87°C.
2023
Hottest Year in 100,000 Years
2023 became the warmest year since modern records began — and, according to paleoclimate data, in over 100,000 years. Every month of 2023 set a new monthly record.
2024
Record Broken Again — 1.28°C Above Baseline
2024 surpassed 2023. NASA's GISTEMP placed global temperatures at 1.28°C above the 20th-century average. Fifteen consecutive months of monthly records ended in August 2024.
2025
Third Consecutive Record Year — 1.19°C
2025 was cooler than 2024 but still the third warmest year in 146 years of records. Ocean heat content hit a new all-time high. Rate of ocean warming more than doubled versus 1960–2005.
May 28, 2026
WMO Issues Landmark 2026–2035 Update
91% probability of temporary 1.5°C breach. 75% probability that the full five-year average (2026–2030) will exceed 1.5°C. Long-term warming now estimated at 1.34–1.41°C above the 1850–1900 baseline.

📌 Less Reported
More than 91% of the excess heat from greenhouse gases is stored in the world's oceans — not the atmosphere. Ocean heat content reached a new all-time record high in 2025, with its rate of warming having more than doubled since 2005. The temperature anomalies in climate headlines reflect only what is happening at the surface.
