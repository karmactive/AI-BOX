# CBS News Correspondent Faces Sexual Harassment Lawsuit From Former ABC Coworker

Matt Gutman seemed to have the job every journalist dreams of. In January 2026, the 48-year-old correspondent was hired by CBS News as chief correspondent—the first on-air hire under newly installed news chief Bari Weiss. But just seven months into his new role, Gutman faces serious allegations. Former ABC News field producer Samira Said sued him on August 14, 2026, alleging he created a hostile work environment by making inappropriate sexual comments and reducing her female colleagues' professional standing to physical appearance ratings. The lawsuit raises uncomfortable questions about institutional vetting, newsroom culture, and whether CBS News adequately screened a prominent hire with a documented history of professional controversies.

## Who Is Samira Said? The Plaintiff's Story

Samira Said worked as a field producer at ABC News from May 2021 through March 2025—four years in a role that typically involves coordinating logistics, managing crews, and supporting on-air talent. As a field producer in the Los Angeles bureau, Said worked closely with correspondents, including Matt Gutman during his tenure at ABC News. The position placed her in direct working relationships with on-air talent, creating hierarchical power dynamics typical of newsroom structures: producers facilitate reporters' work but remain behind-the-scenes, while on-air correspondents receive public recognition and professional status.

Said's lawsuit claims that Gutman exploited these power dynamics to create a hostile work environment. She was terminated in March 2025, months before Gutman left ABC News and eight months before joining CBS News.

## The Specific Allegations: Appearance Ratings and Sexualized Comments

The lawsuit details specific incidents. According to the filing, Gutman made comments reducing female colleagues to numerical appearance ratings. Said alleges he verbally scored female correspondents' appearance on numbered scales, making comments designed to rank their attractiveness as central to their professional value. Beyond appearance ratings, the lawsuit claims Gutman made sexualized comments to Said and other female colleagues, creating an environment where his personal commentary on women's bodies became normalized workplace behavior.

The allegations characterize the behavior as creating a "male-only team" dynamic within the broader newsroom—a pattern where Gutman favored male crew members and correspondents while subjecting female colleagues to sexualized commentary. Said alleges this environment impacted her ability to work effectively and ultimately contributed to decisions affecting her employment status.

## Who Is Matt Gutman? Career History and Prior Controversies

Gutman spent 17 years at ABC News, rising to the position of chief national correspondent. His career included major story coverage and high-profile assignments. Yet his professional history includes documented controversies that raise credibility questions.

In 2020, Gutman was suspended for misreporting details about the Kobe Bryant helicopter crash. His reporting initially contained inaccuracies that prompted suspension and correction. The incident became a benchmark case in discussions about fact-checking standards in broadcast journalism. Years earlier, Gutman faced accusations of embellishing details in a Venezuela prisoner story—claims he disputed but which contributed to questions about his accuracy standards.

His move to CBS News in January 2026 represented a career reset: a prominent hire at a major network, beginning what appeared to be a new chapter. The August 2026 harassment allegations transformed that perception.

## Prior Controversies: A Pattern or Isolated Incidents?

The timing of Gutman's CBS hire and the subsequent lawsuit raises a critical question: should CBS News have discovered evidence of alleged harassment during vetting? The Kobe Bryant incident was well-documented and public; the Venezuela story concerns were industry knowledge; and allegations of professional misconduct could have been discoverable through reference checks and industry conversations.

The lawsuit suggests CBS News either failed to conduct adequate due diligence or chose to overlook red flags. Bari Weiss, who made the hiring decision, had not previously worked in broadcast news management. Her hiring of Gutman as her first on-air talent hire became immediately controversial when the harassment allegations emerged.

The question of pattern versus isolated incidents is crucial. If Gutman's harassment of female colleagues is established as fact, it suggests a pattern of behavior spanning years and multiple professional contexts. If allegations can be resolved as misunderstandings or isolated incidents, the pattern question collapses. The lawsuit will determine which characterization is accurate.

## Newsroom Culture at ABC: Institutional Context

The lawsuit names ABC News and Disney (ABC's parent company) as defendants, alongside Gutman. This institutional naming suggests the case addresses not just individual behavior but organizational responsibility. Did ABC News know or should it have known about Gutman's conduct? Did the network create environments where such behavior could persist? Were complaint procedures available and effective?

ABC News has faced multiple harassment allegations in recent years, contributing to a broader industry conversation about newsroom culture in broadcast news. The Gutman allegations emerge within this context of institutional accountability questions.

## Legal Claims and Institutional Liability

The lawsuit asserts multiple legal claims: sexual harassment (unwelcome conduct based on sex creating hostile work environment), hostile work environment (severe and pervasive conduct altering terms of employment), gender discrimination, and retaliation for protected activity. Said also alleges wrongful termination—that her employment ended as retaliation for objecting to Gutman's conduct or for reporting it through proper channels.

The named defendants—Gutman, ABC News, and Disney—face potential liability exposure. Disney, as the corporate parent, could face damages if courts determine ABC News failed to maintain reasonable workplace protections. Gutman faces personal liability for the alleged conduct. ABC News faces institutional liability for failing to prevent or respond adequately to the harassment.
