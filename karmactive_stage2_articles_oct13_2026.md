Commissioner Bisignano's 90-year solvency claim is a political statement, not a new funding mechanism. Congress has held hearings on switching the benchmark to CPI-E, the Consumer Price Index for the Elderly, which weights medical and housing costs more heavily than the standard CPI-W. No legislation has passed to make that change.

The legal divide runs between two federal statutes. If the court sides with the administration and draws a bright line protecting advisory-status bodies regardless of how much operational influence they wield, future administrations gain a clear template for structuring powerful advisory commissions beyond public-records reach.

The case's implications extend past DOGE. It directly challenges the modern executive practice of bringing in private-sector figures as special advisors who exercise sweeping influence over agency operations.
