## STEP 2 — What others are covering vs. what's missing

**What most coverage does (Indian news wires/regional outlets):** report each region as a separate, siloed story — Assam flood numbers, Uttarakhand Yatra disruption, Mumbai rain alert — usually without connecting them, and rarely cite the IMD's own seasonal forecast or academic research on *why* rainfall is behaving this way.

**Missing points identified and integrated into the article:**
1. **The forecast-vs-reality contrast** — IMD's own May 29, 2026 Long Range Forecast predicted a *below-normal* monsoon (90% of LPA) nationally, yet three regions are seeing severe flooding/landslide activity simultaneously. This is rarely explained in daily spot coverage.
2. **The scientific "why"** — IITM Pune's peer-reviewed finding (Roxy Mathew Koll et al., *Nature Communications*, 2017; maintained on the lab's own site) that widespread extreme-rainfall days over central India have tripled since 1950, and India's flood losses run ~$3 billion/year — ties the three regional stories to one documented climate pattern (rainfall concentrating into fewer, heavier bursts rather than total rainfall rising).
3. **Human-exposure scale for Uttarakhand** — most spot stories don't mention that Char Dham Yatra 2026 registrations (~17.8 lakh) and footfall (48.3 lakh, a record) are far higher than prior years, meaning the helicopter suspension affects a much larger pilgrim base than similar past disruptions.
4. **Practical safety guidance** — NDMA's official flood Do's & Don'ts, rarely reproduced in news articles, is included as actionable reader value.
5. **Direct link to the season's other recent Uttarakhand tragedy** (the Gaurikund helicopter crash, 5th incident in 6 weeks) for context on why authorities are being cautious now.
6. **Central government financial/relief response** (PIB-confirmed ₹1,066.80 crore SDRF/NDRF release, PMO statement) — sourced first-hand, not paraphrased from wire copy.
