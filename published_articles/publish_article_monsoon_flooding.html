India's monsoon season has brought a serious crisis to multiple states, with Gujarat and Assam facing the most severe flooding. The India Meteorological Department attributes the flooding to two active low-pressure systems combined with a western disturbance, creating conditions for intense rainfall across the country.

Gujarat has emerged as one of the hardest-hit regions. The state evacuated over 40,000 people to safer locations as heavy monsoon rainfall continued throughout late July 2026. The state government activated emergency response protocols and coordinated with the National Disaster Response Force. In Valsad district, the situation became particularly desperate—the area received more than 100 centimeters of rainfall in just 24 hours, a quantity that normally falls over several months during monsoon season. Native Planet's Gujarat emergency update documented the scale of flooding across the district.

Rescue operations in Gujarat have been ongoing. Local administration deployed boats and coordinated with emergency services to reach people trapped by floodwaters. Fire department personnel rescued 20 people from a residential society in Navsari city. In a separate operation, 21 people stranded on the Karjan river bank near Rajpipla were rescued in joint operations with state authorities. The speed at which water levels rose made these rescue operations critical for saving lives. Over 9,500 people were evacuated from Navsari district alone.

Assam's situation is equally dire, with a rising death toll and widespread displacement. As of late July 2026, 61 deaths have been confirmed across the state due to flood-related incidents. More than seven lakh people—roughly 700,000 individuals—have been affected by the flooding. About 872 villages have been submerged under floodwaters in Assam alone. Down To Earth reported that landslides are worsening the Himalayan crisis alongside the flood disaster.

The monsoon system remains active across North India. The Indian Meteorological Department has issued red alerts for 17 states, indicating conditions are expected to worsen or remain dangerous. Red alert designation means extreme rainfall is forecasted, and residents should exercise maximum caution. Power supplies have been disrupted in affected areas. Schools and offices remain closed in many regions. Supply chains for essential goods have been affected, particularly in rural areas.

Authorities have issued specific warnings for states including Rajasthan, Maharashtra, and Madhya Pradesh, where extremely heavy rainfall exceeding 204.5 millimeters in isolated areas is expected. North Indian states like Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, and Himachal Pradesh are similarly affected. The combination of heavy rainfall and hilly terrain creates significant risk of landslides and flash floods in vulnerable areas.

Hospitals in flood-hit regions are receiving overflow patients with water-related illnesses and injuries from flooding incidents. Public health officials warn that waterborne diseases like cholera and typhoid often emerge in flood aftermath. Meteorologists warn that this pattern may persist for several more days, with additional extreme rainfall possible in some areas before the situation stabilizes.
