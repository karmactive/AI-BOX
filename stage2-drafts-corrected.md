# Trump $500 ACA Refund Checks: Who Qualifies and What to Do When It Arrives

More than 950,000 Americans enrolled in Affordable Care Act marketplace plans are receiving $500 payments from the U.S. Treasury. Payments began going out on September 30 and are being delivered as paper checks or direct deposits, along with a letter signed by President Trump. Here is what you need to know before you cash or deposit yours.

**What the checks are and who gets one**

The Trump administration announced the payments on September 10, describing them as refunds to ACA enrollees who the administration says were overcharged. The administration says the money comes from surplus funds associated with the federally operated ACA exchanges.

Roughly 950,000 people qualify. To receive a payment automatically, you must have bought health insurance through HealthCare.gov, paid your full monthly premium without receiving a federal premium tax credit, and live in one of the 30 states that use the federal marketplace. The administration says primary recipients are people earning above 400% of the federal poverty level — roughly $63,000 a year for an individual. No application is required. The Treasury is issuing payments based on existing enrollment records.

Payments are being sent as paper checks or direct deposits. Some families with more than one qualifying household member will receive multiple $500 payments.

**What to do if you receive one**

If you receive a $500 payment that appears to be part of the ACA refund program, verify the sender and accompanying information before depositing it. The payment is being distributed based on government enrollment records, so verify it matches your account details. The IRS has not published guidance on how these payments should be treated for federal tax purposes. If you have questions about your specific tax situation, consult a tax professional or wait for IRS guidance.

**The tax question most news coverage skipped**

What most reporting hasn't addressed: the taxability question. Whether the $500 counts as taxable income, or interacts with ACA premium tax credit reconciliation, depends on individual circumstances and has not yet been addressed by IRS guidance. Save any documentation that arrives with your payment. Flag the amount for your tax preparer rather than assuming it is non-taxable.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Who is eligible for the Trump $500 ACA refund check?**
Eligibility is automatic for approximately 950,000 people who bought ACA coverage through HealthCare.gov, paid full premiums without federal premium tax credits, and live in one of the 30 federally facilitated marketplace states. The administration says primary recipients are people above 400% of the federal poverty level. No application is required.

**Are the $500 ACA checks taxable income?**
The IRS has not yet published guidance specific to this program. Keep any documentation that arrives with your payment and ask a tax professional about your individual situation before assuming a tax treatment.

**Do I need to apply to receive the $500 healthcare check?**
No. If you qualify, the payment is issued automatically based on government enrollment records. Payments began going out on September 30.

Payments are being delivered through October to addresses and accounts on file with the federal exchange.

# 2027 Social Security COLA: Expected Raise, Announcement Date, and October Payment Schedule

The Social Security Administration is set to announce the 2027 cost-of-living adjustment on October 14, and more than 70 million beneficiaries are watching. Current forecasts point to an increase of around 3.5% to 3.6% — but your October check won't reflect it, and the actual dollar gain may be smaller than it looks.

**When the announcement comes and what triggers it**

The SSA calculates the annual COLA using a federal inflation measure called the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers (CPI-W). Specifically, it compares the average CPI-W reading from July, August, and September of this year against the same three-month average from 2025. Once the Bureau of Labor Statistics releases the September CPI-W data, SSA publishes the official percentage — expected on October 14, 2026.

That official figure replaces all current estimates, which are based on July and August data only. Forecasts from the Senior Citizens League, AARP and other retirement-focused outlets have clustered around 3.5%–3.6%, but treat those as projections until SSA confirms.

**Your October check is not the new amount**

This is the most common misunderstanding. The higher benefit does not start in October. Your October 2026 payment reflects your current benefit amount. Beneficiaries who started receiving benefits before May 1997 are paid on October 2. For everyone else, payments go out on the second, third, or fourth Wednesday of the month based on your birthday: birth dates 1–10 receive payment on October 14, 11–20 on October 21, and 21–31 on October 28. SSI recipients received their October payment on October 1, with a second SSI payment on October 30 representing the November payment arriving early because November 1 falls on a Sunday. The 2027 COLA applies to December 2026 Social Security benefits, which most beneficiaries receive in January 2027.

**The Medicare deduction that reduces your real gain**

When planning your 2027 budget, subtract estimated Medicare Part B premium increases from your gross COLA calculation to find your actual take-home change. Part B premiums are deducted directly from Social Security checks. The 2026 Medicare Trustees Report projects the 2027 standard Part B premium at $209.50 per month, up from $202.90 in 2026 — a projected increase of $6.60. The final 2027 premium will be confirmed by CMS in November 2026 and may differ from the Trustees projection. One protection worth knowing: under the "hold harmless" provision of the Social Security Act, Medicare premium increases generally cannot push the net benefit for most beneficiaries below what they received in December of the prior year, though exceptions apply.

**Why the number feels smaller than inflation**

The CPI-W formula uses spending weights for urban wage earners and clerical workers rather than spending patterns specific to older Americans. The experimental CPI-E index gives greater weight to medical care and other categories more common among older households, and has often produced a higher calculated inflation reading than CPI-W. Advocacy groups have pushed for years to use CPI-E as the COLA basis, but no legislative change has passed.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**When will the 2027 Social Security COLA be announced?**
The SSA is expected to announce the 2027 COLA on October 14, 2026, immediately after the Bureau of Labor Statistics releases the September CPI-W report. The 2027 COLA applies to December 2026 Social Security benefits, which most beneficiaries receive in January 2027.

**What will the 2027 Social Security COLA percentage be?**
Current forecasts from groups including the Senior Citizens League and AARP estimate 3.5%–3.6%, based on July and August CPI-W data. The official figure depends on September data and will be confirmed by SSA on October 14.

**What are the Social Security payment dates for October 2026?**
Beneficiaries who started receiving benefits before May 1997 are paid on October 2. For other beneficiaries, payments go out on October 14 (birth dates 1–10), October 21 (11–20), or October 28 (21–31). SSI recipients are paid on October 1 and October 30. October amounts reflect your current benefit, not the 2027 increase.

The official announcement is expected on October 14. Check back for updates when SSA confirms the final percentage.

# Claiming Age Clarity Act: Bipartisan Social Security Bill Heads to President's Desk

Congress has passed bipartisan legislation that would change Social Security terminology used to describe claiming ages — and if you're approaching 62, understanding how the new language works could affect one of the biggest financial decisions of your retirement.

**What the bill does**

The Claiming Age Clarity Act, H.R. 5284, passed the Senate on September 29 and is headed to the President's desk. The bill was sponsored by Representative Lloyd Smucker (R-PA).

The legislation directs the SSA to update its rules, regulations, guidance and other materials to replace confusing age-related terms with language intended to make the financial consequences of claiming at different ages clearer. The SSA would need to make these changes no later than 12 months after enactment or January 1, 2027, whichever is later.

Specifically, the bill replaces three sets of terms:

- "Early Eligibility Age" becomes "Minimum Benefit Age"
- "Full Retirement Age" and "Normal Retirement Age" become "Standard Benefit Age"
- The age up to which delayed retirement credits apply becomes "Maximum Benefit Age"

**The penalty most early claimers don't fully understand**

Filing at 62 reduces your monthly benefit by up to 30% permanently compared to your benefit at Full Retirement Age — for people born in 1960 or later, that is age 67. That reduction never goes away. Delaying from Full Retirement Age to age 70 increases the monthly benefit through delayed retirement credits of 8% per year.

The current terminology — "early eligibility age" and "full retirement age" — can lead workers to misunderstand the permanent financial difference between claiming ages. The bill's sponsors argue that terms like "Minimum Benefit Age" and "Standard Benefit Age" more directly signal that claiming earlier means a permanently smaller monthly payment.

**Why Congress focused on terminology rather than changing the benefit structure**

The bill does not alter eligibility ages, payroll tax rates, or benefit formulas. It addresses terminology that lawmakers and researchers say has long contributed to misunderstanding among workers who claim at 62 without realising the reduction is permanent.

You can check your current Social Security Statement on SSA.gov now. Under the new terminology, the ages used in communications would label the trade-offs more directly than the current system does.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**What is the Claiming Age Clarity Act?**
H.R. 5284 is bipartisan legislation requiring the SSA to replace existing age-related Social Security terms with clearer language. "Early Eligibility Age" would become "Minimum Benefit Age," "Full Retirement Age" would become "Standard Benefit Age," and the age ceiling for delayed retirement credits would become "Maximum Benefit Age."

**How much do you lose if you claim Social Security at 62?**
For people whose Full Retirement Age is 67, claiming at 62 can reduce the monthly benefit by up to 30% compared to the Standard Benefit Age amount. That reduction applies for life.

**What is Full Retirement Age for Social Security?**
For anyone born in 1960 or later, Full Retirement Age — which the bill would rename Standard Benefit Age — is 67. Between ages 67 and 70, benefits increase by 8% per year you delay.

The bill moves to the President's desk following Senate passage. Once signed, SSA has until the later of 12 months post-enactment or January 1, 2027, to update its materials and communications.

# Andy Burnham Proposes Pension Triple Lock Reform to Fund Social Care Amid 48% Public Backing

Prime Minister Andy Burnham has set out plans to adjust how the state pension increases from April 2030 — replacing the current triple lock mechanism with a modified system — and directing the fiscal savings toward a new National Care Service. A fresh YouGov poll shows nearly half of Britons back the plan when they're told what the money would fund.

**What Burnham is proposing**

The current triple lock guarantees the state pension rises each year by whichever is highest: wage growth, inflation, or 2.5%. From April 2030, Burnham's government proposes adjusting this to a system under which the state pension would increase by the higher of CPI inflation or 2.5%, with a separate mechanism to ensure the pension keeps pace with average earnings growth over time. This removes the possibility of the pension spiking directly from volatile short-term wage growth while retaining the 2.5% minimum floor and an earnings backstop.

The current triple lock arrangement will remain unchanged through the rest of this Parliament before the adjusted mechanism takes effect in 2030.

The government says savings from adjusting the triple lock are intended to fund the proposed National Care Service, which it says would provide free personal care based on need.

**The YouGov polling**

YouGov surveyed 4,503 GB adults on September 29 and found 48% support changing the triple lock to fund a National Care Service, with 28% opposed and 24% undecided. The margin holds up at net positive (+20) when the reform is explicitly framed as ring-fenced for social care — a framing that appears to transform the question from a pension reduction into a benefit trade-off.

**Where the government stands**

The government's official policy commits to maintaining the current triple lock unchanged until April 2030. From that point, the adjusted mechanism described above would take effect. The government estimates savings of around £15 billion a year by the end of the 2030s, though independent economists have noted that the actual savings are highly scenario-dependent and will vary with future inflation and wage growth conditions.

**What it means for your pension**

Your state pension payment will not change immediately. The government's official policy maintains the current triple lock through this Parliament. If the adjusted mechanism takes effect from April 2030, your annual increase would still track the higher of CPI inflation or 2.5%, with the earnings backstop ensuring the pension does not fall behind wages over the long term. The government says the proposed National Care Service would provide free personal care based on need.

# UK-France 'One-in, One-out' Migrant Deal Scrapped: Why the Pilot Ended

The UK-France pilot agreement designed to manage small-boat Channel crossings through a bilateral exchange system has been scrapped, with the Home Office confirming on September 30 that it is no longer processing new cases. **What the scheme was and what it tried to do**

Under the agreement, people who arrived in the UK by small boat could be returned to France in exchange for an equivalent number of asylum seekers transferred to Britain through a legal route. Home Office data records approximately 1,087 returns to France and 1,117 transfers into Britain between August 6, 2025 and June 30, 2026. About 1,500 returns took place across the full period of the scheme.

**Why the deal fell apart**

The UK government's stated reason for not extending the scheme was that it had not been able to scale up the numbers of people involved to the level it had hoped. France, for its part, had indicated it did not want to continue as the sole European partner in a bilateral arrangement and wanted a broader EU-wide approach to Channel migration rather than a France-only agreement with the UK.

**What happens now**

The Home Office confirmed the pilot is no longer processing new cases. Some previously arranged returns can still proceed.

The ending of the pilot leaves the UK without this specific bilateral returns framework. The Home Office has not announced a replacement arrangement.

# Judge Dismisses Police Officers' Lawsuit Over Capitol Jan. 6 Memorial Plaque

A federal judge has dismissed a lawsuit brought by two police officers who sued to compel the placement of a commemorative January 6 plaque at the required permanent location on the Capitol's western front. The court did not rule on whether the law had been complied with — it ruled that the officers had no legal standing to bring the case.

**What the lawsuit sought**

Congress included a plaque requirement in Section 214 of the Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2022 (Pub. L. 117-103). The provision directed the Architect of the Capitol to obtain an honorific plaque listing the names of all officers who responded to the Capitol on January 6, 2021, and place it at a permanent location on the western front within one year. A plaque was installed, but it listed law enforcement agencies and organizations alongside a QR code linking to officers' names, rather than listing individual names on the physical face of the plaque, and its placement was not the permanent western-front location specified in the law.

Former US Capitol Police officer Harry Dunn and Metropolitan Police Department officer Daniel Hodges filed suit seeking a court order compelling compliance with the statutory requirements.

**Why the court dismissed it**

Judge Dabney Friedrich ruled the plaintiffs lacked Article III standing — the constitutional requirement that a person must have a direct, personal, legally recognisable injury to bring a federal lawsuit. The court found that Dunn and Hodges had not demonstrated the concrete, personal injury required for Article III standing.

The dismissal was jurisdictional. The court did not reach the question of whether the Architect of the Capitol had complied with the 2022 statutory requirement.

**What the ruling means**

The ruling ended the plaintiffs' case in the district court unless they pursue an appeal. The Architect of the Capitol holds responsibility for the plaque under the 2022 statute. Any further action to bring the plaque into compliance with the law's location and naming requirements rests with the Architect and congressional oversight, not the courts, following this dismissal.

The plaintiffs' attorneys have indicated they are reviewing whether an appeal is viable.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

The federal court ruled that officers Harry Dunn and Daniel Hodges had not demonstrated the concrete, personal injury required for Article III constitutional standing. # Yacht Sinks After Hitting Sperm Whale in South Pacific: 4 Sailors Rescued Alive

Four sailors were rescued in the South Pacific after their 51-foot sailing yacht struck a sperm whale, began taking on water, and sank, leaving them in a liferaft for approximately 17 hours until a cargo ship reached them. The rescue unfolded roughly 250 nautical miles north of Norfolk Island.

**What happened**

The Australian-flagged yacht *Tai Tam* struck a sperm whale in the early hours of Saturday, September 27. The collision caused damage and the vessel began taking on water. The four-person crew deployed their liferaft and activated their emergency position-indicating radio beacon (EPIRB), transmitting a distress signal to Maritime New Zealand's Rescue Coordination Centre.

A Royal New Zealand Air Force P-8A Poseidon maritime patrol aircraft was tasked to the scene, located the survivors and established two-way radio communications with them while vessels in the area were directed toward their position. The sailors remained in the liferaft until around 6 a.m. on Sunday, when they were picked up by the 143-metre cargo ship *Sofrana Surville*, the first merchant vessel to reach the scene. All four were reported safe.

**How the crew's preparation made the difference**

The crew activated their EPIRB promptly, which allowed the Rescue Coordination Centre to begin coordinating the response. For offshore sailors and maritime travelers: this survival outcome came down to having the right gear stowed and accessible before anything went wrong. A pre-packed, buoyant grab-bag with an active EPIRB and a means of two-way communication gives rescue services the data they need to find you in open ocean.

Collisions between whales and sailing vessels occur along major ocean routes, though documented sinkings remain rare. All four crew members of *Tai Tam* were rescued safely after deploying a liferaft and activating a distress beacon approximately 250 nautical miles north of Norfolk Island.
