What started as a scrappy MTV series in October 2000 — the kind of show that drew congressional criticism before its second episode even aired — spent 25 years becoming one of the most durable comedy franchises in Hollywood history. Jackass: Best and Last, which opened in US theaters on June 26, 2026, is the fifth and reportedly final theatrical entry in that franchise. Directed by Jeff Tremaine and produced alongside Spike Jonze and Johnny Knoxville, the film mixes all-new stunts with never-before-seen footage and the franchise's greatest hits — a 25-year victory lap and a farewell at the same time. Knoxville, who has confirmed this is the last film, said on the final day of production: "I cried a few times that day." For those following entertainment franchise finales, few have tracked a cast this closely from the margins of cable TV to more than a billion dollars in global theatrical revenue.

Jackass: Best and Last opened in US theaters on June 26, 2026 with an $8.4 million domestic opening weekend — the franchise's lowest theatrical debut — against a production budget of $10 million. Its worldwide opening weekend reached $10.3 million. By comparison, Jackass Forever opened at $23 million domestically in 2022, and the franchise's all-time peak remains Jackass 3D at $171.7 million worldwide. The film's streaming debut on Paramount+ is planned after the standard 90-day theatrical window. Brad Pitt's appearance — via extended, previously unreleased footage from the original 2001 "Abduction" stunt — was confirmed by the cast after release. Steve-O described seeing the remastered clip as "wild." The film carries an R rating for dangerous stunts, crude material throughout, graphic nudity, pervasive language, and sexual material.
