# STORY 1: ABHIJEET DIPKE FIR AND THE SCHOOL AUDIT CONTROVERSY

An activist's campaign to inspect [government schools](https://www.karmactive.com/category/india) in Maharashtra has been followed by an FIR against him and others. Abhijeet Dipke, founder of the [Cockroach Janta Party](https://www.karmactive.com/tag/cockroach-janta-party), was booked after a complaint alleged that he and his team entered a government school without authorization.

The incident occurred at a [Zilla Parishad Urdu school](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2299439) in Jawali, near Ausa in Latur district. According to the complaint filed by teacher Sayyed Shamshadbano Khairatali, the visit disrupted classes and involved recording video footage inside classrooms. Police registered cases under [BNS sections 132, 329(3), 351(2), and 356(2)](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/20062/1/a2023-45.pdf), which concern assault or criminal force against a public servant, house-trespass, criminal intimidation, and criminal defamation respectively.

What makes this incident significant is its broader context. Dipke had been running the "School Thik Karo" campaign—translating to "Fix the School"—which involved visiting [government schools](https://www.karmactive.com/tag/school-audit) to document facility problems. Earlier visits recorded issues including broken windows, dirty washrooms, insufficient drinking water, and damaged benches.

The campaign reflects concerns about [government school accountability](https://www.karmactive.com/category/policy). Dipke has publicly questioned why ministers and education officials don't enrol their children in government schools. He has also called for using [PM CARES funds](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2299046) to improve government school infrastructure, pointing to questions about resource allocation and educational priorities.

The allegations themselves remain unverified as the investigation continues. Claims about the nature of questioning, student involvement, and whether teachers faced threats originate from the formal complaint filed by school staff. No charges have been proven, and the case is under [police investigation](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/aurangabad/fir-against-abhijeet-dipke-for-entering-govt-school-without-permission-threatening-teachers/amp_articleshow/133426295.cms) at Ausa Police Station.

The Ausa Police Station is handling the investigation. The case will determine whether Dipke's team violated school access regulations, whether documentation activities crossed legal boundaries, and whether intimidation allegations have substance.

# STORY 2: GOOGLE'S ANDROID PULSE - THE SYSTEM COMPONENT THAT SPARKED MYSTERY

A [Google app](https://support.google.com/product-documentation/answer/14343500) called Android Pulse has been catching attention recently. It appeared in [Google Play Store](https://support.google.com/product-documentation/answer/14343500) updates with a blank icon and no home screen presence, accumulating over 10 million downloads reported on the Play listing. However, what matters most is understanding what Android Pulse actually is: a background [system component](https://www.apkmirror.com/apk/google-inc/android-pulse/pulse-1-0-873949644-release/), not a traditional app.

Android Pulse runs quietly in the background. It is designed as a system component that performs [anomaly detection](https://support.google.com/product-documentation/answer/14343500) functions related to critical system-resource consumption on Android devices.

[Google's documentation](https://support.google.com/product-documentation/answer/14343500) supports this. The company's system services release notes from April 2026 mentioned Android Pulse in GMS Core, describing improvements aimed at better device performance.

The timeline reveals important context. [Android Pulse versions](https://www.apkmirror.com/apk/google-inc/android-pulse/) existed months before August 2026, with records showing releases from March 2026. The August visibility spike didn't introduce something new—it made an existing component visible in your update queue for the first time. An embedded system tool became updatable through [Google Play](https://support.google.com/product-documentation/answer/14343500) separately from major Android releases. When users checked updates, they saw something unfamiliar and naturally wanted to know what it was.

This distinction matters significantly. The component was already present; only its visibility changed. This isn't about Google secretly installing software but rather Google making a [system-level tool](https://support.google.com/product-documentation/answer/14343500) independently updatable.

The underlying purpose of the component is facilitating distribution of detection rules used by the [Android operating system](https://www.karmactive.com/tag/android) for monitoring system behavior.

The confusion around Android Pulse reflects broader challenges in communicating about [system-level technology](https://www.karmactive.com/tag/ai-technology). Most users encounter familiar apps but rarely hear about the infrastructure running underneath. When something previously invisible becomes visible—like Android Pulse in the [update queue](https://support.google.com/product-documentation/answer/14343500)—people naturally wonder what just appeared. In this case, understanding that the component existed all along but only gained independent visibility changes the entire narrative from "Google secretly added something" to "Google made existing maintenance software independently updatable."

# STORY 3: THE 96% PARTIAL LUNAR ECLIPSE INDIA WON'T SEE

An unusual astronomical event is happening this month, but Indian readers won't be able to see it. On August 27–28, 2026, a significant [partial lunar eclipse](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/LEplot/LEplot2001/LE2026Aug28P.pdf) will occur. The eclipse will not be visible from India.

During maximum eclipse on August 28, 2026, at [04:12 UTC](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/LEplot/LEplot2001/LE2026Aug28P.pdf), the Moon will enter Earth's shadow at a deep [umbral magnitude](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/LEplot/LEplot2001/LE2026Aug28P.pdf) of 0.9299, with approximately 96.2 percent of the Moon's visible disk immersed in Earth's dark umbral shadow. The visibility zone covers North America, South America, Western Europe, and West Africa.

This is specifically a [partial eclipse](https://www.space.com/stargazing/lunar-eclipses/august-2026-lunar-eclipse-everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-96-percent-blood-moon), not a total one. The distinction directly affects what viewers see. With approximately 96.2 percent of the Moon's visible disk in Earth's dark shadow, the remaining thin edge will remain directly illuminated by sunlight.

The visual appearance occurs due to physics. [Light passing through Earth's atmosphere](https://www.space.com/stargazing/lunar-eclipses/august-2026-lunar-eclipse-everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-96-percent-blood-moon) gets scattered and filtered toward the Moon's surface, creating a reddish tint on the eclipsed portion. The majority of the visible Moon will appear coppery-red while a thin crescent remains bright white.

The eclipse will last approximately [five hours and 38 minutes](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/LEplot/LEplot2001/LE2026Aug28P.pdf) total. The sequence follows a specific progression: the penumbral phase begins (barely visible shadow), the partial phase becomes noticeable, maximum eclipse occurs, then reverses through identical phases in reverse order.

For observers in visibility zones like the Americas and Europe, this represents a significant astronomical opportunity. Unlike [solar eclipses](https://www.karmactive.com/tag/astronomy), lunar eclipses require no special eye protection. The human eye can safely observe the entire event. Binoculars or telescopes enhance detail but aren't necessary.

The eclipse is part of [Saros Series 138](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/LEplot/LEplot2001/LE2026Aug28P.pdf), a series of eclipses with cycles of approximately 18 years with additional shifts in date and timing characteristics. This particular event will be notable for its deep partial coverage.

Understanding why India won't see this eclipse involves basic geometry. During the eclipse's observable phases, the Moon will be positioned below the horizon for viewers in South Asia. [Moon visibility](https://www.karmactive.com/category/space) depends on geographic location and precise timing. This eclipse simply occurs when the Moon isn't in the sky above India.

This reflects a fundamental truth about [celestial events](https://www.karmactive.com/tag/astronomy-events): they're not visible from everywhere simultaneously. For skywatchers in the Americas, Europe, and Africa, August 27–28 presents an opportunity to observe a deep partial lunar eclipse.

# STORY 4: EPFO EMPLOYEES' ENROLMENT CAMPAIGN 2026 - YOUR WINDOW TO COVER MISSING WORKERS

The [Employees' Provident Fund Organization](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2300475) has opened a one-time window for employers to enrol workers left outside EPF coverage. The Employees' Enrolment Campaign 2026, or [EEC 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2300475), came into effect on July 1, 2026, and runs until October 31, 2026. This matters because the regularization comes with specific financial terms for employers.

The campaign covers workers who should have been part of the [EPF system](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2300475) between April 1, 2009, and March 31, 2026. Employers can now voluntarily enrol qualifying employees during this limited period.

Who qualifies? Employees must meet standard [EPF eligibility requirements](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2300475). They need to be alive and employed with their establishment on the date of declaration.

The financial framework built into the campaign addresses a major employer concern. Normally, employers deduct 12 percent from employee wages for the [employee's EPF contribution](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2300475) while contributing 12 percent as employer share. For unregistered workers, the campaign offers specific relief. If the employer never deducted the employee's share from wages during the previous years, the employer isn't required to pay those retrospective employee contributions. The employer must remit the employer's statutory share plus applicable [statutory interest](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2300475) under Section 7Q.

[Damages for past non-compliance](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2300475) are set at ₹100 as a lump sum. Employers participating in the campaign must also remit [administrative charges](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2300475) on past contributions.

The enrollment process is digital and structured. Employers identify eligible workers from their records, generate [Universal Account Numbers](https://www.karmactive.com/tag/epfo) using Face Authentication through the UMANG app, and remit contributions via the [Electronic Challan-cum-Return](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2300475) (ECR) system.

Workers need to understand their role. The campaign is [employer-initiated](https://www.karmactive.com/tag/employment), so establishments must take action. Employees should verify whether their employer qualifies and whether they fit the eligibility window. Reviewing [employment records](https://www.karmactive.com/tag/labor-laws) and wage documentation is prudent. Discussions with HR departments clarify coverage status. Once employers complete enrollment, workers should verify that their UAN was properly generated and EPF records were correctly established.

This campaign addresses a documented gap in [social security coverage](https://www.karmactive.com/tag/social-security). The EPF system provides provident fund savings, pension coverage, and insurance benefits. Missing this coverage means forgoing both retirement savings accumulated during working years and family protection benefits for dependents.

The October 31 deadline is fixed. This is a limited-time regularization. After this date, the campaign closes.

# STORY 5: DELHI'S H1N1 SURGE - UNDERSTANDING THE OUTBREAK AND WHEN TO SEEK HELP

Delhi has recorded a significant increase in [influenza A (H1N1)](https://dgehs.delhi.gov.in/dghs/public-health-wing) infections. The increase has prompted the [Union Health Ministry](https://www.mohfw.gov.in/) to review preparedness and hospitals to expand capacity. Understanding this situation requires examining the actual clinical facts.

[H1N1](https://www.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/49049173711477913766.pdf) is a subtype of influenza A virus commonly associated with what is often called swine flu. It now spreads human-to-human through respiratory droplets, similar to seasonal influenza. [Delhi's health authorities](https://dgehs.delhi.gov.in/dghs/public-health-wing) use a three-category system to organize clinical response and allocate resources.

[Category A](https://dgehs.delhi.gov.in/dghs/public-health-wing) cases present with mild symptoms: fever, cough, and sore throat without serious complications and no high-risk factors. Management is home isolation, rest, adequate fluids, and symptom management. Antiviral medications and laboratory testing are unnecessary.

[Category B](https://dgehs.delhi.gov.in/dghs/public-health-wing) cases are more significant. These involve high-grade fever with severe sore throat. Patients also qualify for this category if they have high-risk factors. High-risk populations include children with predisposing risk factors, adults over 65, pregnant women, and people with [chronic conditions](https://www.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/49049173711477913766.pdf) affecting lungs, kidneys, heart, metabolism, or immune function. These patients should receive treatment as guided by clinical protocols. Home isolation continues, but medical involvement becomes essential.

[Category C](https://dgehs.delhi.gov.in/dghs/public-health-wing) cases constitute medical emergencies. Red-flag symptoms requiring immediate medical evaluation include breathlessness, chest pain, drops in blood oxygen levels, coughing up blood, confusion, and low blood pressure. These patients need hospital admission and intensive medical support.

Seasonal context provides important perspective. India experiences [influenza activity](https://www.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/49049173711477913766.pdf) during different seasons depending on region. The [Ministry of Health](https://www.mohfw.gov.in/) has noted that in north, northwest and central India, influenza surge usually occurs in January through March, while other regions experience peaks during monsoon seasons. Delhi's current increase aligns with seasonal patterns.

[Transmission mechanics](https://dgehs.delhi.gov.in/dghs/public-health-wing) are straightforward. Respiratory droplets from sneezing, coughing, and close contact spread the virus. Prevention involves hand hygiene, mask use in crowded settings, avoiding face touching, and [staying home during illness](https://www.karmactive.com/tag/health-alert). The seasonal influenza vaccine can provide protection when an [H1N1 component](https://www.karmactive.com/tag/vaccination) is included.

[Antibiotics](https://www.karmactive.com/tag/medicine) are ineffective against viral infections like H1N1. Self-medication with antibiotics serves no therapeutic purpose. Medical evaluation is appropriate when symptoms worsen significantly.

[Delhi's Health and Family Welfare Department](https://dgehs.delhi.gov.in/) released updated public guidance on August 20, 2026. The [Directorate General of Health Services](https://dgehs.delhi.gov.in/dghs/public-health-wing) published detailed clinical protocols for medical professionals.
