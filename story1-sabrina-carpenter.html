Los Angeles County Superior Court records, an LAPD detective's declaration, and Ring doorbell footage all formed the basis of a temporary restraining order granted on May 29. Two hearings are scheduled in June. This is every documented incident, in order — drawn from court filings and official law enforcement statements.

Know the Law
Key legal context behind this case — and California's stalking protections
What Is California Penal Code § 602(m)? ▼
Under California Penal Code § 602(m), it is a misdemeanor to enter and occupy real property or structures of another person without the consent of the owner, their agent, or the person in lawful possession. Applegate was charged under this statute following his May 23 arrest. A misdemeanor conviction in California can carry jail time up to one year in county jail, fines, and probation.
What Does the Restraining Order Actually Cover? ▼
The temporary restraining order issued on May 29 prohibits Applegate from coming within 100 yards of Carpenter, her home, her vehicle, her workplace, and her household residents. It also bans all contact — in person, by phone, by message, and online. The June 17 hearing will determine if this becomes a permanent civil harassment restraining order. Under California law, a civil harassment restraining order can be extended for up to five years and can be renewed.
California's Anti-Stalking Law: The History Behind It ▼
California enacted the first anti-stalking law in the United States in 1990, the year after actress Rebecca Schaeffer was shot and killed at her Los Angeles home by a stalker who had spent three years pursuing her. The law has been amended multiple times since, each time expanding definitions and penalties. Cyberstalking was specifically added as a criminal category in later revisions, recognizing that dangerous obsessive behavior does not require physical proximity to escalate.
What Is a "Fixated, Obsessional Individual" in Law Enforcement Terms? ▼
LAPD Detective Peter Doomanis applied this classification to Applegate in his court declaration. In law enforcement methodology, a fixated individual is one who develops a single-minded, persistent focus on a specific target — often a public figure — disconnected from any real relationship with that person. Research published via PubMed Central (PMC) on celebrity stalking patterns identifies this profile as one associated with escalating physical risk, particularly when the individual makes repeated approaches despite legal intervention.
