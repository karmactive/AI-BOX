## ARTICLE 10 — Vietnam's 135,000-Seat Trong Dong Stadium Will Be the World's Largest — and the Ecological Bill Is Enormous

Vietnam is building the world's biggest football and multi-sport stadium in Hanoi. The arena's scale would surpass every existing venue on earth. The land it is being built on is a different story.

The Trong Dong Stadium, backed by conglomerate VinGroup under its VinFast brand, is designed to seat 135,000 people inside a 408-metre-diameter retractable dome shaped after the traditional Dong Son bronze drum. It sits at the centre of a $38-billion (approximately £28.8bn) Olympic Sports City complex on 73.3 hectares of land in southern Hanoi. Construction was officially announced with a July 2027 completion target — accelerated by 13 months from the original 2028 schedule — meaning 24-hour continuous construction is now underway. The project will surpass North Korea's Rungrado 1st of May Stadium at 114,000 seats and Morocco's Grand Stade Hassan II at 115,000.

Vietnam's ambitious plan to open the world's largest stadium by July 2027 marks a defining global sports infrastructure statement. But the project poses significant ecological challenges for Hanoi. The 135,000-seat Trong Dong complex occupies 22,200 acres of low-lying Red River delta basin in the Thuong Tin district. Urban planners warn that building over vital agricultural retention wetlands increases regional monsoon flood risks downstream. The round-the-clock accelerated construction schedule also carries heavy embodied carbon costs — in diesel generator emissions and concrete volumes — that cannot be offset by the solar canopy the development is marketing as green infrastructure.

### The Environmental Trade-Off the Launch Materials Don't Lead With

The 22,200-acre development perimeter encroaches on agricultural buffer zones and peri-urban retention lakes in the Red River floodplain that absorb seasonal monsoon and typhoon runoff. The permanent removal of these natural drainage buffers forces municipal planners to design artificial canal systems to prevent downstream urban flooding — a substantial public infrastructure cost that will be met by Hanoi's city government rather than the developer.
