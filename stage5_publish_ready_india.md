### Article Body
<p><em>Estimated reading time: 6 minutes</em></p>

<p>An Air India Airbus A320neo dropped 300 feet mid-cruise between Phuket and Delhi on August 4, injuring 17 people. The aircraft, registration VT-EXO, suffered simultaneous failures in all three hydraulic systems—an exceptionally rare event that aviation regulators are now examining.</p>

<p>Flight AI2379 carried 137 passengers and 8 crew members. Among those injured were 4 cabin crew with spinal and neck injuries. The aircraft's automated systems triggered nine technical warnings within one minute of the descent, according to post-flight data.</p>

<h3>What Happened</h3>

<p>The A320neo's autopilot disconnected without crew input. Both elevator systems faulted almost simultaneously. All three independent hydraulic systems—designed for redundancy—malfunctioned in the same incident. Stall warnings activated. The pilot applied manual nose-down input to recover the aircraft and regain altitude.</p>

<p>The Directorate General of Civil Aviation classified this as a "Serious Incident" under ICAO Annex 13. The Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau is leading the investigation, with assistance from Airbus and France's Bureau of Enquiry and Analysis.</p>

<h3>Questions Around Pilot Status</h3>

<p>The pilot-in-command underwent psychoactive screening. Initial results are described as "requiring further analysis." Both pilots were removed from roster pending further investigation.</p>

<p>Captain Sam Thomas of ALPA India (the Airline Pilots Association's India branch) confirmed that all three hydraulic systems had faulted, calling it an extraordinary failure sequence.</p>

<h3>Corporate Transparency Concerns</h3>

<p>Air India initially attributed the incident to turbulence. Subsequent statements from the airline dropped this explanation. The timeline and reasoning behind the shift remain unclear.</p>

<p>The investigation is ongoing. Results may take weeks or months. Regulators will likely examine whether the aircraft's maintenance protocols, design, or other factors contributed to the simultaneous hydraulic failures.</p>

<p>India's aviation safety record and the DGCA's oversight capacity are now in focus. Air India operates over 200 aircraft. The outcome of this investigation could shape how hydraulic systems are monitored across the fleet.</p>

<h3>Delhi H1N1 spike: 1,344 cases this season versus 229 last year</h3>

<p>Delhi has recorded 1,344 confirmed H1N1 cases this season, compared with 229 for the entire previous year—a more than six-fold increase. Surveys indicate 69% of Delhi households have reported flu-like symptoms. The Health Minister, Pankaj Singh, has confirmed all 1,344 patients were treated and discharged with no deaths. AIIMS has confirmed that H3N2 is also circulating.</p>

<h3>The monsoon temperature anomaly and viral amplification</h3>

<p>Monsoon season in Delhi typically brings lower temperatures, higher humidity, and crowded indoor spaces—perfect for respiratory virus transmission.</p>

<p>Virus survival in air increases with humidity. Spore germination rates for competing viral pathogens shift. Did this specific monsoon variant (unseasonably wet or warm) select for H1N1/H3N2 amplification, or is this ordinary seasonal circulation met with lower vaccination coverage?</p>

<h3>Vaccination coverage and the differential severity puzzle</h3>

<p>Delhi's vaccination infrastructure is extensive: AIIMS Delhi, Safdarjung, government dispensaries in all 11 districts, private clinics across South Delhi. Yet not all populations maintain equal flu vaccination uptake.</p>

<p>Severity differentials emerge when:</p>
<ul>
<li>Unvaccinated elderly (&gt;60) face exponentially higher ICU risk.</li>
<li>Children (&lt;5) unvaccinated for influenza can develop severe bronchiolitis.</li>
<li>Healthcare workers, if unvaccinated, become vectors in hospital wards.</li></ul>

### Article Body

<h3>Court Declares Women Free After Five Years of Unlawful Confinement</h3>

<p>The Allahabad High Court declared two women "free to reside at any place and with any person of their own choice" on August 6, ordering their immediate release from unlawful confinement. The women, aged 35 and 20, had been held by their father since 2021 after converting from Hinduism to Islam.</p>

<p>Justice Sandeep Jain heard the habeas corpus petition and found the detention unlawful. The court ruled the confinement constituted "an exceptionally grave violation of constitutional rights."</p>

<h3>Compensation Ordered</h3>

<p>The court ordered ₹25 lakh in compensation. Half must be paid by the father, Anil Kumar Bhatia. The remaining ₹12.5 lakh is to be split between a delinquent public servant and the Uttar Pradesh state government.</p>

<p>The order indicts both the father's actions and the state's failure. UP authorities had, despite being notified, permitted the confinement to continue for five years.</p>

<h3>Constitutional Violation</h3>

<p>The women's detention violated Article 19 (freedom of movement) and Article 21 (right to life and liberty) of the Indian Constitution. The court did not contest their right to convert. Rather, it centered on bodily autonomy—the principle that no individual can be imprisoned by family for exercising constitutional freedoms.</p>

<p>India's Parliamentary Standing Committee on Communications and IT issued a 3-day ultimatum to Meta founder Mark Zuckerberg on August 2, demanding an "unqualified apology" after the platform removed Prime Minister Narendra Modi's Facebook post.</p>

<p>The post was offline for 5 to 6 hours. Meta blamed automated moderation. The committee, chaired by BJP MP Nishikant Dubey, rejected this explanation and escalated the matter.</p>

<h3>The Ultimatum</h3>

<p>The committee threatened to withdraw Meta's Section 79 safe harbor exemption under India's Information Technology Act if Zuckerberg did not comply. Safe harbor protection shields platforms from liability for user-generated content. Losing it would expose Meta to legal action over every post on its network—a regulatory catastrophe.</p>

<p>The committee signaled that compliance is not optional. The tone and timeline made clear that the government views Meta as accountable for how its systems treat India's highest office.</p>

<h3>Broader Concerns</h3>

<p>The committee's demands extended beyond the Modi post removal. Three additional issues frame the standoff.</p>

<p>First: child sexual abuse material (CSAM). The committee flagged Meta's handling of content depicting child exploitation. Second: deepfakes. Third: content demeaning women. All three areas remain under scrutiny.</p>
