## STORY 2 — US MEAT RECALL (Priority 1, Consumer Safety)

**Headline:** 167,639 Pounds of Beef, Pork and Goat Recalled Over False USDA Inspection Mark

**Hook**
The US Department of Agriculture has issued a recall covering approximately 167,639 pounds of raw beef, pork and goat products from Star Meat Delivery. The products were distributed nationwide by A&D Foods to retailers and restaurants. FSIS said the products were produced without federal inspection while carrying a USDA inspection mark they were not entitled to display. The affected products may still be sitting in household refrigerators and restaurant storage.

**Core Facts**
USDA's Food Safety and Inspection Service announced the recall of raw beef, pork and goat products from Star Meat Delivery. FSIS classified the recall as Class I — its most serious designation. Distributor A&D Foods sent the products to retailers and restaurants across the country. The packaging carries a false establishment number — EST. 1363 — indicating inspection that never took place. FSIS states the products were produced without the benefit of federal inspection, meaning federal inspection did not occur before the products were distributed. No confirmed illnesses had been reported at the time of the announcement.

**Consequence**
Check any raw beef, pork or goat products in your home or business for the establishment number EST. 1363. If that number appears on the packaging, do not eat the product. FSIS advises throwing it away or returning it to the place of purchase. The problem with this recall is different from a typical contamination recall — the meat bypassed the federal inspection process entirely. Consumers cannot determine at home whether uninspected product meets federal safety requirements.

**Why the Inspection Status Matters**
When FSIS inspects meat before sale, federal inspectors assess the facility, the animals and the finished product for contamination, disease and correct labeling. Products that do not receive federal inspection do not receive the federal inspection controls that would otherwise apply.

The false USDA mark on these products meant consumers and retailers had no visible indication that the standard review had not taken place. A&D Foods distributed the products nationally, reaching both retailers and food-service operators, which is why FSIS warns the meat may still be in commercial cold storage as well as household freezers.

**What is EST. 1363, and why does it matter?**
EST. 1363 is the establishment number on the recalled packaging. Establishment numbers on USDA-inspected products identify the federally inspected facility where the product was made. The products bear this establishment number despite FSIS saying they were produced without federal inspection — making it a false establishment mark. Any product carrying it from this recall should be treated as unsafe.

Full visa-free travel would require a formal EU process that has not concluded, and the EU decision-making process — involving the Commission, Council and European Parliament — has not been completed.

**Does Dutch support mean Surinamers can travel visa-free to Europe?**
No. The September 2026 development is the Netherlands indicating its support for Suriname's visa-liberalisation objective — it is diplomatic advocacy, not a grant of exemption. The EU visa-liberalisation process involves the European Commission, Council and Parliament, not the Netherlands alone. EU member states can advocate for a country's exemption, but no individual member state can unilaterally remove the Schengen visa requirement.
