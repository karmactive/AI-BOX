## Story 129 — Uranus and Neptune's Bizarre Magnetic Fields Explained — And Their Poles May Shift Within Decades

For four decades, astronomers have known that Uranus and Neptune carry some of the strangest magnetic fields in the solar system — wildly tilted, off-center, and unlike anything seen on Earth or other planets. New computer simulations published November 15, 2026, offer the clearest explanation yet for why, and add an unexpected twist: the poles of both ice giants may shift by tens of degrees within just a few decades.

**Voyager 2's 40-year mystery**

When NASA's Voyager 2 flew past Uranus in 1986 and Neptune in 1989, its instruments measured magnetic fields that defied what scientists expected from planets that size. Earth's magnetic dipole is tilted about 11 degrees from its rotation axis — close enough to the pole that navigational compasses work reliably. Uranus's field is tilted 59 degrees. Neptune's is tilted 47 degrees.

Both are also significantly off-center, meaning the magnetic axis doesn't pass through the planet's core. That combination — large tilt, offset center — was hard to explain with the models scientists used for Earth and the gas giants Jupiter and Saturn, whose magnetism arises from liquid metallic hydrogen or liquid iron deep inside.

Uranus and Neptune are "ice giants," built largely from water, ammonia, and methane compressed under extraordinary pressure. The behavior of those materials at depth was the missing piece.

**How ionized water conducts electricity**

The new simulations, published in Communications Physics (2026; 9 (1), DOI: 10.1038/s42005-026-02841-y) by lead author Chi Yan at the University of Texas Jackson School of Geosciences and colleagues from UCLA, Imperial College London, and Northumbria University, modeled what happens to water under the pressures and temperatures found inside ice giants.

Under those conditions, water molecules break apart into electrically charged ions. That ionization makes the material conductive — able to carry electrical current and therefore generate a magnetic field when the planet rotates. The critical detail the simulations captured is that electrical conductivity increases with depth rather than being relatively uniform, as it is in Earth's iron core or Jupiter's metallic hydrogen layer.

That depth-dependent conductivity produces a magnetic dynamo that is naturally asymmetric. Instead of organizing into a clean, nearly aligned dipole like Earth's, the field generated in this model tilts sharply and shifts off-center — matching what Voyager 2 actually measured at both planets.

**Why ice-giant poles could swing tens of degrees in decades**

Earth's magnetic pole drifts slowly. The dipole tilt of about 11 degrees has moved roughly 1 degree over the past 50 years under normal conditions — fast enough that navigation charts require updates but slow enough that it rarely affects daily life. Reversals, when they happen, take thousands of years.

The ice-giant dynamo works differently. Because electrical conductivity varies sharply with depth and temperature, the simulations show the magnetic axes of Uranus and Neptune can swing by tens of degrees within just a few decades. That is not a reversal in the traditional sense — it is a rapid reorientation of the existing field driven by changes in the deep convective flow of ionized material.

The practical implication is significant: the magnetic fields that exist at Uranus and Neptune today may look substantially different from what Voyager 2 measured in the 1980s. The poles could have moved. The tilt could have changed. No spacecraft has visited either planet since, so there is no direct measurement to confirm or deny it — yet.

**How the James Webb Space Telescope tests the models**

That is where the James Webb Space Telescope comes in. Tom Stallard at Northumbria University is leading an auroral observation campaign targeting both ice giants. Auroras are generated when charged particles from a planet's magnetosphere interact with its upper atmosphere — they trace the shape and location of the magnetic field's polar regions. By observing where JWST detects auroral emissions at Uranus and Neptune and comparing those positions to the Voyager 2 field maps, researchers can check whether the poles have moved.

If Stallard's team finds the auroras sitting tens of degrees away from where Voyager 2's data would predict, it would be direct observational support for the rapid-shift predictions in the new simulations. That comparison is not yet published, but Yan and colleagues explicitly designed their models to generate testable predictions for the JWST campaign.

**Why future ice-giant missions still matter**

The simulations resolve the basic question of why ice giants produce tilted, off-center fields, but they also highlight how much is still unknown. Seventeen different PFAS — no wait, that's the wrong article. Seventeen years separate the Voyager 2 encounters at the two planets, and both were fast flybys. A dedicated orbiter — similar to Cassini at Saturn or Juno at Jupiter — could monitor how the field evolves in real time, track the depth of the ionized layer, and determine whether the rapid-shift predictions hold up over human-observable timescales. NASA's Planetary Science Decadal Survey has identified an Uranus orbiter and probe as its top priority for large missions, though no launch has been confirmed.

For now, the simulations provide a physical mechanism where none existed before, and JWST's auroral data will offer the first post-Voyager test of whether that mechanism is correct.

## Story 128 — QIOMALA Recalls HWT12P Electric Pressure Washers Sold on Amazon Over Electrocution Hazard

Homeowners who bought a QIOMALA electric pressure washer on Amazon between April and June 2026 should unplug it immediately and stop using it. The Consumer Product Safety Commission issued Recall #27-084 on November 12, 2026, covering 80 QIOMALA 1.2 GPM pressure washers, model HWT12P. The hazard is a power cord that isn't long enough for typical outdoor use, which the CPSC says pushes users toward extension cords and creates a risk of electric shock or electrocution. No injuries have been reported.

**What the recall covers**

The recalled pressure washers are QIOMALA model HWT12P, a 1.2 GPM electric unit available in green and black or blue and black. Each unit measures approximately 14 inches deep by 11 inches wide by 15 inches high. The package includes a 500 ml soap tank, four quick-disconnect nozzles, and a 20-foot hose. The washers were sold on Amazon.com between April and June 2026 for approximately $130. The importer is Ningbo Miyao E-Commerce Co., doing business as QIOMALA, based in China. The units were manufactured in China.

Outdoor cleaning tasks — washing driveways, decks, vehicles, and siding — typically require moving around a wide area. A pressure washer with an inadequate power cord length forces users to reach that range with an extension cord. Using an extension cord with high-current electrical equipment near water is exactly the scenario that creates the most dangerous shock conditions. The CPSC identified this design as the core hazard.

This recall is not about a defective component that randomly fails — it is about a predictable pattern of use that the product's cord length actively encourages. That makes the recall relevant to anyone who has used one, even without experiencing an incident.

As with other recent Amazon recalls, do not discard the unit before you have photographed the HWT12P label and received confirmation of your refund request.

## Story 127 — Kīlauea Episode 57 Forecast: USGS Sets Nov. 18–22 Window After Rare Overnight Vent Flames

Residents on Hawaiʻi Island and visitors planning a trip to Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park over Thanksgiving week may witness Kīlauea's next lava fountaining episode. The USGS Hawaiian Volcano Observatory set a forecast window of November 18 through November 22, 2026, for Episode 57 of the ongoing summit eruption series, based on a November 15 update showing rapid reinflation and unusual overnight activity at the southwest vent.

**What the forecast is based on**

Kīlauea's summit remains under an ADVISORY alert level and YELLOW aviation color code as of November 15. The primary instrument driving the Episode 57 forecast is the Uēkahuna tiltmeter (station UWD), which measures ground inflation — the buildup of magma pressure beneath the summit caldera.

Episode 56, the most recent fountaining episode, erupted on November 5, 2026, from the north vent. It produced a lava fountain reaching 660 feet (200 meters), sent a plume to 18,000 feet (5,500 meters), and erupted approximately 8 million cubic yards (6 million cubic meters) of lava at a peak rate of 260 cubic yards per second. When Episode 56 ended, the UWD tiltmeter recorded 17 microradians of deflationary tilt — the pressure loss that marks the end of a fountaining episode.

Since Episode 56 ended, the summit has recovered approximately 13 of those 17 microradians through inflationary tilt. That recovery rate — combined with an SO2 emission rate averaging about 2,000 tonnes per day recorded on November 14 — suggests the volcano is rebuilding toward the conditions that triggered the previous episodes.

**Overnight flames at the southwest vent**

Before Episode 56, all recent fountaining originated from the north vent. What makes the current recharge period different is what USGS summit webcams captured overnight: spatter and flames coming from the southwest vent — the other active vent on the floor of Halemaʻumaʻu crater.

Overnight webcam activity showing glowing material at a vent that hasn't been the primary eruption site is unusual in this episode sequence. It doesn't necessarily mean Episode 57 will originate from the southwest vent rather than the north vent, but USGS noted it as part of the monitoring picture supporting the November 18–22 forecast window.

**Vog, SO2, and Peleʻs hair hazards**

An SO2 rate of 2,000 tonnes per day — the November 14 average — is enough to create downwind vog (volcanic smog) across leeward districts of Hawaiʻi Island. Trade winds during this period are carrying volcanic gas to the southwest. People with respiratory conditions, the elderly, and young children in affected areas should monitor air quality advisories from the Hawaiʻi Department of Health.

During and after active fountaining episodes, wind can remobilize fine glassy volcanic material from the eruption. Pele's hair — thin strands of volcanic glass carried by wind currents — poses a respiratory and eye irritation hazard downwind of the eruption site. Episode 56 deposited trace tephra on Highway 11, the main route connecting the volcano to communities to the south. Drivers and outdoor workers in that corridor should be prepared for similar conditions if Episode 57 produces a comparable or larger fountain.

**Park closures and viewing access**

Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park remains open during inter-episode pauses. The Kīlauea Overlook and Kīlauea Visitor Center provide viewing access when eruption activity is visible from public areas, though specific closures depend on where active lava and gas hazards are located. During active fountaining, the park typically implements closures of areas closest to the eruption vent.

Visitors planning travel during the November 18–22 window should check the park's official website and the USGS Hawaiian Volcano Observatory (hvo.wr.usgs.gov) for updated closures and eruption status before arriving. Episodes in this series have ranged from a few hours to less than a day, so timing a visit to coincide with active fountaining requires monitoring updates closely.

**Live monitoring**

The V1cam and other USGS summit webcams provide continuous live imagery of the caldera floor. The Uēkahuna tiltmeter trace is publicly accessible through the HVO website and updates in near-real time. USGS issues update notices when eruption status changes, and the aviation color code shifts from YELLOW to ORANGE or RED when active fountaining begins.

If Episode 57 follows the trajectory of previous episodes in the current series, the eruption will begin with lava oozing onto the caldera floor, followed by rapid escalation to fountaining within minutes. The full episode timeline for prior events this series has ranged from under six hours to approximately 12 hours.

## Story 126 — Kakgold Recalls 4WD Remote-Controlled Car Toys Sold on Amazon Over Deadly Button-Cell Battery Hazard

Parents who bought a lime-green or blue Kakgold 4WD stunt remote-controlled car on Amazon between July and September 2026 should take it away from children immediately. The Consumer Product Safety Commission issued Recall #27-085 on November 12, 2026, covering about 294 Kakgold 4WD Stunt Vehicle Toys. The remote control contains button-cell and lithium-ion batteries that children can easily access, posing a risk of deadly internal chemical burns if swallowed. No incidents or injuries have been reported.

**What the recall covers**

The recalled toys are Kakgold 4WD Stunt Vehicle Toys featuring a lime green and blue body on a black chassis, four black wheels with lime green trim and diagonal rollers, checkered patterns, and "X," "PRO," and "RACING" markings on the body. A white hang tag attached to the product reads "Do Not Twist the Shaft" in red text.

They were sold on Amazon.com from July through September 2026 for $34 to $50. The retailer is Shenzhenshi Juteng Lechuang Technology Co., Ltd., doing business as Kakgold, based in China.

The hazard is in the remote control, not the car itself. The button-cell and lithium-ion batteries inside the remote are accessible to small children without tools — meaning a young child can remove and swallow them without an adult noticing.

**Why button cells are dangerous**

Button-cell batteries can pass through the throat of a small child and lodge in the esophagus or digestive tract, where they generate an electrical current that causes rapid internal chemical burns. That damage can occur within two hours and can be fatal. The batteries in question are small enough that swallowing one is not obvious from the outside — a child may not show symptoms immediately.

This is the same mechanism behind ongoing federal legislation and industry standards aimed at making button-cell battery compartments harder for children to open. The Kakgold remote's compartment did not meet the mandatory toy safety standard for accessible battery containment.

**How to identify the toy**

Look for the lime green and blue body, black chassis, diagonal wheel rollers, "PRO" and "RACING" markings, and the white hang tag reading "Do Not Twist the Shaft" in red. If you purchased a matching RC car on Amazon for $34 to $50 between July and September 2026, it is part of the recall regardless of whether a child has accessed the battery compartment.

**How to claim the full refund**

CPSC Recall #27-085 provides a full refund through the following process:

1. Take the toy away from children immediately.
2. Write **"RECALLED"** on the product.
3. Photograph the toy with the word "RECALLED" written on it, in a trash disposal setting.
4. Email the photo to **kakrecall@outlook.com** to request a full refund.
5. Dispose of the toy.

**Safe battery disposal**

CPSC specifically instructs consumers not to discard the button-cell batteries in regular household trash. Button-cell batteries contain materials that require hazardous waste handling. Most municipal areas operate household hazardous waste drop-off programs, and many retail electronics stores — including Best Buy locations — accept loose batteries for recycling. Earth911.com's search tool can locate the nearest accepted drop-off point by zip code.

This recall arrives weeks before the holiday shopping season. If a Kakgold RC car was purchased as a gift and is currently stored unopened, it falls under the same recall. Check Amazon order history for July through September 2026 purchases matching this description.

## Story 123 — USDOT: Obsolete 2001 Routers Flooded FAA Network During Arkansas Maintenance, Grounding 3,500+ Flights

Air travelers who experienced delays during Friday's FAA nationwide ground stop now have a detailed technical explanation. The US Department of Transportation disclosed on November 15, 2026, that the outage affecting more than 3,500 flights traced back to a routine copper-wire replacement in El Dorado, Arkansas — not a cyberattack — but the specific interaction it triggered exposed aging network infrastructure purchased in 2001 and declared obsolete in 2006.

**The El Dorado and Amarillo cascade**

The sequence began with a standard contractor operation: replacing a discontinued copper wire at a facility in El Dorado, Arkansas. That routine work interacted with a separate system in Amarillo, Texas, that L3Harris had replaced approximately three weeks earlier. The interaction caused the older routers on the FAA network to begin broadcasting — a network condition known as a broadcast flood, in which routers repeatedly send data to every node on a network rather than directing traffic normally. The result was a congestion cascade that brought the FAA's air traffic management network down across the country on November 13, 2026.

Transportation Secretary Sean P. Duffy confirmed no foreign cyberattack is indicated. The router involved in the flood is currently being examined by FAA engineers and L3Harris technicians at an L3Harris laboratory in Florida. The FBI offered assistance and the investigation is ongoing, but all current evidence points to a hardware interaction rather than external intrusion.

**What those 2001 routers reveal**

The affected routers were purchased in 2001 and classified as obsolete in 2006 — twenty years ago. That detail is not incidental. It reflects a broader pattern documented in a 2024 Government Accountability Office report, which identified 51 of the FAA's 138 critical systems as unsustainable. The core FAA network architecture was designed in 1980. The radar systems feeding it date to the 1960s and 1970s. Many of the operational software systems layered on top of that infrastructure were built in the 1990s.

A network built on 1980s architecture, running 1990s software, with routers that became obsolete in 2006, is not resilient to unexpected hardware interactions even during routine maintenance — which is exactly what happened on Friday.

**What $12.5 billion covers — and what it doesn't**

The July 2025 Working Families Tax Cut Act provided $12.5 billion for FAA infrastructure modernization. That funding is allocated and moving: 70% of the FAA's copper wiring is being replaced with fiber, 143 towers are being upgraded through the Surface Awareness Initiative, 469 radio sites are being updated, 224 IP Voice Switches are being replaced, and 9 SMR4 Surface Movement Radars are being installed. The full deployment timeline runs through the end of 2028.

What that $12.5 billion does not fully cover is a replacement telecommunications backbone — the equivalent of switching the entire FAA network from aging infrastructure maintained under separate contracts to a modern carrier network. USDOT is seeking additional Congressional funding for that transition, targeting a network built on AT&T, Verizon, and Lumen infrastructure. The target completion date for that telecom overhaul is late 2028 to early 2029, assuming Congress acts on the funding request.

The El Dorado incident happened on a portion of the network that the current $12.5 billion modernization program is in the process of upgrading. The copper-wire replacement that triggered the cascade was part of that upgrade work — which makes the outage not a failure of the modernization program so much as a complication of it, occurring in the transition period before older components are fully retired.

**What it means for holiday travel**

No residual operational disruption remains from the November 13 outage as of November 15. Flight operations returned to normal the same day. For travelers booked over the Thanksgiving period — November 26 through December 1 — USDOT's disclosures do not point to a specific upcoming risk. The L3Harris lab investigation is intended to identify whether the interaction between the replaced Amarillo system and the older network components requires a broader audit of similar installations nationwide.

The practical takeaway for holiday travelers is not to expect a repeat event but to monitor USDOT and FAA status pages during travel. Major network disruptions from infrastructure interactions have historically been single-event occurrences once the specific hardware involved is identified and replaced. The router now in the Florida lab will not return to service.

The FAA's current flight delay information is available at fly.faa.gov. USDOT will provide further updates when the L3Harris diagnostic results are available.
