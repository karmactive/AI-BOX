## **ELABORATION OF THE SUGGESTIONS POINTS**

**1. Two storms already this season (Arthur, Bertha).** NOAA's August update states the 2026 Atlantic season has produced two named tropical storms so far — Arthur and Bertha — and no hurricanes. This directly counters the impression that the basin is dormant. Fact source: NOAA August 2026 Atlantic seasonal outlook update (noaa.gov news release). Why it matters for the piece: readers scanning "3 areas of interest" may think "nothing has happened" — the accurate frame is "two storms already, more possible, season below normal overall."

**2. NOAA below-normal numbers.** The May 2026 outlook called for 7–13 named storms, 2–6 hurricanes, 1–3 major (NOAA). The August update held a below-normal call with a range of 8–14 named storms, 3–6 hurricanes, 1–3 major hurricanes (per NOAA August update material). These are the figures to anchor the "below normal" claim. The season runs June 1–Nov 30.

**3. El Nino and wind shear, simply.** El Nino is a warming of the central/eastern Pacific that changes global wind patterns. Over the Atlantic it strengthens high-altitude winds (wind shear) that tilt and shred developing storms. That is the physical reason the central Atlantic tropical wave is expected to weaken near the Lesser Antilles — the NHC TWO explicitly cites "upper-level winds expected to become less conducive." Plain-language: warm Pacific air up high = hostile winds for Atlantic storms.

**4. Saharan Air Layer (dry dust).** The Cabo Verde low sits downstream of the Sahara. Bands of dry, dusty air (the Saharan Air Layer) suppress thunderstorms needed for a storm to organize. NHC notes the system "will have to navigate bouts of Saharan Air." This is the swing factor for whether it becomes Tropical Storm Dolly (FOX/Bryan Norcross) or falls apart.

**5. Climatological peak ~Sept 10.** Historically the most active day of the Atlantic season is around September 10; the ramp builds from mid-August. This explains why "3 areas of interest" in early August is normal seasonal behavior, not an anomaly.

**6. Exact NHC percentages (Aug 10 TWO):** Central Atlantic wave — 48h 10%, 7-day 10%. Eastern/Central Atlantic low (south of Cabo Verde) — 48h 20%, 7-day 60%. Western subtropical (NW of Bermuda) — 48h 10%, 7-day 10%. No warnings; no land threat per NHC and all local sources.

**7. Name order.** WMO 2026 list: Arthur, Bertha, Cristobal, Dolly, Edouard, Fay… Arthur and Bertha already used; Cristobal and Dolly are next. Confirms local reporting.

**8. Preparedness (helpful, trustworthy).** With no immediate threat, the calm window is the time to confirm a household hurricane plan: know your evacuation zone, check supplies, sign up for local alerts. This serves E-E-A-T without speculating on future storms.

**9. Central Pacific note (optional).** A separate system (CP93) ~1,100 mi ESE of Hawaii carries 50%/90% 7-day formation odds (NHC Central Pacific). Out of Atlantic scope; include only if broadening the "basin-wide" view.
