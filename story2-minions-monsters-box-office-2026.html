Box Office

"Minions & Monsters," the seventh film in the Despicable Me franchise and Illumination's third Minions spinoff, opened in 4,243 North American theaters on Wednesday, July 1, 2026, and closed out the July 4th holiday frame with the smallest opening in the series' 16-year history. The film took in $61.4 million over its first five days domestically and $36.4 million across the traditional Friday-to-Sunday window, coming in below the studio's hopes for the long weekend. Despite the soft domestic number, the film opened to the best reviews of any Despicable Me or Minions entry yet, holding a 91% score on Rotten Tomatoes alongside an A- CinemaScore from opening-weekend audiences. Internationally, the film added roughly $85 million across 71 markets over the same weekend, pushing its worldwide total past $159 million. Box office analyst David A. Gross, who publishes the FranchiseRe newsletter, described the result plainly: "Seven is further than any animation series has gone. Audiences are showing fatigue now," adding that "the movie will be profitable, but it's a misfire." Illumination's release calendar may have played a role too — "Minions & Monsters" was originally scheduled for mid-2027 before moving up a full year to fill the July 1, 2026 date vacated when DreamWorks postponed "Shrek 5," landing in a release week that also had "Toy Story 5" still playing in its third weekend. That Pixar sequel actually out-earned "Minions & Monsters" on a same-weekend basis despite already being in theaters for three weeks. The shifted release calendar can be tracked alongside other 2026 studio slate changes, including the delay of "Avengers: Doomsday" to December 2026. Since its 2010 launch, the Despicable Me and Minions franchise has taken in more than $5.5 billion worldwide, making it the highest-grossing animated franchise in box office history. Illumination founder and CEO Chris Meledandri has said the studio's earlier commercial track record "let us completely cut loose on this film," while director Pierre Coffin has described wanting each Minions project to feel distinct: "If I was going to pull back into that world and direct another movie with Minions, I really, really needed, just for my own sanity, to make it really different from whatever had been made before." In the studio's official box office statement, Universal's domestic distribution chief Jim Orr said, "Illumination once again provides global audiences with a film that audiences are embracing." In Summary

"Minions & Monsters" opened domestically at $61.4 million over five days and $36.4 million over the traditional three-day weekend, the lowest opening totals recorded for the Despicable Me and Minions franchise since it began in 2010. The film carried a Rotten Tomatoes score of 91% and an A- CinemaScore, its highest marks to date. An estimated $85 million production budget was reported alongside a worldwide opening gross above $159 million, with roughly $85 million of that total coming from international markets. Analyst commentary attributed the domestic result to franchise fatigue and same-weekend competition from "Toy Story 5."
