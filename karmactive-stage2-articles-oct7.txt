The proportion of adult vapers in Great Britain who had never regularly smoked tobacco rose from approximately 0.5% in 2020 to over 3.5% by 2024–2026 — translating to more than one million people in absolute terms. The NHS, Cancer Research UK, and successive governments promoted vaping as a quit-smoking tool throughout the 2010s. For young adults who never smoked cigarettes, the entry point was not harm reduction — it was flavored disposable vapes marketed as a consumer lifestyle product. The pending Tobacco and Vapes Bill includes provisions to restrict disposable e-cigarette sales, ban many of the flavors most associated with youth uptake, and impose higher excise duties. People who do not smoke and are considering vaping should weigh the absence of any harm-reduction benefit against a genuine risk of nicotine dependency.

Boise Secures $9 Million Federal Grant to Build 50 Permanent Supportive Housing Units


Boise has secured a $9 million federal grant to develop a 50-unit permanent supportive housing complex for individuals exiting chronic homelessness, with the funding covering both capital construction costs and multi-year on-site behavioral health services. Mayor Lauren McLean announced the award from the US Department of Housing and Urban Development's Continuum of Care Builds program, which funds new permanent housing rather than temporary shelter beds.

The $9 million HUD award is directed to The Fulton, a planned development near 15th and Front streets in Boise. It will create 50 permanent, subsidized apartments combined with mandatory on-site mental health counseling, addiction recovery support, and healthcare services. Under HUD program rules, no more than 20% of the grant may go to eligible activities outside direct capital construction. The project requires additional financing beyond the federal grant before a construction schedule can be confirmed; no groundbreaking date has been announced. Mayor McLean attributed the award to the city's Continuum of Care application to the HUD housing competition.

For Boise taxpayers and downtown residents, the $9 million federal investment directly funds 50 permanent homes paired with mandatory on-site mental health and addiction recovery services. By moving chronically unhoused individuals into permanent supportive housing, the project aims to reduce recurring emergency room visits, police dispatches, and public camping enforcement costs across Ada County without drawing from the city's municipal general tax fund.

The distinction between permanent supportive housing and emergency shelters matters for what the $9 million actually purchases. Temporary shelters provide overnight beds but do not break the cycle that brings people back repeatedly: emergency room visits, police contacts, short-term incarcerations. Permanent supportive housing — the model federal policy calls Housing First — provides a legal residential lease backed by wraparound services addressing the underlying health conditions driving chronic homelessness. The $9 million covers both the physical construction and guaranteed long-term behavioral health specialist staffing, preventing the facility from becoming an unfunded liability in future city budgets.

Studies of comparable projects in other mid-sized Western US cities have found that per-person costs for subsidized permanent housing are lower than the combined municipal emergency services expenditure for chronic street homelessness. Ada County taxpayers already absorb the recurring cost of emergency responses in the absence of this type of facility.

On what Boise's new $9 million homeless housing project is: it is a 50-unit permanent supportive housing development funded by a $9 million federal HUD grant. It provides permanent, subsidized apartments integrated with on-site medical care and mental health counseling for people exiting chronic homelessness.

Additional city and state financing is still being assembled before construction can begin. No groundbreaking schedule has been confirmed. Further city approvals and funding announcements will precede work beginning near 15th and Front streets.
