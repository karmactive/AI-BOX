**Title:** Check Your Halloween Candy: Crystal Temptations Chocolatey Eyeballs Recalled for Undeclared Milk
**SEO Slug:** halloween-chocolatey-eyeballs-recall-milk-allergen-2026
**Category:** Health / Food Safety
**Tags:** food-recall, food-safety, fda, consumer-safety, allergy, allergy-alert, consumer-alert

If you picked up Halloween candy from TJ Maxx, Marshalls, HomeGoods, or Sierra Trading Post recently, stop and check the bag before anyone eats it.

[Crystal Temptations](https://www.karmactive.com/wegmans-chocolate-recall-affects-9-states-2026/), a food company based in North Arlington, New Jersey, has recalled its Halloween [Chocolatey Eyeballs](https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/crystal-temptations-issues-allergy-alert-undeclared-milk-chocolatey-eyeballs#:~:text=Crystal%20Temptations%20issues%20allergy%20alert) because the product contains [milk that was never listed on the label](https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/crystal-temptations-issues-allergy-alert-undeclared-milk-chocolatey-eyeballs). The recall was announced on August 31, 2026.

This is not a lactose intolerance concern. It is a [milk allergy warning](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-labeling-nutrition/food-allergies#:~:text=milk%20allergy). For people with a milk allergy or severe sensitivity to milk, consuming the product could cause a serious or life-threatening allergic reaction.

A TJX analyst first caught the problem when whey, a protein derived from milk, was found in the product while milk was completely absent from the ingredient label.

The candy was sold across 13 states: Arizona, California, Connecticut, Georgia, Indiana, Massachusetts, Nevada, New Jersey, North Carolina, Ohio, Texas, Virginia, and Wyoming.

Five specific product styles are affected:

- 54040-CHEY — 10 oz plastic bag
- 54077-CHEY — 7 oz acrylic box
- 58008-CHEY — 10.5 oz round plastic jar
- 58089-CHEY — 16 oz designer plastic pouch
- 54083-CHEY — 11 oz plastic bag with tag

No illnesses had been reported at the time of the recall notice.

Do not try to return the product to the store — retail locations are not offering refunds. Instead, hold onto your proof of purchase and contact [Crystal Temptations](https://www.karmactive.com/donutful-chocolate-dipped-donuts-recall-undeclared-milk-2026/) directly regarding a possible refund.

**Title:** Only 15% of San Francisco Schools Are Connected to the Existing Biking Network
**SEO Slug:** san-francisco-safe-school-connections-sfmta-2026
**Category:** News / Policy / USA
**Tags:** education, community, policy, government, safety, transportation, cycling, public-transportation

Walking or [biking your kid to school](https://www.sfmta.com/projects/safe-school-connections) in San Francisco sounds simple. The reality is that only 37 out of 249 schools — roughly 15% — are currently connected to the city's established biking and rolling network for people of all ages and abilities.

That's the opening number from the San Francisco Municipal Transportation Agency's [draft Safe School Connections plan](https://www.sfmta.com/safe-school-connections-draft-approach-september-2026#:~:text=Safe%20School%20Connections%20draft%20approach), which aims to establish a safe and accessible connection from all 249 K-12 schools in San Francisco to the city's biking and rolling network.

The math shows just how far there is to go. Another 57 schools, about 23%, are connected to a planned future network that doesn't fully exist yet. Along with schools within one block of current and potential networks, 73 remaining schools are located farther out, requiring distinct custom routing solutions.

The reason the numbers vary by school is partly by design. For the 2026–27 school year, SFUSD continues to use a citywide-choice assignment system, and the district says many families travel across San Francisco to reach their schools.

What SFMTA is calling a "School Connector" is not just a painted bike lane. The draft approach includes a toolkit of possible measures, including traffic calming, speed humps, raised crosswalks, signage and other street-safety treatments, with the specific mix determined by local conditions.

SFMTA says connecting every school could take up to 10 years. The first schools in line are those already within one block of the existing or planned network — so progress will build outward from where the infrastructure is already closest.

The agency hasn't been standing still in the meantime. It says it has already completed more than 40 school crossing improvements and removed sight-blocking objects from more than 2,300 school intersections, a process called daylighting. New 15-mph and 20-mph school zone speed limits have also been put in place.

The current draft is open for public comment until September 30, 2026. After that, SFMTA expects to present the final approach to its Board for approval by early 2027.

If you're a parent, school employee, or neighborhood resident in San Francisco and you have specific intersections or routes you think need attention, the window to say so officially closes at the end of this month.

On September 2, a major Chicago roadway came to a standstill because the bridge wouldn't close.

The [DuSable Lake Shore Drive bridge](https://www.karmactive.com/columbus-ohio-heat-advisory-104-heat-index-september-4-2026/#:~:text=extreme%20weather) over the Chicago River was lifted for a routine pass-through around 10 a.m. When operators tried to bring it back down, it wouldn't fully close. The bridge stayed open — and traffic stayed blocked — until shortly after 2:30 p.m., a disruption of more than four hours.

The cause, according to [city officials](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/cdot.html#:~:text=bridge%20operations), was heat. Extreme temperatures can cause steel components to expand, and engineering experts said the expansion interfered with the bridge's ability to close properly.

Crews from the Chicago Fire Department cooled the steel structure with water and made adjustments that allowed the bridge to close.

What made this story more than a heat-related traffic inconvenience is what a [2024 inspection had found](https://idot.illinois.gov/#:~:text=bridge%20inspection). City and state inspection records classified the DuSable Lake Shore Drive bridge as both structurally deficient and functionally obsolete.

That terminology is worth understanding carefully. Structurally deficient does not mean a bridge is about to fall down. Under federal inspection standards, the designation means that certain structural elements have deteriorated to a condition where they need additional monitoring, maintenance, or eventual rehabilitation. It indicates that [elements need to be watched and maintained](https://www.karmactive.com/rogers-stadium-toronto-storm-damage-collapse-2026/#:~:text=structural%20damage), but not that structural collapse is imminent.

Chicago has a large and aging bridge network crossing the Chicago River, and maintenance on urban infrastructure is a common challenge across American cities.

No injuries were reported.

**Title:** Conservation Groups and Tribes Are Suing to Restore Utah's National Monuments. The Legal Battle Just Got a New Chapter.
**SEO Slug:** bears-ears-grand-staircase-escalante-lawsuit-2026-antiquities-act
**Category:** Policy / Environment / Nature
**Tags:** environmental-law, environmental-lawsuit, conservation, policy, politics, donald-trump, environmental-protection, indigenous

Two of America's most significant stretches of public land had their protections dramatically reduced in July 2026. Now that decision is heading back to court.

On July 13, President Trump signed [proclamations reducing the size](https://www.whitehouse.gov/proclamations/bears-ears/#:~:text=proclaimed) of [Grand Staircase-Escalante National Monument](https://www.whitehouse.gov/proclamations/grand-staircase-escalante/#:~:text=monument%20proclamation) and [Bears Ears National Monument](https://www.whitehouse.gov/proclamations/bears-ears/#:~:text=Bears%20Ears) in Utah. The numbers are striking. Grand Staircase-Escalante was cut from approximately 1.87 million acres to about 181,500 acres — a reduction of more than 90%. Bears Ears was cut from approximately 1.36 million acres to about 121,100 acres, a similarly steep reduction.

The White House's stated rationale is that the remaining areas still protect specific historic, scientific, and cultural objects, while the land removed from monument status can now be managed for multiple uses, including mining and grazing.

On September 2, [conservation groups and other plaintiffs](https://www.nrdc.org/press-releases/nrdc-sues-block-trumps-gutting-bears-ears-and-grand-staircase-escalante-national#:~:text=conservation%20groups) moved to revive their earlier federal challenges and contest the 2026 proclamations.

The core legal question is whether a president can actually shrink or eliminate a national monument once it's been established. The [Antiquities Act of 1906](https://www.karmactive.com/trump-birthright-citizenship-orders-august-2026-scotus/#:~:text=executive%20actions) gives the executive branch clear authority to create national monuments. The challengers argue that the Antiquities Act gives presidents authority to designate monuments but does not authorize them to dismantle existing monuments. That legal dispute is now being contested in federal court.

The [Bears Ears case](https://bearsearscoalition.org/#:~:text=Bears%20Ears%20National%20Monument) carries specific weight because of how the monument was established. It was created in 2016 following unified advocacy by five Tribal nations: the Hopi Tribe, Navajo Nation, Ute Indian Tribe, Ute Mountain Ute Tribe, and Pueblo of Zuni. The monument included a co-management framework designed to give those nations a formal role in decisions about the land. According to the Bears Ears Inter-Tribal Coalition, the reductions remove that framework from the majority of the originally designated area.

The administration's position is that its 2026 proclamations set more appropriate boundaries. It argues that the law grants the president both the power to designate and the power to adjust monument boundaries. Conservation groups and tribal nations say that reading goes far beyond what Congress intended when it passed the Antiquities Act more than a century ago.

The legal proceedings are now active in federal court. No ruling has been issued.

**Title:** An 85-Year-Old WWII Shipwreck Off Albania Is Now Home to Critically Endangered Seals — and an Invasive Venomous Fish
**SEO Slug:** wwii-hospital-ship-po-shipwreck-albania-monk-seal-devil-firefish-2026
**Category:** Science / Nature / Marine Life
**Tags:** marine-life, conservation, endangered-species, ocean, ecosystem, archaeology, science, discovery, history

In August 2023, researchers photographing marine life near a sunken WWII warship off the Albanian coast captured something that hadn't been recorded in that bay for 27 years: a [Mediterranean monk seal](https://www.iucnredlist.org/species/13653/45227543#:~:text=Mediterranean%20monk%20seal).

The vessel is the [Hospital Ship Po](https://www.karmactive.com/uk-hottest-summer-2026-record-16-5c-met-office-130-times-climate-change/#:~:text=marine%20warming) — an Italian vessel that was converted for military hospital service and sunk in 1941. It sits at between 33 and 37 meters depth in Vlora Bay, inside the Karaburun-Sazan Marine Protected Area along the Albanian coast. The ship is approximately 135 meters long and has provided hard substrate for marine organisms to colonize over the last 85 years.

Researchers conducted a detailed monitoring program between 2022 and 2024. They used scientific dives, remotely operated vehicles, high-resolution sonar, 3D photogrammetry, and biological and sediment surveys.

What they found was substantial. In the broader seabed survey around the wreck, researchers recorded 151 different benthic taxa — that's the term for organisms living on or near the seafloor, including sponges, corals, worms, mollusks, and various invertebrates. On the wreck structures themselves, 54 distinct taxa were recorded. More than 80% of the ship's surface was covered in what researchers describe as biogenic material — meaning living organisms had colonized most of it.

The monk seal photograph is the most striking finding. The Mediterranean monk seal, known scientifically as Monachus monachus, is one of the most endangered marine mammals in the world. The researchers describe the August 2023 photograph near the Po wreck as the first confirmed sighting in Vlora Bay since 1996. Whether the seal was using the wreck as a feeding ground, a resting area, or was passing through remains unknown — the authors say longer-term behavioral research is needed to understand how the seal is using the area.

The wreck is not entirely a conservation success story, and the researchers are careful not to present it as one. Alongside the native species, the study documented the non-indigenous devil firefish, Pterois miles, one of the northernmost reported occurrences of the species in the Adriatic. The [peer-reviewed research](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ocean-sustainability/articles/10.3389/focsu.2026.1762680/full#:~:text=shipwreck%20research) examining this complex ecosystem establishes important ecological baselines.

The ecological picture the study presents is genuinely complex. A shipwreck provides hard structure in an otherwise soft-bottomed environment. Fish and invertebrates colonize it. A critically endangered seal turns up near it. But so does an invasive predator that wasn't supposed to be there at all.

The researchers also found evidence of squid-family spawning on or near the wreck — another sign that the structure is being actively used by marine life.

What the study establishes is that the Po wreck has high benthic diversity and structural complexity. The researchers describe the site as having these characteristics while emphasizing that the ecological indices are preliminary and site-specific. What the study does not establish is that the wreck has restored or repaired the surrounding ecosystem. The long-term effects, including how the monk seal and the devil firefish each continue to use the area, are still being studied.
