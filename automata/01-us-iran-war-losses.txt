US Iran war losses revealed as Pentagon watchdog reports damaged aircraft, hundreds of buildings and $37.5 billion cost

If you have been wondering what the US-Iran war has actually cost America in real terms — beyond the battlefield headlines — a new Pentagon watchdog report puts the damage into plain numbers for the first time.

A Department of Defense Inspector General assessment covering April through June 2026 has tallied the full scale of losses and costs from Iranian attacks on US military and diplomatic facilities. The figures go far beyond what official statements have acknowledged until now.

Among the losses documented:

- Up to 30 MQ-9 Reaper drones lost or damaged
- Seven KC-135 aerial refuelling aircraft lost or damaged
- Hundreds of buildings at US military bases damaged
- 12 US service members killed
- $184 million in damage to US diplomatic facilities
- $113 million in emergency response and evacuation costs
- Approximately 9,000 Americans evacuated
- Total US war cost: approximately $37.5 billion for the period reviewed

**What this means for Americans paying attention to the war**

These figures give US taxpayers and military families the clearest picture yet of what Iranian retaliation has cost. The $37.5 billion war cost covers only the period under the watchdog's review — not the full conflict. The loss of KC-135 refuelling aircraft is particularly significant because those planes extend the range of US strike aircraft across the region. Losing seven of them is not an equipment footnote — it is a constraint on what the US military can do and where.

**The logistics story behind the equipment losses**

The Pentagon assessment does something most battlefield reporting does not: it connects the equipment damage to operational consequences. Iranian attacks did not only destroy hardware. They forced US Central Command to reroute supply and logistics chains away from affected bases, with Diego Garcia — a remote island base in the Indian Ocean — becoming a more heavily relied-upon alternative.

That shift matters because Diego Garcia is significantly farther from the operational theatre. Longer supply routes mean slower resupply, higher fuel costs, and greater logistical strain on forces in the field. The drone and aircraft losses compound this: the MQ-9 Reaper is the US military's primary armed surveillance drone, used for both reconnaissance and strike missions. Losing up to 30 of them reduces the military's ability to monitor and respond to threats simultaneously.

The KC-135 losses are equally telling. These aircraft are not combat planes — they are the flying fuel stations that allow fighter jets and bombers to operate over long distances. Their loss narrows the geographic reach of US air power in the region.

As reported previously on Karmactive, US CENTCOM launched significant retaliatory strikes targeting Iran's strategic maritime assets in the Strait of Hormuz. The watchdog report now provides the cost side of that exchange. Earlier coverage of Iran's attack on the Bahrain naval base and USS Abraham Lincoln also documented the scale of Iranian retaliation — the Inspector General assessment confirms the cumulative toll.

**The $37.5 billion figure in context**

To put $37.5 billion in terms that connect to daily life: it exceeds the annual budget of several US federal agencies. It is roughly the equivalent of funding the entire US Department of Energy for three years. That spending covers weapons, logistics, personnel, emergency evacuations, and the reconstruction of damaged facilities — but represents only the period the watchdog reviewed, not the total cost of the conflict.

The 9,000 Americans evacuated from the region represent a significant non-military dimension of the war's impact — a reminder that the conflict has affected civilian personnel and diplomatic staff, not only military forces.
