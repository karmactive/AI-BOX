## Headline
India's Circular Economy Policy Emphasizes Resource Value and End-of-Life Vehicle Requirements

## Article

India's approach to the circular economy is shifting emphasis. At the World Circular Economy Forum 2026 in Gandhinagar, officials are focusing on keeping resources useful longer rather than simply measuring waste volumes. For India's automotive sector, this means policy attention is turning toward how vehicle materials can be recovered and reused, driven by the existing End-of-Life Vehicles EPR framework.

**WCEF 2026 Sets the Policy Direction**

The World Circular Economy Forum 2026 opened in Gandhinagar, Gujarat on September 15 and runs through September 18. More than 3,600 participants from 62 countries and 135 exhibitors attended the forum, making it India's first hosting of WCEF in South Asia. The forum's official theme is "Circular Economy: Transition for People and Prosperity."

Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav opened the forum with remarks emphasizing value preservation. He stated the true measure of a circular economy should be "how little value we allow to be wasted," rather than simply measuring volumes of waste recycled. India's existing EPR framework already covers more than 83,000 producers and 4,802 recyclers, with 482.24 lakh tonnes of waste processed to date.

**India's End-of-Life Vehicles Rules**

India's Environment Protection (End-of-Life Vehicles) Rules, notified on January 6, 2025, establish mandatory EPR obligations for vehicle producers. These rules are already in force, not pending. The framework sets EPR targets based on the steel content used in vehicles from earlier model years. For the initial compliance period from 2027–28, producers must meet an 8% EPR target calculated against the steel in vehicles from the relevant prior year. Targets rise to 13% and then 18% in later compliance periods.

**What EPR Means in Practice**

The EPR framework requires producers to ensure end-of-life vehicle materials are recovered and recycled according to the specified targets. This is not a new 2027 mandate—the rules were notified in January 2025. However, the enforcement timeline for manufacturers is phased. Starting 2027–28, producers are responsible for demonstrating they have met the steel-recovery targets for vehicles they placed on the market in earlier years.

This distinction matters for supply chain planning, as plug-in hybrids require both battery systems and conventional drivetrains, while battery-electric vehicles do not.

**How Close Is the Target**

August 2026 CAAM data, as reported by Chinese government information services, shows NEVs reached 60.6% of China's monthly new-car sales. The September 2026 plan targets 70% by 2030, representing approximately 10 additional percentage points. This timeline is a significant push, but from a baseline where the category is already dominant in the market.

## Headline
UK Environment Agency to Implement New Waste Compliance Metrics

## Article

The UK's Office for Environmental Protection found that the Environment Agency's current waste-site compliance metrics are misleading. The agency has been reporting high compliance rates based on a system that does not accurately measure environmental improvement. The government has accepted the OEP's recommendation and is developing new key performance indicators.

**What the OEP Found**

The OEP's review, published in May 2026, examined Environment Agency inspections of waste operations and installations in England. The key finding: the current compliance metric gives the agency credit for inspections performed without necessarily measuring whether environmental hazards identified in those inspections were actually resolved.

More specifically, the OEP found that the 97% compliance KPI includes automatic Band A classification for sites that have not been inspected. This means the reported compliance rate does not reflect actual environmental assurance. In fact, the OEP concluded the Environment Agency could only be confident in compliance at approximately 64% of waste sites, even though the agency's reported figure was 97%.

This gap between reported compliance and actual confidence in environmental protection is the central problem the OEP identified.

[INTERNAL LINK — UK environmental enforcement or waste operations policy: place after "environmental protection"]

**What the Government Response Says**

The government's response, dated September 15, 2026, accepts the OEP's findings and commits to change. The government says the Environment Agency will develop new key performance indicators that better reflect actual environmental compliance and improvement, rather than administrative throughput metrics. The new KPIs are planned for introduction in 2027.

This means the current system will remain in place until 2027, but the direction of change is set: inspections will be assessed on their environmental outcomes, not simply on numbers of inspections conducted.

**What Doesn't Change**

The government explicitly states that inspections themselves are not ending. The Environment Agency will continue using site inspections as a compliance tool, along with alternative assessment methods. What is changing is how those inspections are measured and reported.

Waste facility operators remain subject to current environmental requirements. There is no grace period. The 2027 implementation timeline refers to when the new metrics themselves take effect, not a deadline for operators to upgrade their facilities.

The new metrics will measure actual environmental improvements and hazards resolved.

**When will the new metrics take effect?**
The government plans to introduce new key performance indicators in 2027. The Environment Agency will continue using existing metrics until the new system is implemented.

**Will waste facility inspections end?**
No. The government explicitly states that inspections will continue. The change is in how inspections are measured and reported, not the elimination of inspections themselves.

**Closure**

The OEP found the Environment Agency's compliance metrics were misleading due to automatic Band A classification for uninspected sites. The government accepted the finding and committed to new key performance indicators in 2027. The current inspection system remains active until the new metrics take effect.
