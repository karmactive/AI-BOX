# Dodamarg Tree Felling: Over 6,000 Trees Cleared Near Mopa Airport as Sasoli Villagers Protest

If you are considering buying land or a plotted villa near Goa's Mopa Airport, or if you are a local landholder in Sindhudurg's Sasoli village, one question matters more than any political statement currently making the rounds: is the real estate being sold in this corridor legally clear for construction?

The answer, based on judicial records and Maharashtra's land registry, is that large portions of this area are not.

## What Is Happening in Sasoli

Over 6,000 trees have been felled and hill terraces leveled in Sasoli village in Sindhudurg district, Maharashtra, according to a formal legislative memorandum submitted by NCP (Sharadchandra Pawar) leader Jayant Patil to Revenue Minister Chandrashekhar Bawankule. Ground video documentation from local creators showed Poclain excavators working on steep laterite slopes in the area. Citizen alerts prompted local officials to temporarily impound the machinery involved.

Patil's letter alleges that land mafia operating through developers based in Delhi and Haryana are behind the clearing operation. The Revenue Minister has promised an administrative inquiry.

The village is located in the Sawantwadi–Dodamarg corridor of Sindhudurg district, adjacent to the Mopa International Airport boundary in North Goa.

## What Buyers and Landholders Need to Know

Anyone purchasing plotted land or villas in the Sasoli–Dodamarg corridor under the assumption that it is regularized Goa-border property faces immediate legal risk. The Bombay High Court maintains an active embargo on tree felling across this Western Ghats wildlife corridor. Unauthorized hill clearing and disputed non-agricultural conversions face administrative stays, frozen property title transfers, and demolition orders from revenue authorities.

That risk is not hypothetical. It is grounded in active judicial orders and Maharashtra land law.

## The Judicial and Environmental Framework

The Bombay High Court issued binding orders in Public Interest Litigation No. 179 of 2012 — Vanashakti & Anr v. Union of India — mandating preservation of the Sawantwadi–Dodamarg corridor and restraining unpermitted tree cutting. Any commercial clear-cutting without authorization in this area is therefore not just an administrative violation. It is a contempt matter.

The Central government has demarcated 212.59 square kilometres across 25 villages in the Sawantwadi–Dodamarg taluka as an Ecologically Sensitive Area (ESA). Under ESA rules, commercial township development, hill leveling, and unpermitted clear-felling are prohibited. Private land cannot be converted for non-agricultural commercial layouts without verified clearances from the District Collector and the Tree Authority.

The Western Ghats Ecology Expert Panel and regional botanists have submitted formal ecological assessments to the Bombay High Court identifying the Sawantwadi–Dodamarg corridor as a prime elephant and tiger movement pathway between the Tillari and Mhadei wildlife sanctuaries. Disrupting this corridor carries ecological consequences that extend beyond Sindhudurg into Goa.

## The "North Goa" Marketing Problem

Developers marketing plots in Sasoli to out-of-state buyers — particularly NRI investors in the US, UK, and UAE — are using the branding "North Goa border villas" and "Mopa Airport suburbs." Maharashtra's land portal (Mahabhumi 7/12) records tell a different story.

These properties remain governed by Maharashtra's strict Western Ghats tree felling embargo. Many parcels involve undivided co-ownership — known as *hissedari* or *hissedar* arrangements — where an agricultural title is shared among multiple family members. Under Maharashtra law, shared agricultural titles cannot be legally converted to non-agricultural (NA) plots without the full consent of all co-sharers. A buyer who purchases from one co-sharer, without obtaining consent from the rest, acquires a disputed title.

The combination of ESA restrictions, High Court orders, and hissedari constraints means secondary buyers face unregularized plots, potential construction halts, and court-ordered demolition risk — regardless of what the developer's marketing material says.

The land is governed by Maharashtra's laws and environmental restrictions, not Goa's.

## What Comes Next

The Revenue Minister's promised administrative inquiry and the pending compliance affidavit before the Bombay High Court are the two official developments that will determine whether this felling episode leads to prosecution, land title freezes, or further clearances. Sasoli's Gram Sabha has already passed a formal, unanimous resolution seeking Eco-Sensitive Area status — a democratic step that puts the village's position formally on record.

The downstream consequences the corridor's communities are already experiencing — dammed mountain springs disrupting water supplies in Gharpi and Udeli villages, and monsoon landslide risk on cleared laterite slopes — will continue to develop regardless of the inquiry's pace.
