STORY 1: HOUSE VOTES ON SUPREME COURT AMENDMENT, MEASURE FAILS SHORT OF TWO-THIRDS
================================================================================

The House rejected a proposed [constitutional amendment](https://constitution.congress.gov/constitution/article-5/) Wednesday that would have locked the [Supreme Court's](https://www.supremecourt.gov/about/faq_general.aspx) membership at nine justices. The vote fell far short of the two-thirds majority needed to advance such a measure.

H.J. Res. 1 received 212 votes in favor and 206 against on September 2, 2026. Advancing the amendment under suspension-of-rules procedures required 291 votes. The outcome reflects partisan division: 211 Republicans voted yes while all 205 voting Democrats opposed it. One Democrat voted in support.

The failed vote made no immediate change to the Court itself. The Supreme Court already consists of nine justices—one Chief Justice and eight associate justices—under [federal law](https://uscode.house.gov/). That number has been set since the Judiciary Act of 1869, though the Court's size fluctuated several times before reaching nine.

The nine-justice structure appears so fundamental today that many assume it has always been the Court's design. In fact, the Court began with six justices when established in 1789. Congress raised the number to seven in 1807, then nine in 1837, and temporarily to ten during the Civil War era.

[Constitutional amendments](https://www.karmactive.com/new-mexico-appeals-court-dismisses-landmark-oil-pollution-lawsuit-fueling-supreme-court-showdown/) differ fundamentally from regular legislation. While the House can pass laws that take effect immediately, proposed amendments require ratification by three-fourths of the states. If this amendment had passed the House and then the Senate with the needed supermajorities, it would have needed approval from 38 of the 50 states before becoming part of the Constitution.

The amendment was proposed to formally lock the Court's size at its current nine-member composition. Under suspension of the rules, a two-thirds majority is required — a threshold the measure did not reach with 212 yes votes.

Wednesday's vote represents the [first formal congressional action](https://www.karmactive.com/trump-federal-court-removal-rejected-34-counts-presidential-immunity-august-2026/) on this issue in the current legislative session, marking an opening debate about the Court's structure.

Several types of storm alerts exist, and the differences matter for safety decisions. A tornado watch means conditions are favorable for tornado development in a region—stay alert but you don't need immediate action. STORY 6: SOUTHEAST WISCONSIN EXPERIENCES WARM SEPTEMBER WEATHER; COOLER PATTERN AHEAD
================================================================================

Temperatures climbed into the upper 80s across Southeast Wisconsin on Wednesday, creating unusual early-September warmth that felt even hotter when humidity was factored in. The [National Weather Service](https://www.weather.gov/mkx/) recorded heat-index values reaching the mid-90s in parts of the region, meaning the combination of temperature and moisture made conditions feel substantially warmer than the actual thermometer reading.

Understanding the difference between temperature and heat index helps residents plan their day appropriately. Temperature is the actual air temperature measured by a thermometer. Heat index is how hot it actually feels when humidity is factored in. High humidity traps heat near the ground, making it harder for sweat—the body's primary cooling mechanism—to evaporate. As humidity rises, the gap between actual temperature and heat index widens. An 88-degree day with high humidity can feel like 95 degrees or more.

Southeast Wisconsin's combination of upper-80s temperatures and elevated humidity created conditions requiring caution for extended outdoor activity. The region also faces [thunderstorm chances](https://www.karmactive.com/climate-change-adds-30-days-of-extreme-heat-for-4-billion-people-doubling-heat-days-in-195-countries/) in scattered locations, a development when warm, moist air moves over land during late summer. Thunderstorms can develop rapidly under these atmospheric conditions.

Forecasters expected the warmth to gradually moderate over the coming days as a front moved through the region, bringing cooler, drier air behind it.

Residents spending extended time outdoors should stay hydrated and drink water regularly. Light-colored, loose-fitting clothing helps reflect heat.

For those tracking the forecast, conditions improve noticeably by Thursday, with even more comfortable conditions expected by the weekend.

STORY 7: VITAMIN C DEFICIENCY IDENTIFIED IN QUEENSLAND CORONER'S FINDINGS
================================================================================

A six-year-old Queensland boy died following cardiac arrest, with the [Queensland Coroners Court](https://www.coronerscourt.qld.gov.au/) finding severe vitamin C deficiency was the likely underlying factor. The official findings were released on August 28, 2026.

The child experienced cardiac arrest following a general anaesthetic. The coroner's investigation identified severe vitamin C deficiency as the likely underlying factor in this event.

The case draws attention to how multivitamin supplements—even those taken regularly—may not deliver all essential nutrients if the label doesn't list them. The boy had been taking a multivitamin, but the label did not include [vitamin C](https://www.karmactive.com/national-vitamin-c-day-april-4-highlights-essential-health-benefits/) among its ingredients. Queensland Health confirms that vitamin C is one of 13 essential vitamins the human body requires but cannot produce in sufficient quantities on its own.

Vitamin C deficiency leads to a condition called scurvy, characterized by weakness, joint and muscle aches, bleeding gums, poor wound healing, and in severe cases, cardiac complications. Historically, scurvy affected sailors on long voyages who lacked access to fresh fruits and vegetables. The disease nearly vanished from developed nations during the twentieth century but remains a concern in populations with severely restricted diets.

The case highlights an important oversight: parents administering a multivitamin supplement to their child may reasonably assume the product covers all essential vitamins. Parents and caregivers should verify that multivitamins actually contain specific nutrients critical for their child's health.

Scurvy develops gradually. Early symptoms include fatigue, joint pain, and swollen gums. As deficiency worsens, bleeding, poor wound healing, and cardiac stress can develop. Testing for vitamin C levels is not part of routine childhood medical screening.

Parents should ensure their child's diet includes regular sources of vitamin C such as citrus fruits, berries, kiwis, tomatoes, and leafy greens, or discuss supplementation directly with their pediatrician.

STORY 8: VICTORIA INVESTIGATES E. COLI CASES INVOLVING PETTING FARM VISIT
================================================================================

A three-year-old girl in Victoria has died after developing a serious illness, with investigation linking the case to a petting farm that was also visited by another affected child. A second child from the same location also became seriously ill.

The bacteria responsible—[Shiga toxin-producing E. coli](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/infectious-diseases/verotoxin-producing-e-coli-vtec), or STEC—can trigger a severe condition called Haemolytic Uraemic Syndrome (HUS) in infected individuals, particularly young children. HUS damages the kidneys and blood cells, potentially causing renal failure and life-threatening complications.

Victoria's [Department of Health](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/) classifies STEC/VTEC as a notifiable infection requiring immediate reporting to authorities. HUS is designated an urgent notifiable condition. Both designations reflect the public-health significance of these illnesses.

Petting farms and petting zoos create risk because animals carry E. coli bacteria in their digestive tracts without becoming sick themselves. Direct contact with animals or contaminated surfaces, combined with hand-to-mouth transfer, transmits bacteria to human visitors. Children under five face particularly high risk of developing HUS after STEC infection.

Symptoms of STEC infection typically appear within two to eight days of exposure, with most cases showing symptoms three to four days after contact. Initial signs include severe abdominal pain and diarrhoea. As infection progresses, diarrhoea often becomes bloody. Some infected people experience only mild symptoms, while others develop severe intestinal illness.

HUS develops in a subset of STEC-infected individuals, particularly young children. The condition causes hemolysis—destruction of red blood cells—combined with platelet destruction (thrombocytopenia) and kidney damage. Severe HUS can require blood transfusions, dialysis for kidney support, and intensive care hospitalization. Long-term kidney complications can persist after acute illness resolves.

The Department of Health recommends handwashing immediately after [any animal contact](https://www.karmactive.com/mcdonalds-e-coli-outbreak-across-10-states-leaves-50-ill-1-dead-cdcs-investigation-ramps-up/), using soap and running water. Avoid consuming food or drink while near animals or in animal enclosures. Do not allow young children to touch their mouths or eat without washing first after animal contact.

Anyone who visited the petting farm and subsequently experienced severe abdominal pain, diarrhoea, or bloody stools should seek medical evaluation and inform their doctor of the animal exposure.

STORY 10: TEENAGER'S FIELD WORK CONTRIBUTES TO NEW SNAKE SPECIES IDENTIFICATION
================================================================================

A 17-year-old student contributed to the formal scientific description of a previously unknown [snake species](https://www.karmactive.com/unveiling-the-hidden-genetics-discovery-of-a-new-venomous-snake-species-in-australia/), demonstrating that rigorous field collection and formal scientific analysis define modern species recognition.

Hu Jiahao spent more than 40 nights searching for an elusive snake on Kuocang Mountain in Zhejiang Province. His field work yielded a specimen that, after detailed laboratory analysis, was formally described as a new species: [Achalinus mirabilis](https://zse.pensoft.net/). The species description was published in the peer-reviewed journal Zoosystematics and Evolution in 2026.

Naming a new species requires far more than finding an unusual animal. Scientists must compare physical characteristics (morphology) to all known related species. They must analyze genetic material to determine evolutionary relationships. They must examine historical museum specimens—called type materials—to verify they haven't discovered something already documented. Only after this rigorous process do they submit findings for peer review and formal publication.

For Achalinus mirabilis, researchers used an integrative approach combining three lines of evidence. First, they conducted molecular phylogenetic analysis—DNA sequencing and comparison to related species. Second, they performed morphological comparison, examining physical features like scale patterns, body shape, and size. Third, they examined type material from related species housed in museum collections to ensure no misidentification.

The genetic work revealed that Achalinus mirabilis differed from related species by uncorrected p-distances of 9.5 to 10.9 percent on a specific genetic marker called CO1. That level of genetic divergence, combined with morphological distinctiveness, constituted evidence of a separate species.

The 2026 paper also included reassessment of a related species, [Achalinus jinggangensis](https://www.karmactive.com/worlds-smallest-snake-resurfaces-in-barbados-4-1-inch-reptile-found-after-20-year-absence/), further characterizing the relationships among these rarely-studied snakes.

Hu's contribution as a field researcher earned recognition from the Zhejiang Forest Resources Monitoring Center, which cited him as an important contributor to the research.

The discovery demonstrates how species identification works in practice. Field effort provides the specimen, but formal recognition requires laboratory analysis, comparison to existing species, genetic examination, peer review, and publication in an established scientific journal.
