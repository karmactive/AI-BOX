The RBA board voted unanimously to lift the cash rate target from 4.35% to 4.60%, effective 30 September 2026. The decision was made at the September board meeting and announced the same day. The board said inflation remains elevated and that some of the upside risks to inflation are materialising since the August meeting. Global energy prices are now much higher than had been assumed in the August forecasts, partly due to further disruptions to global oil supply. AI-related demand is also driving rapid growth in global prices for technology-related goods.

The RBA's September statement contains a tension that's worth understanding. Housing prices have fallen in most capital cities. New housing loans have declined noticeably. Consumer spending is easing. In most circumstances, those signals argue for caution on rate increases.

The board raised regardless because the inflation picture moved in the wrong direction. Global oil-supply disruptions pushed energy prices above the RBA's August assumptions. The board also said recent data suggest that growth and inflation in Australia have been higher than expected.

The board explicitly named AI-related demand for the first time in a rate decision — noting it is driving rapid growth in global prices for technology-related goods. Whether this becomes a more dominant pressure on domestic inflation will depend on how those global price shifts flow through.

The combination meant the board judged that another increase was warranted to ensure inflation returns to the 2–3% target band and does not become embedded in the economy at elevated levels.

The next scheduled board meeting runs 2–3 November 2026, with the monetary policy decision announced on 3 November. A quarterly Statement on Monetary Policy is also released at that meeting.

Borrowers on fixed rates aren't directly affected by Tuesday's announcement, but fixed terms set in 2022 and 2023 will continue rolling off through 2026 and 2027. ## Kelvin Wave Heading for California Could Raise Coastal Sea Levels This Fall as El Niño Strengthens

If you live on or near the California coast, a large-scale ocean phenomenon is heading your way this fall that most people don't know how to think about. A Pacific Kelvin wave is moving toward the US West Coast as a strong El Niño develops, and California's governor has already declared a state of emergency to prepare for what officials described as a potentially historic season. The wave can temporarily elevate sea levels by several inches to locally around a foot in some locations — and that additional height changes flood risk calculations for coastal communities.

A Kelvin wave is a slow-moving pulse of warm water that travels eastward across the Pacific Ocean during El Niño events. It spans thousands of kilometres and moves primarily below the ocean's surface. When it reaches the West Coast, it becomes a coastally trapped Kelvin wave — a feature NOAA specifically monitors because it can temporarily raise sea levels along the coastline. NOAA's Fisheries division has confirmed it is actively tracking El Niño indicators for the current season, including these wave features on the US West Coast.

A temporary sea-level increase can worsen flooding when it coincides with high tides, waves or storms. It shifts the threshold at which ordinary events — a winter storm, a king tide, a large swell — become flooding events. Streets that drain normally under a standard high tide no longer do. Low-lying areas that rarely take water can start to. For California's coastline, a Kelvin wave arriving during peak winter storm season adds a layer of risk onto an already elevated baseline.

Governor Gavin Newsom declared a state of emergency on 21 September 2026 to prepare for what the state described as a potentially historic El Niño season. The declaration enables California to pre-position emergency resources and take preparedness steps before conditions deteriorate.

### What El Niño Does to California's Coast

El Niño is a periodic warming of the central and eastern Pacific Ocean that reconfigures global weather patterns. For California, El Niño conditions typically bring wetter winters in the south, stronger storms along the coast, and elevated flood risk for rivers, creeks and coastal-zone properties.

California officials are preparing for a potentially very strong 2026–27 El Niño event. The California Coastal Commission notes that strengthening El Niño conditions can add periodic sea-surface-height increases and increase the vulnerability of low-lying coastal areas during the period of impact. The combination of Kelvin wave sea-level rise on top of winter storms on top of long-term background sea-level rise creates layered risk that is harder to manage than any single factor alone.

This layering is what often gets lost in reporting on individual phenomena. The Kelvin wave is temporary — lasting weeks to months depending on the event. But "temporary" only helps if there are no major storms during those weeks. If a strong atmospheric river makes landfall while the Kelvin wave is still elevating sea levels, the combined risk of coastal flooding is higher than during a standard winter storm.

### The Background Sea Level Already Rising

The California Coastal Commission notes that the state's shoreline faces ongoing background sea-level rise separate from El Niño. The Kelvin wave effect is an acute event on top of a chronic problem. Communities that have adapted planning and infrastructure to historical flood levels are now facing baseline conditions that those plans didn't account for.

Low-lying coastal areas identified by the Coastal Commission as facing elevated risk during El Niño conditions include parts of San Francisco Bay, the Los Angeles Basin coast and the Central Coast.

### What Coastal Residents Should Do Now

California's Office of Emergency Services recommends coastal residents in flood-risk zones review their insurance coverage before winter. Standard homeowners insurance typically does not cover flood damage — that requires separate flood insurance, often through the National Flood Insurance Program. New NFIP policies generally take around 30 days to process, subject to policy rules and exceptions, so waiting until a storm is forecast is too late.

Residents should also understand their evacuation routes and confirm that local emergency alerts are enabled on their phones. The governor's emergency declaration makes additional preparedness resources available through county offices of emergency services. NOAA's National Ocean Service publishes daily tide predictions that can help track conditions at specific coastal gauge stations.

For broader context on [how El Niño has historically affected California's coast and infrastructure](/california-el-nino-history) and [what sea-level rise projections mean for West Coast properties over the next 20 years](/sea-level-rise-california-projections), both topics have additional background on Karmactive.

## Card Surcharges Banned in Australia From 1 October 2026: What Businesses Can Still Charge You

The credit card surcharge on your morning coffee, your online order and your phone bill payment disappears from Thursday. From 1 October 2026, Visa, Mastercard, American Express and eftpos are introducing no-surcharge rules under their card network schemes — meaning businesses that accept these cards can no longer add a percentage-based checkout fee for doing so. But the change has boundaries worth understanding before you assume every extra payment cost is gone.

The change takes effect 1 October 2026 through each card network's scheme rules and merchant contracts. The Reserve Bank of Australia enabled this by varying its own payment standard, allowing networks to impose no-surcharge rules on merchants. From that date, merchants who accept Visa, Mastercard, American Express or eftpos are prohibited under their merchant agreements from charging customers a surcharge for using those cards. The card networks and payment service providers are responsible for enforcing these rules through merchant contracts. The ACCC's role is separate: it administers Australian Consumer Law prohibitions on misleading pricing practices, which means renaming a surcharge to avoid the ban could attract serious penalties.

The change removes card-payment surcharges as a line-item cost. It does not remove payment processing costs from the economy — businesses still pay terminal fees and bank processing charges. The reform changes how those costs can be recovered; what they're allowed to add to a specific card transaction has changed.

### What Businesses Can Still Legally Charge After 1 October

**Weekend and public holiday surcharges in hospitality remain.** These cover higher staff wage costs on penalty-rate days and have nothing to do with card payment processing. They are a separate legal category and are unaffected by Thursday's change.

**Cash discounts are permitted.** The ACCC's pricing guidance allows businesses to charge a lower price for cash payment, provided the standard advertised price is the card price and the pricing is not misleading.

**Refusing to accept cards entirely is permitted for most businesses.** Businesses that choose not to accept card payments at all and require cash or direct bank transfer are not bound by the no-surcharge rules — which only apply if cards are accepted. Note that separate ACCC cash acceptance rules now apply to supermarkets and petrol stations, which are covered by the ACCC's cash acceptance industry codes.

**Platform handling fees and transaction minimums are an area for case-by-case assessment.** Some school canteen platforms, childcare payment systems and council payment portals have introduced flat handling fees or minimum transaction thresholds. Whether specific fee structures breach the spirit of the reform depends on the circumstances and is subject to ACCC monitoring of misleading pricing conduct.

For Australian small business owners managing the transition, [how the RBA's interchange fee caps work and what they mean for merchant processing costs](/rba-interchange-fee-caps-explained) covers the cost side in more detail. And for consumers wondering [how the surcharge ban affects reward credit card programs](/reward-credit-cards-surcharge-ban), that story also has changes worth checking.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Can a shop still refuse to accept card payments after 1 October?**
For most businesses, yes. The no-surcharge rules prevent businesses from adding surcharges if they accept cards — but they do not require businesses to accept cards at all. Note that separate ACCC cash acceptance rules apply to supermarkets and petrol stations. A business outside those categories that moves to cash or bank transfer only is operating within the existing rules.

**Do weekend restaurant surcharges disappear on 1 October?**
No. Weekend and public holiday surcharges in hospitality are separate from the card surcharge change. They cover penalty-rate wage costs for staff, not payment processing fees, and remain legal after 1 October.

**Will the card surcharge change make everyday purchases cheaper?**
In most standard retail settings, yes — percentage-based card fees should stop appearing at checkout. However, some platforms for school and childcare payments are introducing flat handling fees or transaction minimums. Your total cost at checkout may not always drop as simply as the line-item change suggests.

From 1 October, checking your receipts for a few weeks will show whether your regular merchants have removed card fees cleanly or introduced alternative charges. Consumers who see businesses continuing to add surcharges after Thursday can report potential misleading pricing conduct to the ACCC at accc.gov.au.

### What NSW Law Requires on Shared Paths

NSW Road Rules and Transport for NSW guidelines define what a legal e-bike looks like on public paths. A legally compliant e-bike in NSW currently must have a continuous rated power output of up to 500 watts, with the motor cutting out when the bike reaches 25 km/h. NSW is transitioning to a stricter 250-watt maximum from 1 March 2029, when all e-bikes must also comply with the European EN 15194 standard. E-bikes with between 250W and 500W purchased before 1 March 2026 can be ridden legally under the transitional provisions until that date.

Fat bikes sold as off-road recreational equipment can have power outputs above these limits. When ridden on shared pedestrian infrastructure, a device that does not meet the e-bike requirements under NSW road rules is not classified as a legal bicycle for path use, regardless of how it was marketed at point of sale.

On shared paths, riders must keep left, give way to pedestrians, and ride at a speed that is safe for conditions. Adults over 16 cannot ride on footpaths (distinct from shared paths) unless accompanying a child. Having a passenger on a shared path is a separate offence under NSW road rules.

## Europe's Gas Storage Is Running Below Historical Levels — And the Commercial Model Behind It Has Changed

European households are heading into winter with gas storage facilities tracking toward a relaxed 80% target but sitting significantly below the five-year average. As of late September 2026, EU underground storage was around 71% full — roughly 16 percentage points below the five-year average for this time of year, and the lowest level recorded for this date since tracking began in 2011. The European Commission has confirmed there is no immediate security-of-supply risk, but the picture is more complex than a single headline figure suggests.

EU underground gas storage facilities were at around 70–71% capacity as of 26 September 2026, according to Gas Infrastructure Europe data. The European Commission's Gas Coordination Group stated on 3 September 2026 that there is no immediate security-of-supply risk heading into winter, citing increased LNG import capacity, diversified supply sources and reduced gas demand. The EU originally set a 90% storage target, but member states and the Commission have agreed that reaching 80% would be sufficient from a security-of-supply perspective for this winter, with the window running from 1 October to 1 December. Storage is refilling at a rate that tracks closely to meeting that relaxed target, but with limited margin for error.

For households budgeting for winter heating, the practical risk is not a supply cutoff. It is a price spike. Below-average storage levels heading into winter mean there is less of a buffer against a cold snap — and the way Europe sources its gas has also structurally changed, creating a different risk profile from previous years.

### Why the Commercial Model Broke Down

Before 2022, European gas storage economics followed a predictable pattern. Gas was cheaper in summer because heating demand was low. Traders bought at summer prices, paid to inject the gas into underground facilities — salt caverns, porous rock reservoirs, depleted oil fields — and sold in winter when heating demand pushed prices up. The summer-to-winter price spread covered storage operating costs and generated a margin.

Russia's invasion of Ukraine and the subsequent cutoff of most Russian pipeline gas changed that model. LNG, which has largely replaced those pipeline volumes, is priced on global markets and responds to weather events and industrial demand spikes in Asia and elsewhere — not to European seasonal heating cycles alone. As Bloomberg and the Financial Times reported in detail this week, the summer-to-winter price spread in European gas markets has flattened or collapsed for many private storage operators, reducing the commercial incentive to inject gas. This structural shift is supported by European Commission and Gas Infrastructure Europe data on storage trajectories.

### The LNG Buffer and Its Limits

LNG has become a major supply source for Europe following the loss of Russian pipeline gas. European LNG import terminal capacity has expanded significantly since 2022, with new regasification infrastructure added in Germany, the Netherlands and Italy.

The key difference from pipeline gas is flexibility. LNG tankers are a global commodity — they go wherever prices are highest. During a severe cold snap, if Asian buyers are bidding aggressively for LNG cargoes, European importers must compete in real time. This does not guarantee a shortage, but it does mean that during extreme events, spot-market prices can move sharply and quickly.

Below-average storage levels heading into winter reduce the buffer available to absorb that kind of demand spike. Under average winter weather, the European Commission assesses supply as adequate. Under a prolonged, severe freeze, the margin is tighter than in years when storage was above the five-year average.

### What European Households and Businesses Should Do

For household consumers, the most direct hedge against winter bill spikes is a fixed-rate energy contract. Variable tariffs track spot prices more closely; fixed tariffs lock in a set rate. Whether fixed or variable is the better choice depends on the tariff structure available in your country and energy market, so comparing current options with your supplier before the heating season begins is worth doing.

Commercial energy buyers across key European markets face a similar calculation. Current forward-curve prices reflect an expectation of adequate supply. If that expectation is tested by severe weather, forward prices will move.

For background on [how European energy markets restructured after the 2022 gas crisis](/europe-energy-market-restructuring) and [Australia's growing role as an LNG exporter to European markets](/australia-lng-europe-exports), both are covered in depth on Karmactive.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Does Europe have enough natural gas for the 2026–2027 winter?**
Under average winter weather, yes. The European Commission's Gas Coordination Group confirmed no immediate supply-security risk as of September 2026. However, storage levels are around 16 percentage points below the five-year average and tracking toward a relaxed 80% target rather than the original 90%. A prolonged cold spell combined with strong competition for global LNG cargoes could draw down reserves faster than planned, pushing spot-market prices higher.

**Why is European gas storage below historical levels this year?**
The transition away from Russian pipeline gas to LNG imports has changed the economics of filling storage. The summer-to-winter price spread that made seasonal storage commercially attractive for private traders has narrowed, reducing commercial injection activity. Storage refilling has been slower than historical patterns as a result.

**Will European heating bills rise this winter?**
That depends on winter temperatures and global LNG demand. Fixed-rate energy contracts provide protection against spot-market volatility. Variable-rate customers are exposed to price movements if demand spikes during cold weather. Reviewing your tariff options before the heating season is the most direct step available.

Gas Infrastructure Europe publishes weekly storage drawdown data. The first meaningful stress test on current reserves will come from any sustained cold weather in January or February 2027. Check back here for updates on European gas supply as the heating season develops.

## Daylight Saving Starts 4 October 2026 in Australia: Which States Change and What Night Shift Workers Should Know

Australian daylight saving starts this Sunday, 4 October 2026. If you're in one of the five participating states or territories, you lose an hour of sleep this weekend. If you're working a night shift when the clocks change, your payslip may look different from usual — and checking your employment agreement before the weekend is worth doing.

At 2:00 am on Sunday 4 October 2026, clocks in New South Wales, Victoria, South Australia, Tasmania and the Australian Capital Territory move forward to 3:00 am. Queensland, Western Australia and the Northern Territory do not observe daylight saving and do not change their clocks. From Sunday morning, the time difference between Sydney and Brisbane shifts from zero to one hour for the first time in six months. Daylight saving ends on Sunday 4 April 2027, when clocks move back one hour.

The practical effect for most people is losing one hour of sleep on Saturday night and gaining an extra hour of evening daylight through summer. Analogue clocks, appliances with built-in clocks and some older software systems don't update automatically — those need manual adjustment.

### What Happens If You're Working an Overnight Shift

An eight-hour night shift starting at 10:00 pm Saturday 4 October will end at 5:00 am clock time rather than 6:00 am — because the clock jumped forward at 2:00 am, effectively removing an hour. That shift covers seven actual hours of work. Whether you're paid for seven or eight depends on your employment contract.

Under Fair Work guidelines, unless your employment award or enterprise agreement explicitly specifies that payment is based on actual hours worked, employees are paid "by the clock." That means your roster shows eight hours, you get paid for eight, even though only seven passed. Workers whose agreements specifically pay for hours worked rather than rostered hours receive pay for seven. If you're on an award or enterprise agreement and aren't sure which applies, check with your employer's payroll team before the weekend.

Cross-border businesses, health services and commuters manage split timetables for the next six months.
