## STORY 1 — Anthropic Files S-1 for Nasdaq IPO Under Ticker ANT

---

### STEP 1 — DRAFT ARTICLE

**Word count target:** 700 words
**Justification:** Complex financial filing with multiple technical concepts — convertible notes, multi-class share structures, SEC mechanics — that require plain-language explanation for a general audience; the story also carries an international investor angle (US/UK/CA/AU) that adds necessary context and breadth.

**SEO Headline:** Anthropic Files S-1 for Nasdaq IPO Under Ticker ANT: $16.3 Billion Revenue, $80.7 Billion Loss — Explained
**Meta (156 chars):** Anthropic filed its SEC Form S-1 to list on Nasdaq under ticker ANT. See H1 2026 revenue ($16.3B), why the net loss hit $80.7B, and what Class A investors actually get.

---

Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI assistant, officially filed paperwork with U.S. regulators on October 26 to go public on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker symbol "ANT." The reaction came fast, and most of it fixated on the same startling number: an $80.7 billion net loss.

That number is almost entirely misleading, and here is why it matters to understand that before anyone touches this stock.

The company's real operating loss for the first half of 2026 was $690 million — actual cash spent on staff, computing power, and infrastructure — set against $16.3 billion in revenue over those same six months. That works out to roughly four cents per dollar of operating loss against every dollar Anthropic earned. The $80.7 billion headline figure includes a $72.4 billion non-cash accounting adjustment. Early investors in Anthropic received convertible notes before the company went public. As Anthropic's estimated worth climbed toward $1.5 trillion to $2 trillion, accounting standards required the company to record the rising value of those notes as a loss on its financial statements. No cash went anywhere.

Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are managing the offering. Investors in the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, and Australia are named in the filing as eligible markets. You cannot buy shares yet. The SEC must formally approve the registration before trading begins.

The share structure inside the filing is the part most coverage skipped entirely. Anthropic created multiple classes of stock that separate economic ownership from actual control of the company.

Public buyers on the Nasdaq will receive Class A shares. These give financial exposure — you participate in revenue growth — but they come with very limited governance power. The filing also creates Class E shares, issued exclusively to employees, not co-founders. Class E carries no economic rights at all and votes only on a narrow defined set of matters. Class F shares also hold zero economic rights. Class T directors sit at the governance level that actually drives the company's direction. In plain terms, retail investors buying ANT on listing day will own a stake in a fast-growing AI business, but the people running Anthropic and setting its mission are structurally insulated from public shareholder pressure. Multi-class share structures are common in tech IPOs, but Anthropic's version is more layered than most.

Infrastructure spending is another major theme inside the filing. Training frontier AI models at the scale Anthropic operates requires data centers consuming enormous quantities of electricity. The S-1 lists power grid capacity as a direct business risk. Capital expenditure on compute and energy infrastructure is projected to remain heavy for years, which is one reason operating losses are likely to continue even as revenue climbs steeply.

Revenue growth has been extraordinary by any measure. The $16.3 billion for the first half of 2026 came primarily from enterprise API access to Claude and from cloud partnerships with Amazon Web Services and Google Cloud. Businesses are embedding Claude-powered features into their own products at an accelerating pace.

The filing also extends to investors in the European Economic Area, Japan, and Switzerland through separate country-specific provisions, without requiring Anthropic to list on local exchanges in those markets.

The next document to watch is an amended filing called the S-1/A, which will disclose the actual price range per share and the total number of shares being offered. That is expected sometime in November 2026. A 25-day prospectus delivery period runs after the S-1/A before shares can officially begin trading.

For anyone who will never buy a single ANT share, this IPO still matters. The price Anthropic's stock opens at will become the first publicly traded benchmark for the value of a frontier AI lab. Every private AI company — OpenAI, xAI, and others — will be measured against that number for years.

That date started a legal clock that controls everything else on the payout timeline.

Under federal court procedure, any objector has 30 days from the final approval order to file a notice of appeal. That 30-day window closes on Monday, November 2, 2026. If that deadline passes without an appeal, the settlement enters what the agreement defines as its "Effective Date" — which arrives 10 days later, on November 12, 2026. From that point, the settlement administrator has 60 days to issue payments. Run that math and the first wave of checks or digital transfers arrives between mid-December 2026 and mid-January 2027.

The catch: if even a single objector files a notice of appeal before November 2, the entire timeline freezes. No payments can leave the settlement fund until the appeal is dismissed or resolved. In federal court, that process can take months.

The $5,000 figure circulating in most headlines is also worth correcting before anyone gets the wrong expectation. That cap applies exclusively to claimants who submitted documented proof of out-of-pocket financial harm — verified fraud losses, identity theft costs, or similar expenses — before the December 2025 claims deadline. Standard claimants who filed without documentation receive a much smaller pro-rata share of the net settlement fund, not $5,000.

The claims filing window closed in December 2025. There is no option to file a new claim at this stage.

For those who did file on time: update your payment method with the settlement administrator now, before November 12. If your bank account details or mailing address have changed since you submitted your claim, failing to update them before the Effective Date risks missing the first distribution wave entirely.

The two 2024 breaches together exposed tens of millions of AT&T customers' personal data. The settlement covers anyone whose information was included in either incident.

November 2 is the real checkpoint. If that day passes clean — no appeal filed — then nothing stands between claimants and their payment except the administrator's 60-day processing window. Watch for any filing activity in the Northern District of Texas on Monday. If there is silence, the December payout schedule holds.

Microsoft is offering voluntary buyouts to roughly 60,000 employees — more than a quarter of its entire global workforce — in a program that extends further up the management ladder than most people expected.

The details came in an internal memo sent on October 26 by Amy Coleman, Microsoft's Executive Vice President and Chief People Officer. The Voluntary Exit Offer applies to selected teams in the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, and India.

The headline element is how high the offer reaches. Eligibility goes up to Level 70 on Microsoft's internal grading scale, which includes general managers and some vice presidents below the Corporate VP level. There is no minimum age requirement, no minimum tenure, and no performance rating condition. If you are in an eligible team at the right level, you qualify.

Employees who receive the offer have until the first week of December 2026 to accept or decline. Those who opt in stay on payroll through the end of the year, with exits effective December 31, 2026. The package includes a severance formula tied to base pay, job level, and tenure, alongside continued stock vesting and subsidized healthcare through departure.

Coleman's memo stated: "This is a time-limited, voluntary program that gives those who are eligible the opportunity to leave Microsoft with financial support and career planning resources." She also noted that not all teams within Microsoft are participating — which organizations are offering the program depends on each business unit's priorities, customer commitments, and workforce needs.

The December 31, 2026 exit date is not accidental. That date coincides with the close of Microsoft's second fiscal quarter for FY2027. Exiting 60,000 eligible positions by the end of Q2 FY27 — while retaining those employees through the critical fourth calendar quarter enterprise sales cycle — delivers a clean structural change to headcount without triggering mass layoff notification requirements that a forced reduction of the same scale would demand.

The 60,000 figure represents those who are eligible, not the guaranteed number of departures. How many people actually accept the offer will be known after the early December decision deadline.

For context: Microsoft employed roughly 223,000 people heading into this program. Earlier in 2026, the company ran a separate retirement-focused buyout aimed at a much narrower group with specific age and tenure criteria. This offer removes those filters entirely and reaches deeper into senior management.

If the voluntary take-up rate falls short of internal workforce reduction targets, watchers of Microsoft's quarterly results will be looking closely at what — if anything — the company announces in Q1 2027.

## STORY 5 — DR Congo Ebola Outbreak Passes 10,000 Cases: Why the Bundibugyo Strain Changes Everything

**SEO Headline:** DR Congo Ebola Outbreak Passes 10,000 Cases: CDC Risk Update and Why the Bundibugyo Strain Has No Approved Vaccine
**Meta (156 chars):** DR Congo's Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak has passed 10,000 cases and 4,900 deaths. See the CDC's US risk update, why vaccines fail, and new border quarantines.

---

The death toll in the Democratic Republic of Congo's ongoing Ebola outbreak passed a grim threshold on October 27 — confirmed cases exceeded 10,000, with nearly 4,900 people dead. It is the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak ever recorded, and the core reason it spread this quickly is that none of the world's existing approved Ebola vaccines can stop it.

This outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo virus, a distinct species within the broader Ebola family. The only vaccines in approved stockpiles — including the widely deployed Ervebo shot used effectively in previous DRC outbreaks — were designed specifically for the Zaire ebolavirus species. Against Bundibugyo, they provide no protection. That single fact removed the primary containment tool responders have relied on for the past decade.

The numbers reflect the difference clearly. In 2018, DR Congo's Zaire-strain outbreak took 235 days from response activation to reach 1,000 confirmed cases. The 2026 Bundibugyo outbreak hit the same mark in 40 days. The problem with antimatter is fundamental: the instant it touches ordinary matter, both the positron and the matter particle it hits destroy each other in a burst of energy. The Standard Model of particle physics — the current best framework for describing how matter and energy work at the subatomic level — predicts that an electron and a positron should respond identically to the same magnetic field, just with opposite signs.

If the measurement reveals even a tiny deviation from that prediction, it would be evidence that the Standard Model is incomplete. That kind of deviation would point toward undiscovered particles, undiscovered forces, or both. Physicists have been searching for such deviations for decades because the Standard Model, despite its extraordinary predictive success, is known to be incomplete — it cannot account for dark matter, for instance, or explain the imbalance between matter and antimatter in the observable universe.

The trapping capability the Northwestern team has now demonstrated allows a precision measurement that was not achievable before. The longer a particle can be held, the more accurately its properties can be measured. Months of trapping time is what shifts this from an experiment into a precision instrument.
