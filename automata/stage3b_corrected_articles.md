## STORY 1 — Pope Leo XIV Lung Cancer Diagnosis [FOLLOW-UP | Rank 1]

### Pope Leo XIV Has Lung Cancer: Vatican Confirms Adenocarcinoma After Right Lower Lobectomy

Pope Leo XIV has been diagnosed with lung adenocarcinoma, the Vatican announced Tuesday, after surgeons at Rome's Agostino Gemelli Polyclinic removed the lower lobe of his right lung using a video-assisted technique. The diagnosis was confirmed through an intraoperative histological examination performed during the surgery itself, and answered the question left open when Sunday's Angelus announcement described only a "well-localised nodule."

The Vatican's October 13 medical bulletin confirmed the diagnosis: lung adenocarcinoma, the most common type of lung cancer, typically forming in the lung's outer regions rather than its central airways. The procedure performed on the 71-year-old Pope was a right lower lobectomy — removal of one of the right lung's three lobes — carried out using a video-assisted surgical technique. He is recovering at Gemelli Hospital, where the Vatican said the surgery was successful with no complications. His Holiness had also been scheduled to personally award the inaugural Villanova Pope Leo XIV Sustainability Medal at the Vatican; the ceremony proceeded as planned without him, with Villanova University president presenting the award to Cardinal Michael Czerny. The Vatican cancelled all of Leo XIV's audiences through October 20.

A right lower lobectomy is the standard surgical approach when adenocarcinoma is confined to a single lobe. Removing the whole lobe rather than only the affected nodule ensures that any cancer cells at the margins of the mass are included in what is excised. The Vatican said the hospital stay is expected to last about a week. The Vatican has not confirmed whether Leo XIV will require additional treatment — chemotherapy, targeted radiation, or biological therapy — a determination that depends on complete post-surgical staging results.

## What the Diagnosis Means in Practice

Lung adenocarcinoma originates in the small glandular cells that line the lung's airways, usually near the outer edges rather than centrally. It is the most common lung cancer subtype in non-smokers, though it occurs regardless of smoking history. Because it tends to grow without producing early symptoms, it is frequently discovered through imaging during unrelated examinations — consistent with the Vatican's account that monitoring, not new symptoms, led to the finding.

The right lung has three lobes. Removing the lower lobe is a well-established procedure for localised adenocarcinoma; the remaining lobes gradually compensate over time. The central clinical question at this stage is staging: if the cancer was fully contained within the excised lobe, the lobectomy may constitute definitive treatment. Any spread visible at the margins generally leads to additional therapy.

When the Vatican [described the mass as well-localised before Sunday's surgery announcement](https://www.karmactive.com/pope-leo-lung-surgery-well-localized-nodule/), that language indicated the tumour had not visibly spread beyond the nodule site. Tuesday's surgical bulletin confirmed the radical resection was complete, though formal staging requires full pathological analysis of the excised specimen.

The Vatican has not set a timetable for Leo XIV's oncological plan or return to public duties. All audiences have been cancelled through October 20. The full treatment schedule — whether further therapy is recommended and what it means for his remaining calendar — is expected once the medical team completes its post-surgical assessment. ## STORY 2 — SCOTUS Sotomayor & Gorsuch on Immigrant Prosecution [NEW | Rank 2]

### Sotomayor and Gorsuch Warn of "Constitutional Absurdity" in Repeat Immigrant Prosecution

Two Supreme Court justices from opposite ends of the bench issued a rare cross-ideological written statement Tuesday condemning the repeated criminal prosecution of a deported immigrant, calling the outcome a "constitutional absurdity" — even as the full Court declined to hear the case.

The Supreme Court denied certiorari October 13 in *Herrera-Salazar v. United States*, No. 25-6989, a case out of the 10th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals. Jesus Herrera-Salazar was first indicted in 2020 for being "found in" the United States after deportation, in violation of 8 U.S.C. § 1326(a). After serving his sentence, he was placed on supervised release and expressly ordered to remain in the Eastern District of Oklahoma. Authorities stopped him in a traffic stop in Heavener, Oklahoma, and he was indicted and convicted again under the same statute. Justice Sonia Sotomayor wrote the statement, which was joined by Justice Neil Gorsuch. In it, Sotomayor wrote that the government is "barred from charging that defendant with the same continuous crime for a second time simply by focusing on a different time marker," and urged courts to avoid "the constitutional absurdity that occurred here."

For anyone facing federal reentry charges after a prior prosecution, the statement does not change current law — the Court rejected the case, so nothing in Sotomayor's writing is binding precedent. What it creates is a clear signal: at least two justices believe the constitutional question is live and waiting for the right vehicle. Defense attorneys in future cases can now point directly to on-record statements from a liberal and a conservative justice both characterising the government's prosecution theory as constitutionally unsound.

## Why the Statement Carries Weight Despite the Denial

When the Supreme Court declines to hear a case, it issues no ruling on the merits. Justices sometimes attach written statements to those denials — formally called "opinions relating to orders" — to flag unresolved questions or signal when they believe a future case should be taken.

The 10th Circuit ruled against Herrera-Salazar, holding that both convictions were for different offenses under the "allowable unit of prosecution" doctrine, meaning the second prosecution was not barred. Sotomayor's statement argued this produced a constitutionally troubling outcome: the government had already located Herrera-Salazar, placed him under supervision, and then prosecuted him again as if he had been newly "found in" the United States. Her concurrence in the denial rested on the hope that these facts were "hopefully anomalous" and that he has since been removed from the United States.

The statement is the kind of roadmap defence attorneys track closely. It identifies the specific constitutional argument — that a person already found by the government and compelled to remain under supervision cannot be prosecuted again under a statute that criminalises being "found in" the country — and signals it warrants serious weight in future cases. The Court will not take up *Herrera-Salazar* further. For the question to return to the Supreme Court, a future petitioner will need to present the same constitutional argument in a case without the anomalous facts Sotomayor described as grounds for the denial. No pending case has been identified as the next likely vehicle.

Bisignano made the 90-year solvency claim in a live interview, without citing a specific actuarial basis. The Social Security Administration's 2026 trustees report, released in June, projects the OASI trust fund will be depleted in the fourth quarter of 2032, at which point incoming revenues would cover only 78% of scheduled benefits without legislative action. The combined OASI and Disability Insurance trust funds face a separate, later depletion date: the combined fund is projected to run short in the third quarter of 2034, when continuing income would cover 83% of scheduled benefits. On the 30 million records: Bisignano first made this claim in July, describing the entries as incorrect or outdated; the agency used AI-assisted tools in the review. A separate step is required for families of children born between 2025 and 2028 to claim the government's $1,000 seed deposit into a Trump Account; automatic enrollment creates the account, but does not transfer the deposit without that additional action.

The gap between Bisignano's 90-year claim and the trustees' 2032 projection is the most consequential point for anyone making retirement or savings decisions now. If the OASI fund is depleted in 2032 without a fix from Congress, benefit payments for all current and future recipients would be cut automatically to roughly 78 cents on the dollar. That is a legal outcome under current statute, not a worst-case scenario. Trump Accounts are a savings program for children. By early October, approximately 70 million accounts had been created, with more than 60 million through automatic enrollment of eligible children. The critical procedural point: automatic enrollment creates the account, but parents and guardians must separately claim it to manage it and to activate the government's $1,000 seed deposit for children born between 2025 and 2028. ## STORY 7 — US Housing Paradox: Sellers Outnumber Buyers by 139% vs. 7.4% Mortgage Rates [NEW | Rank 7]

### Nashville Has 139% More Home Sellers Than Buyers — and Buyers Who Can Still Qualify Have Real Leverage

Nashville now has 139% more home sellers than buyers, according to Redfin's most recent analysis of August 2026 data, making it the strongest buyer's market on record in Redfin's tracking going back to 2013. The homes are listed. The buyers are largely absent. Mortgage rates averaging 7.40% for the week of October 8, according to Freddie Mac's weekly survey, have made the monthly payment on a median-priced home unworkable for the majority of households who don't already have a low-rate mortgage locked in.

The result is a market that looks like a buyer's market on paper — more supply, less competition, sellers sitting on stale listings — but functions like a freeze because the cost of financing has priced out the demand that would normally absorb that supply. Redfin's seller-surplus metric measures the imbalance between home sellers and active buyers in a given market; Nashville's 139% figure means sellers outnumber buyers by that margin. Miami held the top ranking in earlier months of 2026. In Nashville, the $400,000-plus segment has seen the sharpest pullback in buyer activity, as buyers who could stretch to that price point at lower rates can no longer qualify at 7.40%.

This gap is directly actionable for buyers who can still qualify. Sellers who have been sitting on overpriced listings for 60 or 90 days are in a structurally weaker position than they were 18 months ago, and there are specific financing tools that shift part of the rate burden from the buyer to the transaction itself — tools many buyers don't ask for because they don't know they exist.

## Two Tools Buyers Can Demand in This Market

**2-1 rate buydowns.** A 2-1 buydown is a seller-funded concession that temporarily reduces the mortgage interest rate — by 2 percentage points in year one and 1 percentage point in year two — before the rate returns to its contracted level in year three. On a $400,000 loan at a note rate of 7.40%, a seller-funded 2-1 buydown means the buyer pays at the equivalent of a 5.40% rate in year one and 6.40% in year two. On a $400,000 loan at 7.40%, the seller-funded cost of that buydown is approximately $9,000 to $10,000 at closing. In a market where sellers are already cutting prices, a buydown often costs the seller less than a price reduction while delivering more monthly relief to the buyer. [INTERNAL LINK: mortgage rates and home buying guide]

**Float-down locks.** A rate lock commits the lender to a specific rate for a set period — typically 30 to 60 days — while a purchase is in contract. A float-down provision added to that lock allows the buyer to capture a lower rate if rates fall before closing, without being exposed to rate increases if rates rise. In a market where the Federal Reserve's rate path remains uncertain, a float-down lock costs a small premium upfront but removes the timing risk that causes buyers to delay. Not all lenders offer float-down provisions; asking at the pre-approval stage, before entering contract, is the only way to access them.

For buyers considering either tool, the starting point is the seller's days on market. A listing that has been sitting without offers in a market where sellers outnumber buyers by 139% is one where a buydown or closing cost concession request is less likely to be rejected outright than it would have been two years ago.

Energy efficiency upgrades are a related factor for buyers using FHA or VA-backed financing: both programs offer energy efficiency mortgages that can add efficiency improvements — insulation, HVAC, windows — to the loan balance at close, which partially offsets higher operating costs when energy prices are elevated.
