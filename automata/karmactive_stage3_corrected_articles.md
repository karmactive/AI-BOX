## 3. Department of Labor PERM Action — REWRITTEN

**Headline:** Labor Department Halts Green Card Processing for Some Tech Firms

The US Department of Labor has suspended permanent labor certification (PERM) filings for some technology employers. PERM is the first step in many employer-sponsored green cards. In September 2026, the Department of Labor Inspector General said it had suspended PERM filings for Cognizant. It later said Cloudera's filings were also suspended, and that H-1B processing for both companies was suspended too. The investigation was coordinated with the White House Fraud Task Force. The Department has not disclosed the specific allegations.

Cloudera had an earlier suspension. In May 2026, the Office of Foreign Labor Certification suspended processing of its permanent labor certification applications for 180 days.

Adobe's pending PERM cases also appear on hold. A PERM Tracker snapshot dated 25 September 2026 lists all 215 of its pending cases as on hold. The Department has not published an announcement for Adobe.

If you work for one of these employers, check your case status with your attorney or employer. Approved I-140 petitions are a separate step, and whether a freeze affects them depends on the case. Talk to an immigration lawyer before making job or visa decisions.

## 5. Trump Accounts — EDITED

Trump Accounts are a federal program for children under 18. The White House says nearly 70 million children have accounts set up for them. [Edit: "about 70 million children" changed to "nearly 70 million children have accounts set up", to match the wording of the White House release.]

Each account can receive a $1,000 federal pilot contribution, but only for qualifying children. A child must be born between 1 January 2025 and 31 December 2028, be a US citizen, have a Social Security number, and have an election made by an authorized adult. Some older children can receive a $250 contribution from Michael and Susan Dell. Sources differ on the birth years for that payment, so check the Dell and Treasury materials before quoting them.

The $250 payment goes to the first 25 million activated accounts for children in ZIP codes with median household incomes below $150,000. Dell's pledge totals $6.25 billion.

Do not plan on the $1,000 as spending money. Money in the account is locked until the child turns 18. Withdrawals after that follow standard IRA rules, which set their own limits and exceptions. Check the investment options before deciding how much to add.

*Removed: the "250% of the federal poverty line" test, the "only three uses" claim, the 84% volatility figure, the $4,500–$12,000 projection, the education-loan collateral claim, and the "fact sheet" citation. None were confirmed.*

Another 54% skipped or delayed care or prescriptions because of out-of-pocket costs. The survey covered more than 1,000 beneficiaries, was fielded in September 2026, and was released on 8 October 2026. The margin of error is plus or minus 3%.
