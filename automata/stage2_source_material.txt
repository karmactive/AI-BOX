STORY 1: U.S. HOUSE VOTES ON FIXING SUPREME COURT AT NINE JUSTICES
===========================================================================
KEY VERIFIED FACTS:
- The measure is H.J. Res. 1, a proposed constitutional amendment requiring the Supreme Court to consist of nine justices.
- The House considered it under suspension of the rules on September 2, 2026.
- It FAILED 212–206. A two-thirds vote was required under suspension rules.
- Partisan breakdown: 211 Republicans voting yes, 0 Republicans voting no; 1 Democrat voting yes, 205 voting no. (Source: Republican Cloakroom)
- The current statutory composition is already nine: one Chief Justice plus eight associate justices. Federal law 28 U.S.C. §1 currently says exactly that.
- The nine-member arrangement dates to the Judiciary Act of 1869, but the Court's size changed several times before that.
- The failed House vote did NOT change the Court's present composition — it was a proposed amendment, not a change.
- If an amendment were successfully proposed, Article V requires ratification by three-fourths of the states — 38 of 50. (Source: National Archives)
- H.J. Res. 1 failed because a two-thirds majority was needed (291 votes); 212 yes votes fell far short.

STORY 10: 17-YEAR-OLD HU JIAHAO AND THE NEW SNAKE SPECIES (ACHALINUS MIRABILIS)
===========================================================================
KEY VERIFIED FACTS:
- Hu Jiahao is 17 years old. He spent more than 40 nights searching for an elusive snake after researchers initially had incomplete remains. (Source: ecns.cn)
- The 2026 taxonomic paper formally describes Achalinus mirabilis sp. nov. from Kuocang Mountain in Zhejiang. (Source: FAO AGRIS)
- The researchers used an integrative approach: molecular phylogenetic analysis, morphological comparison and examination of type material. (Source: FAO AGRIS)
- CO1 fragment genetic divergence: uncorrected p-distances of 9.5%–10.9%. (Source: FAO AGRIS)
- The paper also reassesses Achalinus jinggangensis.
- Published in Zoosystematics and Evolution in 2026. (Source: ResearchGate)
- Hu was considered an important contributor by the Zhejiang Forest Resources Monitoring Center. (Source: ecns.cn)
- The research was NOT simply "a teenager found a snake" — it involved field collection followed by formal taxonomic and molecular analysis.
HOW SCIENCE RECOGNISES A NEW SPECIES: (1) Finding an unusual specimen is only the beginning. (2) Researchers compare physical characteristics. (3) Genetic evidence is examined. (4) Existing species and type specimens are compared. (5) Evidence is formally published and the species is named.
