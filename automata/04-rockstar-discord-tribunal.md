# Rockstar Games Tribunal Hears Discord Emails Were an "Egregious" Policy Breach

---

## ARTICLE

If you follow GTA VI, you probably know Rockstar North dismissed a large group of employees in October 2024. What is now playing out in a UK employment tribunal is the dispute over whether those dismissals were unfair — and a Discord server is at the centre of both sides' case.

### The Rockstar Discord Tribunal: Key Facts

A UK employment tribunal is hearing claims from former Rockstar North employees who say they were unfairly dismissed in connection with trade-union activity. Thirty-four people were dismissed in October 2024 across Rockstar's UK and Canada operations. Twenty-three are participating in the Rockstar Games Discord tribunal.

Rockstar disputes the union-activity claim. The company says the dismissals followed gross misconduct — specifically, that employees shared confidential company emails through a Discord server in what Rockstar describes as an "egregious" breach of policy.

The tribunal is examining both sets of claims. It has not issued a judgment.

### What the Discord Server Means to Both Sides

The tribunal is relevant to GTA VI readers because the dispute involves former Rockstar North employees who worked on the game, but the proceedings have not established that Rockstar unlawfully dismissed them. Former workers claim the dismissals were linked to union activity, while Rockstar says they followed gross misconduct involving confidential information. The distinction should remain explicit.

The Discord server is not an incidental detail. It sits at the centre of Rockstar's case and the workers' case simultaneously, for different reasons.

Rockstar's argument is that employees used the server to share confidential company emails — the "egregious" breach it has presented to the tribunal. The former employees' account is that the forum was used to discuss workplace matters and union activity. Whether the server's primary function was protected workplace organisation or policy-breaching information sharing is central to what the tribunal must decide. Trade-union membership and activities receive statutory protection under UK law, but whether the conduct at issue qualifies for that protection in this case is for the tribunal to determine.

### What Each Side Claims

Former employees allege that Rockstar dismissed them because of their union activity, not because of their conduct. UK law provides statutory protection against dismissal for trade-union activities; if the tribunal finds that a prohibited trade-union reason was the reason for dismissal, the claim can fall within the statutory protections against automatically unfair dismissal.

Rockstar's position is that the dismissals were for gross misconduct related to confidential information shared through Discord. Rockstar argues that the alleged misconduct was the real reason for dismissal. That is a separate legal basis which, if established by evidence, can be a valid reason for dismissal.

The tribunal has heard evidence from both sides. Nothing in the proceedings so far constitutes a finding that either version is correct. The tribunal is still examining the competing claims.

### Why GTA VI Makes This a Bigger Story

GTA VI is scheduled for release on November 19, 2026. Some of the workers involved in the tribunal worked on the game during their time at Rockstar North in Glasgow. The game's release date means this dispute is occurring during the run-up to a major game release.

The timing creates an unusual backdrop: a major game launch and an active employment tribunal involving people who contributed to it, running simultaneously. The tribunal itself does not concern the game's development or quality — but GTA VI followers and people tracking the Rockstar North employment case represent overlapping audiences.

### The Broader Significance

The case matters beyond the immediate parties. How UK employment tribunals assess digital communications — Discord, Slack, internal messaging platforms — as evidence in dismissal cases may touch questions that affect employers and employees across the technology and games industries. The case involves questions about workplace communication, confidentiality and trade-union activity.
