The broader jalapeño-linked outbreak it connects to sickened 488 people across 34 states, with 65 hospitalizations and zero deaths reported.

The recall was initiated by Salata Dressings LLC of Houston, Texas, with the company announcing on August 11, 2026 and the FDA publishing the notice on August 14. The product was distributed through Texas HEB stores; check the FDA notice for the specific best-by dates and production lots that are affected. The outbreak was traced to diced jalapeños from Sinaloa, Mexico, distributed by Coast Citrus Distributors. The contamination was identified as Salmonella Javiana. The 488 cases and 65 hospitalizations are associated with the broader multistate jalapeño outbreak — the FDA notice for Salata's specific product states no illnesses were reported in connection with the recalled dressing itself.

Check the current FDA recall notice at FDA.gov for the exact lot numbers and best-by dates before discarding or returning the product. If your bottle matches the recalled identifiers, stop using it and follow the instructions in the recall notice for remedy and contact information. Swallowing Salmonella-contaminated food brings on symptoms — stomach cramps, diarrhea, fever — within 6 hours to 6 days of exposure. People with weakened immune systems, adults 65 and older, and children under five face higher risk of severe complications including bloodstream infection and reactive arthritis. If you develop severe symptoms that are not improving, contact a healthcare provider.

**What the Class I recall classification means — and what the outbreak tells us**

A Class I recall is the FDA's most serious classification, reserved for situations where the agency determines there is a reasonable probability that consuming the product will cause serious health harm or death. This does not mean harm is certain — it reflects the level of risk the FDA assigns.

The CDC linked the broader outbreak to a common grower in the jalapeño supply chain through epidemiological and traceback evidence, though the FDA stated it could not definitively determine the exact contamination route. Salata's recall was initiated because its product used diced jalapeños from the suspect supply chain; the recall does not confirm that every unit of the dressing was contaminated.

The CDC declared the broader jalapeño-linked outbreak over on October 2. That closure reflects that no new cases had been linked to this contamination in the surveillance window. The recall on the specific Salata product remains relevant for consumers who may have the product at home, because some of it would still be within shelf life.

The CDC's investigation named Chipotle Mexican Grill and QDOBA among restaurants where affected patients had eaten. Both chains removed the affected jalapeños; the CDC and FDA said there is no ongoing risk at those locations. The illness onset dates for the broader outbreak ran from June 16 to August 26, with implicated meals occurring between June 14 and July 29.

The distinction between "suspected plague" and confirmed plague matters significantly for public risk assessment. The institute studies natural plague foci in the surrounding region, where the bacterium is associated with wild rodent populations. This is the standard scope of an anti-plague research institute's work.

The World Health Organization's existing guidance on plague confirms that the disease can spread between people through respiratory droplets after close contact with a pneumonic case, that it is treatable with antibiotics, and that early treatment improves outcomes. ## PRIORITY 4 — Story 1: Walgreens Lubricant Eye Drops Recalled Over Sterility Concerns

**HEADLINE:** Walgreens Lubricant Eye Drops Recalled: How to Check If Your Bottle Is Affected

---

More than 500,000 Walgreens-branded lubricant eye drop products are being pulled from store shelves following an FDA recall tied to manufacturing problems at the plant that makes them. If you use Walgreens store-brand artificial tears or lubricant drops, you need to check whether your specific product is affected.

The recall covers Walgreens Lubricant Eye Drops, part of a broader action involving nearly 750,000 total ophthalmic products made by Sterling Pharmaceutical Services LLC at its facility in Dupo, Illinois. The Walgreens-branded portion comprises approximately 301,114 individual bottles and 199,600 twin packs. The FDA classified this as a Class II recall, meaning there is a remote possibility of serious harm or a chance of temporary adverse effects. Sterling initiated the recall on August 20, 2026, and the FDA classified it on September 21.

Stop using any Walgreens-branded lubricant eye drops that match the recalled identifiers and check the current FDA enforcement record at FDA.gov for the specific lot numbers and product identifiers. Compare your product against the recall notice before deciding to stop use and return it. Follow the instructions in the recall notice for the remedy and contact information. Contact an optometrist if you have been using a recalled product and experience redness, discharge, increased light sensitivity, or blurred vision — nonsterile ophthalmic solutions carry a risk of eye infection and irritation when applied directly to the eye.

**What "lack of sterility assurance" means**

The reason for this recall is lack of sterility assurance — meaning the manufacturing process could not be verified as meeting sterility standards, not necessarily that bacteria were confirmed inside consumer bottles. The recall reason is a manufacturing quality problem, not a reported infection outbreak in users of these products.

When product sterility cannot be confirmed by the manufacturer, the FDA requires a recall even without confirmed contamination in distributed products. The logic is straightforward: eye drops must be sterile because they go directly onto corneal tissue, and products whose manufacturing controls failed verification cannot be labeled as sterile.

This is why the classification is Class II rather than the more severe Class I. The 2023 outbreak involving Pseudomonas aeruginosa-contaminated eye drops from a different manufacturer — which caused dozens of severe infections and multiple deaths — involved confirmed bacterial contamination in distributed consumer products. This Walgreens recall involves a different scenario: a manufacturing quality verification failure, not a confirmed outbreak.

For consumers buying store-brand eye drops at major pharmacy chains, the practical guidance is the same in either case: check your lot numbers against the recall notice, stop using recalled products, and see an eye doctor if you have symptoms.

## PRIORITY 8 — Story 10: Trump $5,000 Midterm Checks

**HEADLINE:** Trump Promises $5,000 Checks to Every Adult US Citizen — Why Economists Say the Math Doesn't Work

---

President Trump has promised that every adult US citizen will receive a $5,000 cash payment if Republicans retain control of both chambers of Congress. The White House is calling it the "Trump Dividend," framing it as money returned to Americans from tariff revenues. The proposal was first announced on September 10, 2026. Financial analysts and nonpartisan budget experts say the numbers do not add up.

The White House published its announcement on September 10, describing the $5,000 payment as conditional on Republican victories in the House and Senate. There is no legislation, no funding mechanism, and no payment schedule in place. The announcement is a campaign pledge, not an active government program.

Do not adjust your household budget, debt repayment plan, or investment strategy based on this promise. The proposal faces two distinct barriers — one fiscal, one constitutional — that make it highly unlikely to become a real payment, regardless of the midterm outcome. Base your financial decisions on tax brackets, credits, and programs that currently exist.

**Why the math doesn't work**

There are approximately 245 million adult US citizens. The Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget estimates the proposal would cost approximately $1.2 trillion. According to CRFB's analysis, new tariff revenue is currently generating less than $200 billion annually — well below what the proposal would require. Absent additional offsets or borrowing, the gap between tariff revenues and the cost of this proposal would add directly to the federal deficit.

The constitutional barrier is separate. Under Article I, Section 9 of the US Constitution, federal spending requires a statutory appropriation bill passed by Congress and signed into law. The President cannot create a broad new $1.2 trillion payment program through an executive order. Even if Republicans win both chambers, passing a bill of this scale would face procedural hurdles including the Senate filibuster, though reconciliation procedures can alter that path.

The White House has not released a legislative text, and no supporting bill has been introduced in Congress. Campaign proposals of this type have been announced before midterm elections without becoming legislation.

For context: the 2020 and 2021 COVID stimulus checks, which were real payments most Americans received, ranged from $600 to $1,400 per person across three rounds, were funded through emergency appropriations legislation that Congress actually passed, and still added hundreds of billions to the deficit.

Any legitimate government cash payment program will be announced through IRS.gov, through a signed law posted at Congress.gov, or through a formal White House press release with legislative details. The White House announcement of September 10 describes the proposal but contains no legislative details or payment schedule.

## PRIORITY 10 — Story 12: E85 Bioethanol Fuel Savings

**HEADLINE:** E85 Bioethanol Fuel Is Cheaper at the Pump — But the Real Savings Per Kilometre Are Different

---

Drivers across the US and Europe are examining E85 bioethanol fuel as pump prices stay high, attracted by lower prices per litre or gallon compared to standard gasoline. Before booking a conversion kit installation, the economics are more nuanced than the price board suggests.

E85 is a fuel blend containing between 51% and 83% ethanol in the US (varying by season and geography) or 65% to 85% in France, derived from agricultural crops — primarily corn in the US and sugar beet or grain in Europe. It is priced at a significant discount to standard unleaded gasoline in markets where it is available. In France, E85 (known as Superéthanol-E85) is currently priced around €0.90 per litre at most stations, compared to roughly €2.17 per litre for standard E10 fuel — approximately 59% lower per litre.

Here is the calculation most promoters skip: the US Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center reports that E85 contains approximately 73% to 83% of the energy of a gasoline-gallon equivalent, depending on the specific blend. That means your engine burns more E85 per trip than it would gasoline, reducing fuel economy by approximately 25 to 30% depending on the vehicle and driving conditions.

Calculate your annual driving distance before investing in a conversion. The pump price gap between E85 and standard gasoline is real, but the fuel economy penalty closes it. Net savings per kilometre depend on local prices, your vehicle's fuel consumption, and available blend. For a driver covering 15,000 kilometres (about 9,300 miles) annually in France at current prices, meaningful savings are possible, but the exact figure depends on individual vehicle efficiency and local prices — calculate it for your own situation using current pump prices rather than relying on a single published estimate.

**What drivers need to know before converting**

Not every car can run on E85. Flex-fuel vehicles — those factory-equipped to handle high-ethanol blends — can switch without modification. In France, vehicles without factory flex-fuel capability may be converted using a homologated kit installed by an authorized installer, subject to specific regulatory requirements under French law. In the US, legal and emissions-compliance requirements differ significantly by state, and converting a non-flex-fuel vehicle may raise warranty, insurance, and regulatory issues.

Installing an uncertified or non-homologated aftermarket kit risks voiding the manufacturer's engine warranty. Cold-start performance in winter temperatures is a known design consideration with E85, as ethanol's volatility characteristics differ from gasoline.

The US Alternative Fuels Data Center maintains a locator for E85-compatible fueling stations. In the US, availability is concentrated in Midwest states. In Europe, French availability has expanded under a bioethanol promotion program; availability varies across other EU member states.

On emissions: lifecycle analyses find that beet- and grain-derived E85 can reduce net greenhouse gas emissions compared to petroleum gasoline, though the exact reduction depends heavily on feedstock, production method, co-products, and land-use assumptions. It cannot be stated as a universal 50% reduction across all E85.

If you drive over 12,000 miles (about 19,300 km) annually, own a flex-fuel or legally convertible vehicle, and have a compatible fueling station within a reasonable distance of your normal routes, the economics of E85 are worth calculating carefully at current prices for your specific situation.

## PRIORITY 11 — Story 7: Alan Alda at 90, Living with Parkinson's

**HEADLINE:** Alan Alda Turns 90 With Parkinson's Disease — What 12 Years of Living With It Actually Looks Like

---

Alan Alda is 90 years old and living with Parkinson's disease. He was diagnosed in 2014 at age 79, more than 12 years ago, and is still writing, podcasting, and doing interviews. In a CBS Sunday Morning conversation, he described his approach to the disease in terms that most medical coverage tends to flatten: "It's not who I am. It's just a hobby."

Alda's diagnosis came in part because his earliest symptom was not a tremor. He told CBS that he first noticed himself physically acting out dreams while asleep — a movement disorder called REM Sleep Behavior Disorder, which can precede Parkinson's motor symptoms by years or even decades. That symptom prompted earlier evaluation and diagnosis, enabling him to begin working with doctors and developing a physical management routine before the more visible symptoms that most people associate with the disease appeared.

Understand what a Parkinson's diagnosis means in terms of practical management. Alda's continued public engagement at 90 reflects a proactive approach to physical activity and medical management — started before significant motor disability developed. The practical guidance from movement disorder neurologists is to start physical therapy and targeted exercise early, rather than waiting for motor symptoms to worsen. Consider asking for a referral to a movement disorder neurologist — a specialist in conditions like Parkinson's — rather than relying solely on general primary care for ongoing management.

**The science behind what Alda actually does**

Alda's regimen includes boxing (non-contact), juggling, and physical activities that demand coordination and balance. Research on exercise and Parkinson's disease, including systematic reviews, supports that physical activity — particularly activities requiring dynamic balance, bilateral coordination, and rhythmic movement — is associated with improved motor function and quality of life in people with Parkinson's. Evidence on whether exercise modifies the underlying disease course or slows progression remains more limited.

The Michael J. Fox Foundation, a major Parkinson's research funder, and clinical rehabilitation guidelines broadly support beginning targeted physical therapy as early as possible after diagnosis, rather than waiting for mobility problems to develop. Alda's approach is consistent with those recommendations.

His use of humor and continued cognitive and social engagement are part of how he describes his daily experience with the condition. Maintaining active engagement is associated in broader research with quality-of-life benefits; the exact neurological mechanisms are not fully established, and humor should not be presented as a medical treatment for Parkinson's biology.

Parkinson's affects approximately one million people in the US and ten million globally according to Parkinson's Foundation estimates. Alda, as a widely recognized public figure talking openly about his diagnosis and daily management, gives the condition a visible face at a stage — more than a decade in, still functioning at a high level — that most public representations of the disease don't show.
