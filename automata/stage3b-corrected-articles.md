## British Airways Owes £29,195 After HR Botched Dismissal of Flight Attendant Over "Mile High Club" Comments

A Reading Employment Tribunal has ordered British Airways to pay £29,195.69 to a former cabin crew member — not because the judge ruled him innocent, but because BA's investigators questioned him before taking a complete statement from the complainant. The ruling illustrates the financial consequences of an employment tribunal finding that an employer's investigation was not sufficiently reasonable.

Charly Mateo, 37, was employed by British Airways from September 2018 until his dismissal in July 2024. The incident at the center of the case took place in March 2024 while Mateo was traveling in a "positioning" capacity — flying as a passenger, not working active crew — when he allegedly told a female colleague he wanted her to join the "mile high club" and touched her knee behind a rest curtain. BA dismissed him four months later. He brought an unfair dismissal claim before Employment Judge Anna Macey at Reading Employment Tribunal. The award came to £29,195.69. His request for reinstatement was denied.

The tribunal found BA's investigation unreasonable because it interviewed Mateo before obtaining a full account from the complainant and failed to investigate her allegation that he touched her knee in sufficient depth. Even when an employer holds a genuine belief that gross misconduct occurred, failures in how the investigation was conducted can render a dismissal unfair and expose companies to significant tribunal awards.

Judge Macey described Mateo's behavior as "strange" and an "invasion of privacy." She accepted that BA held genuine grounds to believe the misconduct occurred and that the breakdown of trust was sufficiently serious to make reinstatement impracticable. Both findings went in British Airways' favor.

The airline still lost because of how it ran its investigation. BA's HR team interrogated Mateo before securing a detailed, finalized witness statement from the complainant. Under the ACAS Code of Practice — the standard employment tribunals apply when judging whether a dismissal was reasonably conducted — employers must investigate allegations, give the employee the opportunity to respond, and be fair and thorough in gathering information from all sides. The tribunal held that BA's investigation did not meet the required standard.

That investigative failure cost £29,195.69, even as the judge simultaneously refused to order Mateo's return to the airline.

[INTERNAL LINK: How unfair dismissal compensation is calculated in the UK]

### Why You Can Win a Tribunal Even If You Behaved Wrongly

The Mateo case answers a question many people get wrong: can you receive compensation even if the tribunal accepts that you acted inappropriately?

Yes. UK employment tribunals evaluate two distinct questions separately. The first is whether the employer had reasonable grounds to believe misconduct occurred. The second is whether the employer followed a fair and thorough procedure before dismissing. A finding on the first does not decide the second.

Mateo's compensation was reduced to reflect his contribution to the situation — a standard tribunal adjustment when employee conduct played a part. He walked away with money, not a job.

[INTERNAL LINK: ACAS Code of Practice: what UK employers must follow in disciplinary cases]

For HR departments handling misconduct cases with time pressure or reputational sensitivity — common in aviation, where crew welfare and brand image are closely linked — the temptation to compress investigative steps is real. Employment tribunals do not accept urgency as a justification. The cost of following the ACAS Code correctly is administrative time. The cost of skipping it, as British Airways now knows, can exceed £29,000 per case.

## Trump Drops the US Diesel Export Ban — But the G7 Reserve Drawdown Leaves Europe Exposed This Winter

*Update, October 5, 2026: Donald Trump has confirmed the US will not impose a diesel export ban following a deal with G7 allies. This article covers the market consequence. [Karmactive previously reported on the original export-ban threat and the initial price spike on October 2 and October 3.]*

The threat is gone. The price relief it is producing may be temporary.

Donald Trump confirmed this week that the United States will not implement a ban on diesel exports to Europe, resolving the most acute phase of a transatlantic fuel standoff that drove diesel price anxiety across UK, EU, and US markets since late September. The decision came after European governments and G7 allies agreed to release 100 million barrels of emergency petroleum reserves — including refined diesel stocks — to offset the supply shortfall that made the export ban politically viable in the first place.

While Trump's withdrawal of the export ban averts an immediate diesel shortage across Europe and the UK, commercial transport operators should not expect long-term price relief. Tapping emergency reserves is lowering pump prices in the near term, but depleting strategic stockpiles leaves refiners with tighter inventories entering winter, meaning a cold snap could trigger sharp, buffered price surges.

Trump stated directly: "We're not going to be doing the export ban... Europe stepped up to release their reserves."

### What Actually Changed — and What Didn't

The G7 reserve release matters more than it sounds on first reading because it includes refined products, not just crude. That is the specific detail that moved markets. Adding crude to supply lowers input costs for refineries over weeks; releasing diesel directly into the physical market addresses product shortages in days.

ING's commodities research, published October 5, shows that middle distillate crack spreads — the margin refineries earn by turning crude oil into diesel — have weakened sharply since the reserve release was announced. When crack spreads fall, refiners earn less per barrel of diesel they produce. If margins continue compressing, some European refinery operators may cut run rates, reducing the very product they were just told to produce more of.

For logistics operators and fleet managers in the UK and EU, the immediate practical outcome is a temporary easing at the pump. UK diesel prices had been elevated against seasonal averages for most of September. Some moderation is now moving through the pricing chain.

The structural risk sits further ahead. European emergency reserves operate under IEA rules that require member states to maintain a 90-day net import cushion at all times. Drawing down those stocks to the levels being discussed in the current release will leave physical inventory buffers thinner than usual as winter heating demand accelerates. If temperatures in northern and central Europe run below historical averages between November and February — which long-range forecasts currently do not rule out — physical diesel markets could re-tighten sharply with reduced buffer.

### What UK and US Operators Should Watch

The current softening in crack spreads is supply-release driven rather than demand-driven, which means it will reverse when the released volumes clear the market. Operators running on tight fuel margins should assess their exposure to price volatility as the released volumes work through the market.

## High LDL Cholesterol Is Dangerous Even in Fit, Healthy People — What Cardiologists Say About the Myths Going Viral

Cardiologists are pushing back against a wave of viral online content claiming that LDL cholesterol is harmless, that statins cause widespread memory loss, and that people who feel healthy can safely ignore elevated lipid numbers. The claims are spreading on social media and leading some patients to discontinue prescribed medication. The medical evidence does not support any of them.

The core argument being circulated — that LDL cholesterol is not actually dangerous if a person is physically fit, slim, and eating well — contradicts decades of cardiovascular research. The American Heart Association and the European Society of Cardiology both conclude that elevated LDL-C is an independent risk factor for heart disease and stroke regardless of other lifestyle markers. The Cholesterol Treatment Trialists' Collaboration meta-analysis of randomized clinical trials shows that every 1 mmol/L sustained reduction in LDL-C produces a 21–22% relative reduction in major cardiovascular events.

If you have considered stopping your statin prescription after watching viral videos claiming cholesterol is harmless, medical evidence contradicts those claims. A large body of genetic, observational and randomized-trial evidence supports a causal role for LDL-containing lipoproteins in atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. Stopping prescribed treatment can increase cardiovascular risk for some patients, so changes should be discussed with the prescribing clinician.

### Can You Be Fit, Thin and Active — and Still Have Dangerous Cholesterol?

Yes. A person can have normal blood pressure, blood sugar, weight, and exercise capacity while carrying elevated circulating LDL. The reason relates to how plaque accumulates. LDL particles penetrate the walls of blood vessels and oxidize over time, initiating an inflammatory process that progressively narrows arterial capacity. This happens independent of cardiovascular fitness. A person who runs marathons but carries genetically elevated LDL from early adulthood will have more arterial plaque by their fifties than a sedentary person with low LDL.

[INTERNAL LINK: What your cholesterol test results mean — a plain-language guide]

Mendelian randomization studies — which use genetic variants as natural experiments to isolate LDL's causal contribution — confirm the mechanism. People born with naturally lower LDL due to specific genetic variants have proportionally lower lifetime cardiovascular risk even without any other lifestyle advantage. This is not a question of association; it is a causal relationship established over more than 30 years of research.

### What About Statin Side Effects?

Muscle aches are the most commonly reported concern among statin users, and social media amplifies individual negative experiences far beyond their statistical prevalence. In the SAMSON trial — a study of 60 people who had previously stopped statins because of symptoms — about 90% of the symptom burden reported during statin months was also experienced during placebo months. This indicates that for that group, a substantial portion of reported symptoms may be explained by the nocebo effect rather than the drug's direct pharmacological action.

Genuine statin-associated muscle symptoms do occur in clinical practice, and they are medically manageable. Changing the statin drug, adjusting the dose, or reviewing interactions with other medications may help. Stopping medication without medical guidance is not the appropriate response.

[INTERNAL LINK: Questions to ask your doctor before starting or stopping a statin]

Treatment decisions are not made on LDL numbers alone. The American Heart Association says overall cardiovascular risk — which includes age, blood pressure, smoking status, diabetes, and family history — guides clinical recommendations. A 55-year-old with a family history of early heart disease and LDL of 4.5 mmol/L faces a very different risk profile from a 28-year-old with no family history and the same number.

Can you be otherwise healthy with high LDL cholesterol? Yes — but high LDL still carries independent cardiovascular risk and warrants discussion with your clinician. Regular exercise and a balanced diet reduce overall metabolic risk, but they cannot fully counteract the biological damage of elevated circulating LDL over time. Excess LDL exposure contributes to plaque accumulation that restricts blood flow to the heart and brain over decades.

The cardiologists currently engaged in correcting this online misinformation wave are not arguing for universal statin use. They are arguing against patients making irreversible decisions about lifesaving preventive therapy based on influencer content rather than evidence-based cardiology guidelines.

## Should Your Bike Saddle Be Level? What Road Cyclists and Triathletes Need to Know About Saddle Angle

The conventional cycling instruction — always set your saddle dead level — has a significant exception that applies to many road cyclists, time trialists, and triathletes. Getting it wrong in either direction can cause discomfort and injury. Here is what actually governs saddle angle in 2026, and how to approach setting it for your riding position.

If you experience soft-tissue numbness or lower back tension during long rides, a perfectly level saddle may be contributing. A small nose-down tilt — typically in the range of two to four degrees as a possible starting point — may relieve direct pressure on the pudendal nerve and allow your pelvis to rotate naturally into an aggressive riding position. However, individual saddle design, rider flexibility, bike geometry, and fit all influence the right angle for any given cyclist. More tilt can transfer weight to the arms, potentially contributing to shoulder and wrist strain.

The traditional "dead level" rule was developed for an era of different saddle geometry. Modern cut-out saddles with curved profiles may require a different measurement approach. Placing a carpenter's level across the full length of these saddles can produce a misleading reading because the curve means the nose and tail sit higher than the middle section where your sit bones actually contact the saddle.

[INTERNAL LINK: How to set saddle height correctly — a step-by-step guide]

### Why Triathletes and Time Trialists Set Their Saddles Lower at the Front

Triathletes and time trialists use extreme forward-lean positions that rotate the pelvis sharply toward the handlebars. In that position, a level saddle can concentrate pressure on the perineum and genital nerves, causing numbness that builds over long events. A modest downward tilt counteracts this by allowing the pelvis to rotate anteriorly without compressing the spine into a bend.

UCI technical regulations limit saddle tilt to less than nine degrees — a rule that applies to competitive cycling events. For most road cyclists who are not using a full aero position, a small nose-down tilt in the range associated with relief from soft-tissue discomfort is a common starting point for a professional bike fit, not a universal prescription. Riders experiencing persistent pain or numbness should seek a qualified bike fitting rather than making trial-and-error adjustments.

The critical point that most saddle tilt advice omits: if you tilt the nose down without simultaneously shortening the reach to your handlebars, you will slide forward on the saddle and brace your upper body continuously to stay in position. This trades groin numbness for neck pain and wrist strain. Any adjustment to tilt should be accompanied by a review of overall fit, ideally during a professional bike fit rather than a trial-and-error garage adjustment.
