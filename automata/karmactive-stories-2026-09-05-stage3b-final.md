# STORY 1: Australia's COVID Vaccine Situation, FluMist Shortage, and a New Kind of Nasal Vaccine Being Tested

### Australia Has Updated COVID Vaccines — Just Not the Newest One From Overseas

There is a version of this story circulating online that says Australia has no new COVID vaccine. That is not quite right — and the difference matters.

Australia does have updated COVID-19 vaccines. What it does not yet have is the newest formulation called XFG, which the United States approved for the 2026–27 season. Pfizer's XFG-adapted shot is now available in the US, but Australia's registered version from Pfizer targets an earlier variant called LP.8.1. Moderna's LP.8.1 version was approved here by the Therapeutic Goods Administration on June 22, 2026.

**So what is XFG, and does it matter?**

SARS-CoV-2 keeps changing. Every time the virus mutates its surface proteins, some of the immunity from older vaccines becomes less effective at blocking infection. Updating the vaccine recipe — called the antigen composition — is how health authorities try to keep protection strong. XFG is a newer variant of the virus, and Pfizer's US-approved shot is designed to match it more closely.

But here is the thing the World Health Organization said in May 2026: their recommendation for this period is actually LP.8.1. They said XFG or NB.1.8.1 formulations are acceptable alternatives if they offer broad protection — but LP.8.1 is the recommended target. Critically, WHO explicitly said people should not wait for a newer vaccine to arrive before getting vaccinated.

Australian surveillance data backs this up. The Australian Centre for Disease Control recorded 99 virus sequences between June 1 and June 14, 2026. NB.1.8.1 made up 56 of them, BA.3.2 accounted for 23, and XFG appeared in 16. All three variants are circulating here. The ACDC noted that the numbers are still relatively small for firm conclusions.

WHO's own review of the evidence found that LP.8.1 vaccination produced increased neutralising antibodies against NB.1.8.1 and XFG. Against BA.3.2, WHO's data showed lower neutralising responses — only modest increases compared with other JN.1-descendant variants, which reflects BA.3.2 being antigenically more distant from the LP.8.1 vaccine strain.

### NSW Families Can't Find the Free Nasal Flu Vaccine — But That Doesn't Mean No Flu Vaccine

A separate problem has emerged for families in the Illawarra region of NSW, where flu cases are rising. Many parents trying to book their children in for the free FluMist nasal spray vaccine are finding it is not available.

FluMist is a live attenuated influenza vaccine — meaning it contains weakened live virus rather than killed virus. It goes up the nose, not into the arm. Australia registered it for the first time in 2026, for people aged 2 to 17 as an alternative to the traditional flu shot. NSW funded it as part of the 2026 flu program, initially for children aged 2, 3 and 4 years, then expanding access to the full 2–17 age group from late May 2026 for a limited time.

The important thing to understand is that running out of the centrally distributed free FluMist supply does not mean running out of flu vaccines. The nasal spray may still be available at some GPs and Aboriginal Medical Services for those who check directly, but the state-distributed supply has run low. NSW Health confirms the injectable influenza vaccine is still available and recommended for everyone aged six months and older. Children aged six months to under five years can still get a free flu injection.

Australia's 2026 flu vaccines are trivalent — they cover two influenza A strains and one influenza B lineage. The B/Yamagata lineage is not included because it appears to be extinct.

If a provider says they have no FluMist, the practical step is to ask about the injectable option and whether the child qualifies for it free of charge.

### Scientists Are Testing a New Kind of Coronavirus Vaccine That Goes Up the Nose

On a completely separate front, researchers published a study on July 22, 2026 in the journal *hLife* that is worth knowing about — with clear caveats about what it actually shows.

The study tested a vaccine approach using tiny gold nanoparticles — specifically 5 nanometre and 15 nanometre particles — delivered into the nose. The idea is to build immunity right at the respiratory surface, where viruses first enter the body. Current vaccines go into the muscle and build immunity in the blood, which is useful, but does not always stop a virus from getting into the nose and throat in the first place.

The researchers loaded these nanoparticles with receptor-binding domains from six different coronaviruses — a mix they called a mosaic formulation. They also used an immune-boosting compound called cGAMP, which activates a cellular alarm system called STING. The tests were done in BALB/c mice, with three to four animals per group.

Results showed that the vaccinated mice produced neutralising antibodies not just against SARS-CoV-2 variants, but also against SARS-CoV, MERS-CoV, and related MERS-like viruses.

This is interesting early science. It builds on a 2023 study that also tested intranasal mosaic nanoparticle vaccines in mice and monkeys. But it is not a human vaccine. No safety or dosing tests in people have been published. The correct description is a preclinical candidate — something that works in mice and may eventually move into human trials.

**Summary:** Australia has LP.8.1 COVID vaccines registered and available. WHO says LP.8.1 is the right choice and vaccination should not be delayed waiting for newer XFG formulations. The free FluMist nasal spray supply is running low in NSW, but the injectable flu vaccine remains available and free for young children. A separate mouse study has shown early promise for a gold nanoparticle nasal vaccine targeting multiple coronaviruses, but it has not been tested in humans.

### Qantas Flight QF122: Flames, Loud Bangs, and a Safe Landing in Christchurch

A Qantas flight heading from Queenstown to Sydney turned back on Saturday after a problem developed with one of its engines shortly after takeoff. The aircraft landed safely in Christchurch, and nobody was hurt.

The flight, QF122, is a trans-Tasman service operated by a Boeing 737-800. It was carrying approximately 158 passengers and crew when the crew reported a suspected engine issue and diverted the aircraft.

People on the ground near Queenstown and passengers on board described hearing loud banging sounds and seeing flames coming from the left engine. This happened not long after the plane had lifted off.

Rather than continuing across the Tasman Sea toward Sydney, the pilots turned toward Christchurch. The flight landed there approximately 50 minutes after the issue first occurred. Qantas operates regular Christchurch–Sydney services, so it is a practical and equipped stop for a trans-Tasman diversion.

Qantas confirmed the incident and said: "One of our flights to Sydney experienced a suspected engine issue after take-off from Queenstown this afternoon and the aircraft diverted to Christchurch. Our pilots are highly trained to manage situations like this and the aircraft landed safely." The airline said it is arranging accommodation and alternative flights for affected passengers.

New Zealand's Civil Aviation Authority initially attributed the incident to a bird strike, but subsequently updated its position, saying the cause of the engine incident remains under investigation. Both engines remained operating throughout the diversion and no engine shutdown occurred. Qantas described the event as a suspected engine issue, and further technical findings from the investigation have not been made public.

**What is not yet known:** The detailed technical sequence of the engine event and the extent of any engine damage remain to be established through the CAA's formal investigation.

**Summary:** QF122 diverted safely to Christchurch on Saturday after a suspected engine issue shortly after departing Queenstown. All 158 passengers and crew are safe. New Zealand's Civil Aviation Authority initially attributed the incident to a bird strike before updating its position to say the cause remains under investigation.

### The Ganga Is at Its Highest Level Ever in Patna. Here Is What Is Happening in Bihar.

The river that runs through the heart of Bihar has crossed a mark it has never officially crossed before. As of Saturday morning, the Ganga at Gandhi Ghat in Patna reached 50.53 metres — one centimetre above the highest flood level ever recorded at that point.

According to the Bihar Water Resources Department bulletin, the water rose 10 centimetres in just four hours. That is not just a rising river. It is a river actively pushing into homes, fields, and streets.

**This is not just a Patna story.**

The flooding in Bihar is being fed from outside the state. Heavy rainfall in neighbouring Jharkhand and upstream in Nepal has driven enormous volumes of water into multiple river systems at once. The Ganga is not alone — the Gandak, Kosi, Burhi Gandak, Punpun, and Ghaghra rivers have all crossed their danger marks at various monitoring points.

The scale of the upstream releases tells you how much water was already coming before it reached Patna. Friday's reports recorded roughly 1.26 lakh cusecs released from the Gandak Barrage at Valmikinagar, 1.91 lakh cusecs at the Kosi's Birpur Barrage, and 5.23 lakh cusecs from the Sone river at the Indrapuri Barrage. That volume of water has to go somewhere, and it is going downstream into Bihar.

**Who is affected?**

Around 10.26 lakh people — that is over one million — are currently dealing with flooding across 11 districts in Bihar. The hardest-hit are Bhojpur, with approximately 2.84 lakh people affected across four blocks, and Patna, with about 2.32 lakh people across nine blocks. Vaishali has nearly 85,000 people affected, Saran has close to 20,000, and other districts including Buxar, Begusarai, Samastipur, Munger, Katihar, Bhagalpur, and Araria are also dealing with flooding.

Low-lying areas along the Ganga in Patna face the sharpest immediate risks. People there are losing access to homes, livestock, and farmland. Many have moved onto raised roads and embankments while waiting for the water to recede.

**The rescue operation running right now:**

The state has deployed 858 boats across the affected districts. Alongside the boats, 34 specialised rescue teams are operating — 27 from the State Disaster Response Force and seven from the National Disaster Response Force. Relief camps, community kitchens, and ration distribution are running in affected areas.

Bihar Chief Minister Samrat Choudhary flew over the affected zones on Friday and instructed officials to keep continuous watch over embankments from Buxar all the way to Bhagalpur. The state Disaster Management Department has warned people in low-lying areas and near riverbanks to avoid unnecessary movement, especially near ghats.

The situation in Varanasi, downstream in Uttar Pradesh, has also drawn attention at the national level. Prime Minister Narendra Modi was briefed on the Ganga's condition there, where the river has crossed its warning level.

**Summary:** The Ganga at Gandhi Ghat in Patna has exceeded its highest recorded flood mark by one centimetre, rising 10 cm in four hours on Saturday morning. Around 10.26 lakh people are affected across 11 Bihar districts. The state has deployed 858 boats and 34 rescue teams to assist those displaced by flooding fed in large part by heavy rainfall in Nepal and Jharkhand.
