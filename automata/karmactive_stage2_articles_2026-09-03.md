## **ARTICLE 5: EPFO UAN Activation Now Requires UMANG App — Portal Option Discontinued**

If your Employees' Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO) account identification number—your UAN—is inactive, you can no longer activate it through the EPFO member portal. The activation process has moved entirely to the UMANG app (Unified Mobile Application for New-age Governance), and it relies on facial recognition.

This change reflects a broader push toward digital identity verification. To activate your UAN via UMANG, you will need three things: your Aadhaar number, a mobile phone number linked to that Aadhaar, and the Aadhaar Face RD (Recognition Data) application. The process uses Aadhaar-based face authentication to confirm your identity before activating the UAN.

The UMANG app handles two separate scenarios. If your UAN has been allotted but never activated, UMANG can activate it. If your UAN already exists but is inactive, UMANG can reactivate it. Both are technically different journeys within the app, but both now route through facial authentication.

Why the change? In FY2024-25, the EPFO allotted 1.26 crore (12.6 million) new UANs, but only 44.68 lakh (4.468 million) were actually activated. That is a 35.30 percent activation rate—meaning roughly two-thirds of allotted UANs sat dormant. Inactive UANs complicate EPFO administration and leave contributions untracked. Shifting activation to a mobile-first, biometric-verified system may boost actual completion rates by making the process harder to abandon halfway.

The EPFO member portal still exists and remains operational for other functions. You can still track claims, retrieve your UAN if you have forgotten it, and process death-benefit claims. The portal itself has not disappeared—only the UAN activation pathway within it.

One separate reform worth clarifying: in June 2025, the EPFO announced a ₹5 lakh auto-settlement limit—meaning if you resign and your balance is ₹5 lakh or less, the amount settles automatically without a claims process. This is different from the UAN activation change and was implemented separately.

Another proposal circulating involves ATM withdrawals from EPFO accounts, but this remains part of the broader EPFO 3.0 direction under discussion. Until an official notification confirms it is live, treat it as a planned feature, not a current option.

Practical advice: Collect your Aadhaar details and ensure your Aadhaar is linked to an active mobile number before attempting UAN activation on UMANG. Download the app in advance so you are not troubleshooting technical issues when facing a deadline.

## **ARTICLE 6: Uttar Pradesh Launches UPCOS for Outsourced Workers — What the New Corporation Does (and Doesn't) Change**

Uttar Pradesh formally launched the Uttar Pradesh Corporation for Outsource Services (UPCOS) on September 2, 2026, at Lok Bhawan in Lucknow. The corporation, registered as a non-profit public limited company under the Companies Act 2013, covers approximately 3.5 lakh (350,000) contractual and outsourced workers across state departments, government agencies, and universities.

UPCOS is not regularization. It is not converting outsourced workers into permanent employees. What it is: a centralized system designed to standardize wages, ensure statutory compliance, and reduce payment delays that have plagued outsourced employment in the state.

The wage structure under UPCOS is ₹16,000 to ₹20,000 per month, with classifications into skilled and semi-skilled categories. The workable month is set at 26 billable days, and employment arrangements run on three-year term contracts. Salary is distributed electronically directly to worker bank accounts, with payment deadlines falling between the 1st and 5th of each month. This schedule differs from the erratic disbursement patterns that prompted the creation of UPCOS—workers in some departments had experienced months-long delays.

Statutory compliance is now tracked centrally. UPCOS mandates coverage under the Employees' Provident Fund (EPF), Employees' State Insurance (ESI), and accidental insurance. Previously, outsourced workers often fell through administrative gaps where these benefits were not deducted or submitted correctly. Centralization aims to close those gaps.

Additional benefits promised include medical facilities, maternity leave, accident insurance, pension, and family pension. The government also instituted a ₹15,000 ex-gratia payment for funeral assistance if a worker dies while employed.

Why create this new corporation? The government documented persistent complaints: arbitrary deductions from salaries, late payments stretching months, lack of EPF and ESI compliance, inadequate benefits, inconsistent commission structures, and violations of reservation policies. Some of these issues stemmed from fragmented hiring across departments, each with different contractor arrangements.

The UPCOS portal and website launched alongside the corporation, creating a digital front-end for workers to track grievances, view salary payments, and access information about benefits. This transparency mechanism is intended to prevent the kind of hidden deductions and undisclosed payment delays that characterized outsourced work previously.

The critical test is implementation. Creating a corporation and announcing policies is the easy part. Actually processing 3.5 lakh worker payrolls reliably, tracking EPF/ESI contributions correctly, and responding to grievances within a reasonable timeframe requires institutional capacity. The state's history with outsourced employment suggests that good policies have sometimes failed in execution. Workers should track whether salary arrives on schedule, whether statutory deductions are correct and tracked, and whether the transparency mechanism actually functions.

UPCOS represents a structural reform. It is not a wage increase or a path to permanent employment, but it is an attempt to bring order to a system that has historically been chaotic and unpredictable for the workers at the bottom.

## **ARTICLE 7: Gloria Steinem, Feminist Pioneer Who Reshaped American Politics, Dies at 92**

Gloria Steinem died on September 2, 2026, in New York City at age 92. The Gloria Steinem Foundation announced that she died peacefully at home. No cause of death was disclosed.

Her life spanned the transformation of feminism from a largely private conversation into a force that reshaped American law, politics, and culture. She did not accomplish this alone, but she was consistently at the center of the movements that made the reshaping possible.

In 1972, Steinem co-founded Ms. Magazine, the first national magazine in the United States to be edited and published entirely by women. Before that, American women's magazines were edited and owned by men, reflecting male editorial judgment about what women should read. Ms. changed that fundamental power structure. The magazine published journalism on reproductive rights, workplace discrimination, domestic violence, and equal pay—topics treated as private or peripheral by mainstream media. Ms. gave those topics national prominence and a feminist editorial voice.

A year earlier, in 1971, Steinem co-founded the National Women's Political Caucus alongside Shirley Chisholm, Bella Abzug, and Betty Friedan. The organization's specific purpose was training and supporting women candidates to run for public office. At the time, women held roughly 5 percent of seats in Congress. The caucus was an explicit attempt to change that mathematics through political power.

One of her lesser-known early experiences shaped her approach. In the late 1950s, as a fellow of the Chester Bowles fellowship, Steinem spent time in India, where she organized with grassroots Gandhian activists. That experience—working at the community level with people building coalitions for social change—influenced her methodology throughout her career. She remained committed to grassroots coalition-building rather than top-down mandate.

Her influence on specific legislation was substantial. She spearheaded public advocacy for the Equal Rights Amendment, the constitutional amendment that would have guaranteed gender equality under law. Though the ERA ultimately failed to achieve ratification, Steinem's activism elevated its profile and legitimacy. She was also a prominent advocate for the Equal Credit Opportunity Act of 1974, which prohibited lenders from requiring a married woman to have her husband co-sign for credit, loans, or mortgages. Before that law, women were financial dependents of their husbands by legal structure—the ECOA broke that chain.

Her coalition work extended beyond white feminist spaces. She organized alongside Dorothy Pitman Hughes and Flo Kennedy, bringing intersectional perspectives to feminist activism at a time when intersectionality was not yet a common term.

In 2013, President Barack Obama awarded Steinem the Presidential Medal of Freedom, the nation's highest civilian honor.

Days before her death, Ms. Magazine published a piece credited to Steinem, dated August 27, 2026, showing that her writing remained active and current even as her life was ending. The Gloria Steinem Foundation, which she established, has preserved her archives and maintains her home as a site for feminist organizing and the "Talking Circles" she pioneered—intimate gatherings where people discuss their experiences and build solidarity. Since 2022, the foundation has hosted more than 50 of these circles.

## **ARTICLE 8: Netherlands Moves 86 Tonnes of Gold to London — Why Central Banks Are Shifting Reserves**

The Dutch central bank (De Nederlandsche Bank, or DNB) has transferred 86 metric tonnes of gold from vaults in the United States and Canada to the Bank of England in London over an eight-month period from March to August 2026. This is not a headline-grabbing repatriation or a renunciation of the dollar. It is a quiet reconfiguration of where a portion of the Netherlands' gold reserves sits—and it reveals how central banks are rethinking crisis preparedness.

Previously, DNB held approximately 313 tonnes of gold combined in the Federal Reserve Bank of New York and the Bank of Canada in Ottawa. The transfer of 86 tonnes leaves substantial holdings in both locations but redistributes a meaningful chunk to London. The rebalancing gives DNB a portfolio that now sits roughly one-third domestically (in the Zeist Cash Operations Centre), one-third in the UK (Bank of England), and one-third across other global locations.

Why move gold across the Atlantic at all? The reasons are practical and unsexy. London is the hub of the London Bullion Market Association (LBMA)—the global center where gold is traded, cleared, and settled. If a financial crisis strikes and a country needs to quickly access gold as collateral for emergency loans, or convert it to foreign exchange, London's position as the LBMA clearinghouse matters enormously. Gold held in London can be traded or pledged as collateral without the logistics delays that cross-oceanic transport entails. In a genuine financial emergency, that speed translates into survival.

The Netherlands is also diversifying geographic risk. Keeping all gold in one location—say, New York—concentrates exposure to a single geopolitical jurisdiction. Splitting holdings across New York, London, and domestic vaults reduces that concentration. It is a hedge against an extreme but conceivable scenario where one country's political relations or financial system become unstable.

The DNB's stated reasons, per the ECB's June 2026 reporting, emphasize improved crisis preparedness, better liquidity access at LBMA, geographic diversification, and easier availability during emergencies. This is defensive language. It is not panicked or radical. It reflects ordinary central-bank risk management.

However, DNB's move occurs in a context of broader central-bank behavior. Global central banks purchased approximately 850 tonnes of gold in 2025, down from more than 1,000 tonnes annually during 2022–24. That plateau in purchases still represents elevated demand compared to decades past. Central banks are maintaining interest in gold as a reserve asset—not abandoning it—and they are recalculating where and how to hold it.

A common narrative frames this as "de-dollarization"—countries rejecting the U.S. dollar as global reserve currency. The data tells a murkier story. As of June 2026, the U.S. dollar still comprised approximately 57 percent of global official foreign-exchange reserves. That dominance has declined from its historical 70+ percent, but the dollar remains the world's overwhelmingly preferred reserve asset. Moving gold between London and New York does not change that.

DNB's transfer is administrative optimization, not a geopolitical rebellion.

## **ARTICLE 9: Strong El Niño Expected to Strengthen Further, Likely Persisting Through February 2027**

The World Meteorological Organization (WMO) released an update in September 2026 confirming that El Niño—the periodic warming of the eastern tropical Pacific Ocean—is firmly established and has a nearly 100-percent likelihood of persisting through February 2027. This is not a forecast of something that might happen. It is a statement about something already happening and how long it will last.

El Niño is expected to strengthen further from its current intensity. The WMO expects it to reach "very strong" intensity, peaking around the end of 2026. For the September-November 2026 period, the WMO forecast shows above-normal temperatures across almost all land areas globally. Regional variations exist—some areas experience heat, others drought, others rainfall—but the baseline expectation is warmer than historical norms.

For South Asian readers, the Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD)—an independent climate oscillation—matters significantly. The WMO expects a positive IOD to develop during the same September-November period, with a seasonal mean forecast of 0.9 degrees Celsius above normal. The IOD can modify how El Niño impacts the region. When both are in positive phases simultaneously, rainfall patterns across India and the broader Indian subcontinent shift. This can mean weaker monsoons in some areas or atypical rainfall distribution. Farmers, water-management officials, and disaster-preparedness planners in the region should monitor both indices.

The agricultural consequences are severe in vulnerable regions. Drought risk is elevated across Southeast Asia, eastern Australia, and parts of Southern Africa. The combination of heat and reduced rainfall stresses crops, water supplies, and livestock. In regions already dealing with water scarcity or crop failures from previous climate events, El Niño amplifies existing stress.

Conversely, some regions face torrential rainfall risk. The west coast of South America and parts of the southern United States are forecast to experience increased heavy precipitation. That brings flood and landslide hazards where rainfall is intense.

Marine ecosystems face thermal stress. Ocean heat contributes to coral bleaching—the process where coral expels algae under heat stress, turning white and eventually dying if conditions do not cool. This year's El Niño is expected to elevate bleaching risk in multiple coral regions globally.

The WMO explicitly states that national meteorological and hydrological services are stepping up preparedness and early-warning systems. The forecasting certainty is unusually high, which gives governments and communities time to prepare. That preparation matters because El Niño's impacts are not uniform globally. A region's vulnerability depends on its existing water supplies, food production, infrastructure, and disaster-response capacity.

The outlook extends across an eight-month window. Unlike short-term weather forecasts, which are uncertain beyond two weeks, seasonal forecasts attempt to predict average conditions over months. They are correct more often than random chance but carry inherent uncertainty. Still, a near-certain El Niño persistence through February 2027 is actionable information for water agencies, agricultural planners, and disaster-management systems.
