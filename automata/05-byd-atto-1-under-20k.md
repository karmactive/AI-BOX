# BYD Atto 1 Drops to $19,990 Drive-Away: Is Australia's Cheapest EV Worth It?

An electric car is now being sold in Australia for less than $20,000 drive-away. BYD has cut the price of its Atto 1 Essential to $19,990 — a figure that puts it in the same range as Australia's cheapest new petrol hatchbacks, and changes the conversation about who EVs are actually for.

**Verification note:** The $19,990 drive-away price is currently being reported by multiple automotive outlets including The Driven and ZeCar. Karmactive recommends confirming the current official price and any applicable conditions directly with a BYD dealer or BYD's Australian site before purchase, as drive-away pricing can vary by state and change without notice.

## What the Price Cut Actually Means

At $19,990 drive-away, the Atto 1 Essential is being described as Australia's cheapest new car — not just cheapest new EV.

That framing matters. EVs have traditionally been positioned as a premium option requiring a significant upfront investment over equivalent petrol alternatives. The Atto 1 Essential at this price removes that framing for buyers in the small car segment. A new small petrol hatchback in the $20,000–$23,000 range is now being compared directly against an electric car at the same price point.

Drive-away pricing means there are no additional on-road costs — registration, stamp duty, and dealer delivery charges are included in the $19,990 figure.

## What You Get

The Atto 1 Essential is BYD's entry-level version of the Atto 1, a small SUV-style vehicle. At the Essential specification, buyers should expect a reduced feature set compared to higher trim levels — the trade-off for the price.

Key specifications reported for the Atto 1 include a range of approximately 300 kilometres on a full charge under official testing conditions. Real-world range will vary depending on driving style, climate, and use of features like air conditioning.

Charging is compatible with standard home chargers and public fast chargers. For Australian buyers considering an EV primarily for city driving and regular commuting within range, the Atto 1's specifications are functional.

On EV range testing conducted by AAA, BYD vehicles have been found to fall short of official range claims — by approximately 23% in prior testing of BYD models. That gap between claimed and real-world range is worth factoring into any purchase decision.

BYD took full control of its Australian distribution in mid-2025, ending its arrangement with EVDirect and moving to a direct sales and service model. That shift affects how buyers purchase, service, and access warranty support for BYD vehicles in Australia.

## What $19,990 Means Against Petrol Cars

The price point creates a direct comparison that hasn't previously existed in the Australian new car market. Small petrol cars in the sub-$25,000 range include running costs — fuel, servicing — that EVs do not carry in the same way.

Electricity costs money, but on a per-kilometre basis, charging an EV at home is consistently cheaper than fuelling a petrol equivalent for most Australian driving patterns. Servicing costs are also lower for EVs, which have fewer mechanical components requiring regular maintenance.

The break-even point between the Atto 1 Essential and a similarly priced petrol car — accounting for fuel, servicing, and depreciation — will depend on the buyer's annual mileage and electricity costs. For high-mileage city and suburban drivers, the EV case is strong. For low-mileage occasional drivers, the calculation is less clear.

## Who Should Consider This Car — and Who Shouldn't

**This car suits:** City and suburban drivers with regular access to home charging, doing primarily short to medium daily trips well within the 300km range. First-time EV buyers looking for a low entry point. Buyers who want to avoid petrol costs and simplified servicing.

**This car is a tougher fit for:** Buyers who frequently drive long distances between regional areas where charging infrastructure is limited. Buyers who rely on apartment or street parking without access to home charging. Anyone who needs to tow — check the Atto 1's towing capacity against your requirements before purchase.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does the $19,990 price include all on-road costs?**
Drive-away pricing in Australia includes registration, stamp duty, compulsory third-party insurance, and dealer delivery charges. The $19,990 figure is reported as drive-away, meaning no additional costs on top of that price. Confirm with the dealer as state-specific variations can apply.

**How far can the BYD Atto 1 Essential travel on a charge?**
The reported range is approximately 300 kilometres under official testing. Real-world range will be lower depending on driving conditions, speed, climate, and use of features like air conditioning. AAA testing of previous BYD models found real-world range approximately 23% below official claims.
