# Australia's Spring Cold Snap: When the Rain, Hail and Snow Will Hit

A cold front is moving through southeastern Australia this week, bringing rain, hail, and snow to parts of the country. If you are in Melbourne, Sydney, or Tasmania and planning outdoor activities in the next 48 hours, here is what the Bureau of Meteorology forecasts say.

## What the Bureau of Meteorology Is Forecasting

Victoria is seeing the sharpest conditions. BOM's forecast for the Gippsland Lakes region shows a 90% chance of showers with possible small hail. Temperatures are expected to drop noticeably below the seasonal average.

Tasmania is also in for a cold stretch, with widespread showers and possible hail across forecast areas.

Sydney and NSW waters show a different picture. The cold front's impact on Sydney is comparatively more moderate than Victoria and Tasmania, with conditions varying across the state.

The unusual factor is timing. This is September — officially spring. Cold fronts in mid-September are not unheard of in southern Australia, but the combination of rain, hail, and snow in the same system tends to catch people off-guard after the first warm days of the season.

## What Cold Snap Conditions Mean for Your City

The "brutal cold snap" framing in some coverage applies more directly to Victoria and Tasmania than to the entire country. This is not a uniform national weather event — it is a southeastern cold front, and its effect varies considerably by location.

For commuters, outdoor workers, and anyone planning to travel through Victoria or Tasmania in the next two days: conditions warrant a check of the current BOM forecast for your specific area before leaving. Road conditions in alpine areas may be affected by snowfall.

Sydney residents and those in coastal NSW will see cooler conditions and some rain, but the severe elements — hail and snow — are concentrated further south.

## When Will It Pass?

Cold fronts of this type typically move through southeastern Australia over 48–72 hours. Current forecasts put the most active conditions in the September 15–17 window, with conditions easing thereafter.

For the most current city-specific forecast, check bom.gov.au directly before any major outdoor plans this week.
