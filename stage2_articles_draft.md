# Medicare Advantage 2027 Changes: What Open Enrollment Means for Your Benefits This Fall

If your Medicare Advantage plan sent you an Annual Notice of Change letter in early October, now is the time to read it carefully. The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services projected in its September 2026 announcement that the weighted average monthly Medicare Advantage premium will drop 16.5%, from $14.37 to $12.00, for 2027 plans. CMS also expects more than 99% of Medicare beneficiaries to have access to at least one Medicare Advantage plan, with 97% having ten or more options. Those headline figures paint a stable picture. But insurer-level decisions are telling a different story for specific markets.

UnitedHealth Group is reducing supplemental benefits in a number of counties, affecting roughly 390,000 members. Humana is pulling back from plans that covered approximately 600,000 members in markets where medical cost ratios have exceeded sustainable thresholds. Both companies cited rising healthcare utilization among their Medicare Advantage populations — particularly in suburban and rural HMO markets — as the driver. What's actually happening is more targeted than blanket cuts: insurers are systematically exiting high-cost rural and suburban HMO plans while maintaining or expanding PPO offerings in denser urban markets where coding margins remain profitable. A beneficiary in one county may face elimination of their zero-premium plan; a neighbor in the next county may see the same insurer offering more options.

The practical effect for anyone receiving a plan termination notice: you are not simply thrown back into fee-for-service Medicare. You have specific federal rights that must be exercised before the enrollment window closes.

If your Medicare Advantage plan is among those being terminated for 2027, doing nothing will automatically return you to fee-for-service Original Medicare without prescription drug coverage or an out-of-pocket spending cap on January 1. To protect access to your current doctors and avoid lifetime Part D late-enrollment penalties, you must actively compare local 2027 formulary changes and select a replacement plan between October 15 and December 7.

## What "Guaranteed Issue" Rights Mean If Your Plan Is Terminated

If your insurer terminates your plan completely — not simply reduces benefits, but exits your county — federal law grants you a Guaranteed Issue right. You can return to Original Medicare and purchase a standard Medigap supplemental policy without medical underwriting. That means private insurers cannot deny coverage or charge higher premiums based on pre-existing health conditions. This right is time-limited and runs through March 4, 2027, for plans terminating December 31, 2026.

The distinction between plan termination and benefit reduction matters for what you're entitled to do:

**Plan termination** (insurer exits your county entirely): Triggers Guaranteed Issue rights for Medigap Plans A, B, C, F, K, or L. You can return to Original Medicare with supplemental coverage regardless of health history.

**Benefit reduction** (same plan, higher co-pays, stripped supplemental allowances, smaller formulary): Does not trigger Guaranteed Issue. You can switch to a different Medicare Advantage plan or a Part D drug plan during AEP, but you cannot buy Medigap without underwriting.

Read your Annual Notice of Change letter to determine which applies to you. The ANOC was mailed October 2 and outlines every change to premiums, cost-sharing, provider networks, and drug formularies taking effect January 1.

Use the Medicare Plan Finder tool at medicare.gov — which opens with the full 2027 plan database on October 15 — to compare options in your zip code. When comparing plans, prioritize the formulary tier for your specific medications and whether your current primary care doctor is in-network for 2027, not the headline monthly premium.

Millions of Americans on fixed incomes navigating [Medicare's annual enrollment cycle](https://karmactive.com/medicare-annual-enrollment-guide) and [the ongoing shift in Medicare Advantage markets](https://karmactive.com/medicare-advantage-market-trends-2026) face real coverage disruption if they miss the December 7 deadline.

## What "Dismissed With Prejudice" Actually Means

Criminal cases can be dismissed in two ways. A dismissal without prejudice leaves the door open for prosecutors to refile charges, rework their evidence, or wait for new developments before bringing the same case again. A dismissal with prejudice is a final ruling on the merits. It closes the case permanently. The government cannot refile, cannot seek new indictments on the same facts, and cannot use a future administration or a different U.S. Attorney to revisit the charges. Judge Edelman's ruling means Hearn faces zero legal exposure from this case going forward.

The procedural context matters here. Prosecutors reportedly sought a dismissal without prejudice — which would have preserved their ability to refile — and the judge rejected that path. The ruling with prejudice was a deliberate judicial choice, not a default outcome.

The underlying facts that drove the dismissal are significant. The National Park Service commissioned an engineering assessment of the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool that showed the waterproof coating on the pool basin was failing due to natural chemical degradation, not human contact. That assessment existed before the government publicly filed charges. Prosecutors had access to internal NPS engineering documentation clearing Hearn of physical destruction while they were still pursuing the case.

The $150,000 restitution demand that Hearn faced was based on restoration bids that the NPS had already budgeted for routine maintenance years before the alleged incident. That means the financial figure prosecutors used to justify the felony threshold — property damage above a certain dollar amount elevates misdemeanor vandalism to a felony — was drawn from pre-existing maintenance cost estimates, not from any new damage attributable to Hearn.

Judge Edelman's ruling cited the complete absence of criminal intent and the engineering evidence on record. He reprimanded the government's handling of the prosecution. Hearn described the ruling publicly as a victory for democratic accountability.
