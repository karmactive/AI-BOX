# 2027 Social Security COLA: What We Know After August Inflation Data

The 2027 Social Security cost-of-living adjustment is not finalized — but August inflation data is now in, and it gives the clearest signal yet. If you receive Social Security benefits and are planning your 2027 household budget, here is what the current data shows, what is still missing, and exactly when the confirmed number will arrive.

---

## Current Estimates and the Official Announcement Date

The Bureau of Labor Statistics released its August Consumer Price Index data on September 11, 2026. The CPI-W — the specific inflation measure Social Security uses — rose 3.5% year-over-year. Organizations tracking COLA closely, including The Senior Citizens League (TSCL) and AARP's Public Policy Institute, have projected the 2027 COLA in the range of 3.5% to 3.6%. At an average Social Security retirement benefit of approximately $1,976 per month, a 3.5% adjustment would add roughly $70 to $75 to your monthly check starting in January 2027. The Social Security Administration has confirmed the official 2027 COLA will be announced on October 14, 2026 — the same date as the September CPI release.

---

## The Part Nobody in the Headlines Is Mentioning

While an estimated 3.5% to 3.6% bump could add roughly $70 to $75 to your average monthly benefit starting in January 2027, do not budget that entire amount for living expenses. The federal government automatically deducts Medicare Part B premiums directly from your check before it reaches your account. Projected increases in healthcare premiums and prescription deductibles mean a meaningful share of your 2027 COLA will be consumed before you spend a single dollar. Plan for a net increase, not the gross figure.

---

## The CPI-W Dilemma — Why the Math Falls Short for Seniors

The COLA formula is not built specifically for retirees. Social Security's annual adjustment uses the CPI-W — the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers — which tracks the spending patterns of working-age adults. They spend more on technology and commuting than retirees typically do.

Older Americans spend a larger share of their income on medical costs, housing, and prescription drugs — the categories that have outpaced general inflation most consistently. A wage-earner's index can produce a COLA that looks adequate as a percentage while falling short of what retirees actually experience in their monthly expenses.

Medicare Part B premiums compound the gap. Premiums are automatically deducted before deposit. CMS typically announces the following year's rates in November. If Part B premiums rise for 2027 — a near-certain outcome given healthcare cost trends — a portion of any COLA increase will be offset before the money reaches your account.

## The Forecast vs. the Official Number

The Social Security Administration's 2026 Trustees Report includes a projected 2027 COLA assumption of 2.4%. That figure is a long-term actuarial planning assumption — it is not a forecast of the actual 2027 COLA and it does not reflect current inflation data. The actual 2027 adjustment will depend entirely on the September CPI-W reading released October 14. The August data already suggests the outcome will be higher than the Trustees assumed, but September's data is what completes the calculation by law.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**When will the official 2027 Social Security COLA be announced?**

The Social Security Administration will officially announce the 2027 COLA on October 14, 2026. That date coincides with the Bureau of Labor Statistics' September CPI data release, which provides the final third-quarter inflation reading required by federal law to complete the COLA calculation.

**How is the Social Security COLA calculated?**

SSA calculates the COLA by comparing the average CPI-W for July, August, and September of the current year against the same three-month average from the prior year. If the current quarter's average is higher, that percentage difference becomes the COLA. September's data — releasing October 14 — is the final input needed. If the third-quarter average is not higher than the prior year's, no adjustment is made.

**Will higher Medicare Part B premiums reduce the benefit of the 2027 COLA?**

Yes, for most beneficiaries. Medicare Part B premiums are automatically deducted from Social Security checks before deposit. The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services typically announces the following year's premium rates in November. If Part B premiums increase for 2027, that amount reduces the net dollar gain from the COLA before any money reaches your account.

**When will the 2027 Social Security increase appear in checks?**

If SSA announces a 2027 COLA in October 2026, the adjusted benefit amount takes effect with January 2027 payments. Social Security deposits for January 2027 are paid in early January based on each recipient's designated payment date.
