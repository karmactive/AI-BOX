# U.S. Flight Delays Hit Major Airports as Weather Disrupts Travel

If you are flying through a major U.S. airport today — particularly on the West Coast, in the Southeast, or through Atlanta — you are likely facing delays. The FAA activated ground delay programs and operational advisories across multiple hub airports on September 11, 2026, following weather conditions that hit several regions simultaneously and cascaded through afternoon connecting traffic.

---

## Which Airports Are Affected

FAA operational advisories on September 11, 2026 identified weather-related constraints at airports including San Francisco (SFO), Los Angeles (LAX), San Diego (SAN), Atlanta (ATL), Miami (MIA), Fort Lauderdale, Orlando, and Tampa. Conditions varied by location — thunderstorms across the Southeast and central U.S., low clouds and coastal winds along California. SFO departure delays ran roughly 45 minutes; San Diego was approaching an hour. Southwest, American, and regional carriers reported affected flights. The FAA's Air Traffic Control System Command Center issued formal operational advisories covering the day's constraints.

---

## What This Means If You Are Flying Today

Do not assume a national delay headline means your specific flight is canceled. FAA data show disruptions were concentrated differently across airports, with weather, low clouds, thunderstorms, and operational restrictions affecting individual hubs at different levels. Check your airline's live flight status before leaving for the airport, and allow extra connection time for any itinerary routing through affected hubs.

---

## What Stranded Passengers Should Do Right Now

Do not stand in the terminal customer service queue with hundreds of other passengers. Use your airline's app to rebook — it is faster than any desk during mass disruptions. If the app is not working, call the airline's UK or Australian English-speaking support line; those desks typically have much shorter wait times than U.S. lines when American airports are under simultaneous pressure.

On refunds: under U.S. Department of Transportation rules, if your flight is canceled or significantly delayed and you choose not to travel, the airline must issue a full cash refund to your original payment method. Not a travel credit. Not a voucher. A cash refund. This is federal law and applies regardless of the stated reason for the disruption.

Note the distinction between weather delays and airline-controlled delays. Weather delays do not automatically trigger hotel or meal vouchers under DOT rules. Delays caused by airline staffing issues or mechanical problems do carry those obligations. That distinction determines what you can legitimately ask for at the service desk.

## Why One Hub's Delay Becomes Everyone's Problem

The FAA advisory for September 11 shows the disruption was not a single-cause event at a single location. Multiple hubs were simultaneously under different types of advisories — ground delays, ground stops, runway restrictions, low-cloud conditions — affecting different airports for different reasons at the same time. When West Coast hubs like SFO enter ground delay programs in the morning, aircraft scheduled to fly east are grounded before their crews reach mandatory federal duty-time limits. By afternoon, that leaves East Coast hubs short of aircraft and crews, multiplying delays that began thousands of miles away.

---

## Are You Entitled to a Cash Refund If Your Flight Is Canceled?

**Am I entitled to a cash refund if my US domestic flight is canceled or heavily delayed?**

Yes. Under federal U.S. Department of Transportation rules, if an airline cancels your flight or significantly changes your schedule and you decline the alternative offered, the airline is legally required to issue a full cash refund to your original payment method. This applies regardless of the delay's cause — weather included.
