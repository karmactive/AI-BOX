### Target Sugar Recall: Full List of Good & Gather Batches and UPC Codes

If you have Target's Good & Gather light brown or powdered sugar in your kitchen, check the back of the bag before you use it. A recall covering nearly 1.7 million pounds of sugar across eight states means some bags may contain wheat kernels — a serious risk for anyone with celiac disease or a wheat allergy.

United Sugar Producers and Refiners, based in Minneapolis, initiated a voluntary recall on August 19, 2026. The FDA formally classified it as Class II on September 21. The recall covers approximately 1.27 million pounds of light brown sugar and 426,000 pounds of powdered sugar, sold under three brand names: United, Good & Gather (Target's store brand), and Crystal Sugar. Affected products were distributed to Iowa, Illinois, Indiana, Kansas, Minnesota, Ohio, Pennsylvania, and Texas.

If someone in your household has celiac disease or a wheat allergy, stop using the product immediately and seal the bag. Continued use risks an allergic or autoimmune reaction. Consumers without wheat sensitivities face no health hazard, but all purchasers can return affected bags to any Target location for a full refund — no receipt required.

Check the back seam of your 2-lb Good & Gather bag for these UPC codes: Light Brown Sugar bears UPC 0 85239-08454 0; Powdered Sugar bears UPC 0 85239-08455 7. Recalled bags carry batch codes MHD26223 through MHD26229. If your bag matches both the UPC and the batch code range, do not use it.

The recall involves cross-contact during bulk grain handling, where raw agricultural wheat kernels entered processing silos alongside the sugar. A [wheat or gluten allergy](https://www.karmactive.com/food-allergy-safety-basics) causes the body to treat wheat proteins as a threat, triggering immune reactions ranging from hives to anaphylaxis. For people with [celiac disease](https://www.karmactive.com/understanding-fda-recall-classes), even trace amounts of wheat can cause lasting damage to the small intestine.

Although media coverage has focused on the 2-lb Good & Gather retail bags sold at Target, FDA filing records show more than 70 percent of the recalled volume moved in commercial 50-lb United and 25-lb Crystal Sugar sacks. That means regional bakeries and food-service kitchens may have processed affected product weeks before the retail consumer alert went public.

The firm initiated the withdrawal 33 days before the FDA published its formal enforcement classification — a gap worth knowing if you purchased sugar in late August from any retailer in the eight affected states.

The recall remains active. Consumers in the eight affected states should verify their product against the UPC codes and batch numbers above, then return eligible bags to the retailer for a refund. The FDA enforcement record is updated at FDA.gov as the recall progresses.

Three counties in the Tampa Bay area declared states of emergency after locally acquired dengue cases surged past 170, with a fourth county confirming its own local case on September 24. This is not a travel-related outbreak — the mosquitoes spreading dengue are breeding in Central Florida neighborhoods right now.

Hillsborough, Pinellas, and Pasco counties each issued emergency declarations on September 24. Pinellas County's official resolution recorded 143 confirmed cases across the tri-county region at the time of the declaration — 134 in Hillsborough, 7 in Pinellas, and 2 in Pasco. Polk County health officials separately confirmed a locally acquired dengue case and launched surveillance, treatment, and community outreach on September 24.

Central Florida residents should expect unscheduled municipal mosquito-control spraying near private properties in affected areas. If you develop sudden high fever, severe eye pain, or joint aches, avoid ibuprofen or aspirin — these medications increase bleeding risk in dengue patients. Request an official dengue diagnostic test from a healthcare provider immediately. Emergency declarations give county officials authority to access and treat standing water on private and abandoned properties without prior notice.

**What to Know About Dengue Symptoms and Transmission**

Dengue fever is transmitted by infected Aedes mosquitoes, not from person to person. Symptoms appear 4 to 10 days after a bite and include sudden high fever up to 104°F, intense pain behind the eyes, severe joint and muscle aches sometimes called "breakbone fever," nausea, and skin rash. Seek emergency care if abdominal pain or persistent vomiting develops — these can indicate severe dengue, which is life-threatening.

The Aedes aegypti mosquito bites during the day, unlike the night-biting mosquitoes most Floridians are accustomed to avoiding. Standard repellents containing DEET, picaridin, or oil of lemon eucalyptus work against Aedes mosquitoes. Eliminate standing water in containers, gutters, and plant saucers around your property — each source can produce hundreds of mosquitoes per week.

Florida health officials note the transmission is concentrated in suburban areas with managed stormwater retention basins, not only neglected urban yards, which means the vector has established breeding in modern residential infrastructure. For guidance on [reducing mosquito breeding at home](https://www.karmactive.com/vector-borne-illness-prevention) and [EPA-approved repellent options](https://www.karmactive.com/epa-repellent-safety-guidelines), see the linked resources.

### White House $810M Rescission Fight: How the 'Pocket Veto' on Spending Works

The White House submitted a package on September 25 seeking to cancel $810 million in already-approved federal funding — and the timing is the central issue. Congress has 45 days to review rescission requests under federal law. The money expires on September 30. The math leaves no room for a vote.

The White House submitted 11 proposed rescissions totaling $810,496,000 to Congress on September 25, citing the Impoundment Control Act of 1974. The largest single item is $567.4 million from HHS's Refugee and Entrant Assistance account. Other proposed cuts include $69.6 million in Education Department international programs, $56.1 million in HUD housing counseling, $27.7 million in the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality, and $8.7 million from the Tropical Forest and Coral Reef Conservation Act program, which funds debt forgiveness tied to conservation investments in tropical forests and coral reefs. **What Is a Pocket Rescission — and Is It Legal?**

Under Section 1012 of the Congressional Budget and Impoundment Control Act, the president may propose rescissions, but Congress must approve them within 45 days or the executive must release the funds. By submitting the package with fewer than 45 legislative days remaining before September 30, the administration creates a situation where the statutory review period outlasts the life of the money — meaning funds lapse into the Treasury before Congress can vote either way.

The Government Accountability Office has previously stated that using impoundment procedures to withhold budget authority through its expiration date is inconsistent with the Act. That position is based on the GAO's reading of the 1974 law and earlier impoundment disputes; it is distinct from the litigation posture of the current 2026 package, which will likely require court action to resolve before the deadline. For background on the [federal budget process](https://www.karmactive.com/federal-budget-process-explained) and [congressional oversight powers](https://www.karmactive.com/congressional-oversight-powers), see the linked explainers.

Beyond the refugee assistance account, the package targets $8.7 million from a program that forgives debt for countries that redirect savings toward tropical forest and coral reef conservation — a smaller item in dollar terms but one explicitly named in the White House submission.

The September 30 fiscal year-end makes this time-critical. By submitting the package fewer than 45 legislative days before that date, the executive branch creates a statutory conflict: Section 1012 requires releasing funds after 45 days, but the appropriation expires before that window closes. D.C. federal courts have issued emergency stays in prior impoundment disputes.

Congressional leaders and advocacy groups representing affected program recipients are expected to seek legal intervention before midnight on September 30. Whether any court acts before the fiscal year closes will determine whether the funds survive. Check back for updates as the deadline approaches.

## STORY 9 — Priority 9th
### Harvest Moon and Saturn Alignment: Best Viewing Times and the Science of the 'Moon Illusion'

The September full moon — the Harvest Moon — reaches peak illumination on the evening of September 26 and rises near Saturn, which is bright enough to see without a telescope. Here is what to look for and when.

NASA's September 2026 skywatching guide confirms the Harvest Moon falls on September 26, near the autumnal equinox. Saturn appears close to the lunar disc and is visible to the naked eye. Neptune is also nearby but requires binoculars or a telescope — it sits around magnitude 8, well below the threshold for naked-eye visibility. The Harvest Moon is the full moon nearest the autumnal equinox, which is what makes its September timing a fixed calendar feature rather than a coincidence.

Skywatchers need no specialized equipment for the Harvest Moon or Saturn. Timing matters more than gear: position yourself with a clear eastern horizon within 30 minutes of local sunset. To resolve Saturn alongside the lunar disc, a pair of 10x50 binoculars or a small amateur telescope will show the planet as a distinct golden oval a few degrees from the moon.

The Harvest Moon looks reddish-orange near the horizon because its light passes through a thicker layer of Earth's atmosphere at that angle than when it is overhead, scattering short blue wavelengths and leaving warm red tones. This process — Rayleigh scattering — is the same one that colors sunsets. Its apparent large size near the horizon is the moon illusion: a neurological effect in which the brain perceives objects as larger when measured against trees, buildings, or other horizon reference points. The moon is not physically larger; a camera without foreground references records the same angular diameter at the horizon as overhead.

The Harvest Moon's traditional significance comes from its timing, not its appearance. Because the full moon nearest the autumnal equinox rises when the ecliptic makes its shallowest angle with the eastern horizon, moonrise happens just 20 to 25 minutes later on successive nights across mid-northern latitudes — compared to the standard lag of around 50 minutes on other nights of the year. Timeanddate.com documents approximately 25 minutes between successive moonrises near New York around this date. Before electric lighting, that compressed delay gave farmers usable post-sunset light for several consecutive evenings during the autumn grain harvest, which is where the name comes from.

For [amateur stargazing basics](https://www.karmactive.com/amateur-stargazing-gear-basics) and an [autumn astronomy calendar](https://www.karmactive.com/autumn-astronomy-guide), see the linked guides.

The Saturn conjunction is specific to this year's positioning. The Harvest Moon itself recurs each September. Next month's full moon rises in October.
