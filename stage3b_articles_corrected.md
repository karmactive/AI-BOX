Plan terminations can also create Special Enrollment Period rights that extend beyond standard AEP timelines — check Medicare.gov or call 1-800-MEDICARE for your specific situation.

## What "Guaranteed Issue" Rights Mean If Your Plan Is Terminated

The distinction between a plan termination and a benefit reduction determines what protections you have.

**Plan termination** — when an insurer exits your county entirely, not just reduces benefits — may trigger Guaranteed Issue rights under federal law. That means you can return to Original Medicare and purchase an eligible standard Medigap supplemental policy without medical underwriting. For a qualifying federal guaranteed-issue event, an eligible Medigap insurer generally cannot use medical underwriting to deny the qualifying policy or charge more because of health conditions. This right is time-limited; for plans terminating December 31, 2026, the window generally runs through 63 days after the termination date. Consult Medicare.gov or a licensed Medicare counselor for the plans available under your qualifying event, as availability varies by circumstance and state.

**Benefit reduction** — the same plan continuing with higher co-pays, stripped supplemental allowances, or a smaller drug formulary — does not trigger Guaranteed Issue rights. You can switch to a different Medicare Advantage plan or a Part D drug plan during AEP, but you cannot purchase Medigap without underwriting.

Your Annual Notice of Change letter outlines every change to premiums, cost-sharing, provider networks, and drug formularies taking effect January 1. Read it before comparing alternatives.

Use the Medicare Plan Finder at medicare.gov — updated with 2027 plan information beginning October 1 — to compare options in your ZIP code. When comparing plans, check whether your current primary care doctor is in-network for 2027 and whether your specific medications appear on the 2027 formulary at an affordable tier. The headline monthly premium is the least useful starting point.

## What "Dismissed With Prejudice" Actually Means in Practice

When prosecutors seek to dismiss criminal charges, the court can grant dismissal either with or without prejudice. A dismissal without prejudice leaves the door open: prosecutors can refile, rework their evidence, or wait for new developments before bringing the same case again. A dismissal with prejudice closes the case permanently. The government cannot refile, cannot seek new indictments on the same facts, and cannot use a different U.S. Attorney to revisit the charges at a later date.

The procedural context matters here. The judge's with-prejudice ruling was not a default outcome — prosecutors had asked for the without-prejudice path, and Edelman rejected it.
