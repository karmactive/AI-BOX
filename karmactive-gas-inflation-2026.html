How a War Thousands of Miles Away Changed Your Gas Bill

Four events. One price spike. Follow the chain reaction.

⚔️
Late February 2026
Iran War Begins
The U.S. and Israel launched military strikes against Iran. Global oil markets responded immediately — Brent crude surged 10–13% within days of the conflict starting.
🚢
March 4, 2026
Strait of Hormuz Closes
The Strait of Hormuz — a 33-km-wide channel carrying 20% of all global oil trade — closed to tanker traffic. The International Energy Agency called the resulting shortfall "the largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market." Production shortfalls reached 11.3 million barrels per day in May.
🛢️
March – May 2026
Oil Climbs Past $120/Barrel
Brent crude surged past $120/barrel following the closure, before easing to around $105/barrel by June. U.S. average gas prices rose more than $1.50/gallon above pre-war levels, reaching $4.48–$4.60 per gallon nationally.
📊
June 10, 2026
BLS Confirms 4.2% Inflation
The Bureau of Labor Statistics released the May 2026 Consumer Price Index: 4.2% year-over-year — the highest reading since May 2023. Energy accounted for more than 60% of the month's price increase. Gasoline alone was up 40.5% over the prior year.

What Comes Next

Key markers to watch in the weeks ahead

⛽
Federal Gas Tax Holiday
A federal gas tax cut (–18.4¢/gal) runs June 1 through October 1, 2026, providing partial relief during peak summer driving season.
🏦
FOMC Meeting: June 16–17
New Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh's first FOMC meeting. Updated economic projections will signal how the Fed intends to navigate energy-driven inflation. Current rate: 3.5%–3.75%.
🚢
Hormuz: Gradual Reopening
The EIA projects Strait of Hormuz flows beginning to resume gradually in Q3 2026. Full normalisation of trade patterns is not expected until early 2027.
📉
May Likely the Peak
Gas prices have already declined slightly in early June. Economists note May could represent the 2026 inflation peak for headline CPI, with a gradual easing expected as supply disruptions ease.
