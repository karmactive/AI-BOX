## STEP 2 — Competitive Coverage Scan & Missing-Points Analysis

**What mainstream/wire coverage (Al Jazeera, CNN, Washington Post, NBC News, Reuters-fed regional outlets, phys.org, CGTN, RTÉ) is already reporting as of 1–2 July 2026:**
- Global ocean hit hottest June on record (~20.98°C C3S monthly average; daily peak 20.86–21.0°C on 21 June, beating 2023/2024 records)
- Marine heatwaves covering roughly 82% of the world's ocean surface
- Mediterranean regional record (98% of the basin in heatwave conditions, 24.3°C)
- El Niño link and Copernicus C3S Director Carlo Buontempo's "uncharted territory" quote
- General mention that more heat/coral bleaching/storm risk lies ahead

**Gaps found in that coverage and folded into the draft (for E-E-A-T/SEO-AEO depth), each backed by a first-hand source below:**
1. The **exact WMO probability numbers and the 2 June 2026 statement** (80% chance of El Niño June–August, rising to 90% thereafter) plus named quotes from WMO Secretary-General Celeste Saulo and UN Secretary-General António Guterres — mainstream coverage mentions El Niño but rarely reproduces the WMO's own figures or officials' words in full.
2. **NOAA's Climate Prediction Center formally upgrading from a Watch to an Advisory on 11 June 2026**, and the **63% probability of a "very strong" El Niño between November 2026 and January 2027** (a threshold that would rank among the largest since 1950) — a specific, actionable data point most general coverage skips.
3. **NASA/JPL's Sentinel-6 Michael Freilich satellite data** (8 June 2026): sea-surface heights over 15 cm above normal across the equatorial Pacific, with JPL sea-level researcher Severine Fournier directly comparing the pattern to conditions before the exceptionally strong 1997 El Niño — an under-reported, highly concrete historical comparison.
4. **NOAA Coral Reef Watch's specific bleaching outlook**: high risk this summer for the northern Pacific (including Hawai'i) and Florida/Caribbean reefs, with the possibility this becomes only the fifth global bleaching event on record, plus Coral Reef Watch coordinator Derek Manzello's point that bleaching is now happening on a near-annual basis, making it increasingly hard to define where one global event ends and the next begins.
5. **The counter-intuitive Atlantic hurricane angle**: despite record ocean heat, NOAA's official 2026 Atlantic season outlook favors a *below-normal* season (55% chance) because El Niño increases wind shear that tears storms apart — a nuance almost entirely missing from general "ocean heat = worse storms" framing.
6. **India/Indian Ocean angle** — largely absent from Western wire coverage: the Arabian Sea is a known marine-heatwave hotspot (first-hand: IMD data), the 2026 southwest monsoon reached Kerala on 4 June, and IMD's own forecast points to below-normal rainfall risk (about 90% of the long-period average, with an elevated chance of a deficient monsoon in northwest/central India) even as El Niño strengthens — directly relevant to KarmActive's audience.
7. **Global fisheries economics**: peer-reviewed analysis found 322 fisheries (15% of those studied) showed statistically significant production declines during high marine-heatwave years, with accumulated global losses since 1985 exceeding 5.6 million tonnes and roughly $6.6 billion — a concrete economic figure rarely cited alongside the temperature-record headlines.
8. **Brief historical framing**: the April 2023–March 2024 sea-surface-temperature jump was independently assessed in a peer-reviewed Nature study as a statistically rare event (return period on the order of centuries under the current warming trend), and 2023, 2024 and 2025 are the three warmest years in NASA's 146-year instrumental record — context that helps readers place a single new record inside a longer pattern rather than treating it as an isolated headline.
9. **Practical, sourced individual guidance** (EPA/NOAA): concrete, non-alarmist steps individuals can take (reducing single-use plastics, choosing certified sustainable seafood, using reef-safe sunscreen, checking bleaching-alert tools before ocean trips) — largely absent from breaking-news coverage that stops at the record itself.

All of the above are woven into the article body as plain factual statements (no "here's what's missing" framing), each backed by a first-hand source.
