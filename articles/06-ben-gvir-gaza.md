## STEP 1: INITIAL DRAFT

Israeli National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir called for conducting nightly military operations in Gaza resulting in 30 to 40 deaths, according to a recorded discussion with former hostage Rom Braslavski released in August. The statements raise questions about military policy and civilian protection in the conflict.

Ben-Gvir stated that operations should not be restricted to imminent military threats. He also reiterated support for Israeli territorial control over Gaza and policies encouraging population departure. These are his advocated positions, not current Israeli government policy or military operations orders.

This distinction matters significantly. Ben-Gvir's role as National Security Minister concerns Israel's domestic security apparatus—police, prisons, and internal security services. He sits on the Security Cabinet that shapes operational policy, but he doesn't command the military. His statements represent his policy views, not automatic military directives.

The statements came during the ongoing conflict in Gaza, which began following October 2023 events. Australia's Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade has stated that civilians, humanitarian workers, UN facilities, and staff must be protected. DFAT also noted that sustained, unimpeded humanitarian assistance must reach Gaza.

In July 2026, the Australian government announced new support for Palestinian recovery, governance, and humanitarian assistance. Earlier that month, Australia joined other nations in describing the humanitarian situation in Gaza as catastrophic, noting that nearly the entire population depends on life-saving services.

The United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) tracks civilian conditions in Gaza. Current data shows significant displacement, food insecurity, and constrained humanitarian access. Deaths and injuries continue to mount. Health systems face severe limitations. The exact figures change continuously as the situation evolves.

International law, specifically the Geneva Conventions, requires military operations to distinguish between combatants and civilians, avoid disproportionate harm, and protect certain categories including children, medical personnel, and the wounded. Civilian casualties in military operations remain one of the conflict's central humanitarian concerns.

Ben-Gvir's statements were made during a period when Israeli political figures expressed varying views on military operations and Gaza's future. Political figures in democracies regularly propose policies that don't become government positions or military operations. His remarks should be understood as his individual advocacy rather than current military doctrine.

The broader humanitarian context is essential. Independent observers, UN agencies, and humanitarian organisations have documented civilian harm, displacement, and medical system collapse in Gaza. The International Committee of the Red Cross and various human rights organisations have raised concerns about protection of civilians and humanitarian access.

Ben-Gvir is one voice within Israeli politics. Prime Minister Netanyahu leads government decision-making. The Security Cabinet includes multiple ministers with varying positions. International diplomacy involves multiple governments, UN bodies, and humanitarian organisations attempting to address the conflict and its humanitarian consequences.

What these statements actually represent is one Israeli political figure's stated position on Gaza military operations and territorial control. They've generated international attention because of his government role and the severity of the proposed operations. But they require careful interpretation: they're his policy proposals, not confirmed military orders or government decisions.

Australia, the United States, European governments, and UN bodies have all called for civilian protection, humanitarian access, and eventual political resolution. These statements reflect the international consensus that the humanitarian situation requires urgent attention regardless of military or political positions.
