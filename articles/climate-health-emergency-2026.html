Published May 30, 2026 · Karmactive
An independent expert commission convened by the World Health Organization's European regional office has formally called on the WHO to declare climate change a "Public Health Emergency of International Concern" — the same designation the world applied to COVID-19. The Pan-European Commission on Climate and Health (PECCH), chaired by former Icelandic Prime Minister Katrín Jakobsdóttir, published its 54-page Call to Action on May 17, 2026. The commission argues that climate change — with its rising toll on heat deaths, expanding infectious disease ranges, food insecurity, and mental health — has long since crossed from environmental concern into a direct, measurable medical crisis.
Climate change is a security threat, a health emergency and an economic time bomb, all rolled into one.
— Dr Hans Henri P. Kluge, WHO Regional Director for Europe

The PHEIC designation — the WHO's highest-tier health alert — has previously been applied to COVID-19, Ebola outbreaks in West Africa, and poliovirus. When declared, it activates mandatory international reporting, emergency funding mechanisms, and coordinated response across WHO member states. The PECCH's central argument is that the current International Health Regulations — designed for time-limited epidemic events — are not equipped for a threat that grows every year without a foreseeable endpoint.

The 2026 Europe Report of the Lancet Countdown on Health and Climate Change, compiled by 65 researchers from 46 institutions tracking 43 health-climate indicators, provides the underlying evidence. It documented that 99.6% of European regions now record more heat-related deaths than they did in the 1990s — a mean annual increase of 52 deaths per million people. Extreme heat warnings have risen 318% from the 1991–2000 baseline. Over one million more Europeans were pushed into food insecurity in 2023 from climate-driven droughts and heatwaves alone.

🗂️ The PECCH's 17 Recommendations Across 4 Domains

The commission's Call to Action asks governments, health systems, cities, and financial institutions to act. Tap each domain to see what's being asked.

🛡️
1. Climate as a Health Security Threat
  • Formally declare climate change a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC)
  • Create a climate-health regional information hub providing countries with trusted, evidence-based resources
  • Treat climate-driven disease spread as an active health security risk requiring emergency surveillance
  • Fund early warning systems for climate-linked health threats before they escalate
🏥
2. Climate-Resilient Health Systems
  • Adapt hospitals and clinics to absorb surges from heat, flooding, and climate disasters
  • Integrate climate projections into national health planning and budgeting
  • Mandate climate-health impact assessments in government health reporting
  • Train health professionals on emerging climate-linked disease patterns and risks
🏙️
3. Local Action in Cities
  • Formalise climate-health mandates for urban and regional networks across Europe
  • Scale up community-level heat action plans and access to cooling
  • WHO to monitor and evaluate climate-health progress at city and country level
  • Build local climate-health capacity in municipalities, especially in high-vulnerability areas
💰
4. Financial System Reform
  • Eliminate fossil fuel subsidies that continue to fund and drive the climate crisis
  • Redirect fossil fuel subsidy savings toward health system adaptation and climate resilience
  • Reform economic frameworks to properly account for climate health costs
  • Increase international climate health finance flows to the most vulnerable regions and communities

The commission's Chief Scientific Advisor, Professor Sir Andrew Haines of the London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine, is one of the world's leading researchers on the intersection of climate and health. The commission's position is that framing climate change as a chronic background problem rather than an acute emergency has systematically resulted in governments underfunding the health system adaptations already needed now — not in 2050.

Research by the commission and the LSE Grantham Research Institute also flagged mental health as a growing but underrepresented dimension of climate's health toll. Climate anxiety, displacement trauma, and the psychological aftermath of repeated disasters are increasingly showing up in clinical settings — yet they rarely feature in government climate planning or WHO health frameworks. Heat-Related Deaths in Europe

Across 854 monitored European cities, 99.6% saw heat deaths increase compared to the 1990s. The Lancet Countdown documents a 318% rise in extreme heat warnings. The most exposed populations: infants, older adults, low-income households, and outdoor workers.
🦟
Dengue — No Longer a Tropical Disease
+297% risk increase in Europe since 1980s 4.6M additional global cases per year from climate change 5M more people entering risk range in Europe each year
The Aedes albopictus mosquito — which carries dengue — is advancing northward at a rate of approximately 5 million people of new exposure per year as European temperatures warm. Dengue was once considered climatically impossible in Europe. That is no longer accurate, and health systems in southern and central Europe are beginning to adapt.
🦟
Chikungunya Spreading Across 40 Countries
400,000+ cases · 2024–2025 155 deaths across 40 countries
Chikungunya — a painful and debilitating mosquito-borne viral illness — spread across 40 countries in 2024–2025, driven in part by climate-driven habitat expansion. Europe's increasingly warm and humid summers are creating conditions for outbreaks previously rare outside tropical regions, with Italy, France, and Spain recording cases.
🦅
West Nile Virus — Italy's 2025 Season
718 human cases in Italy (2025) 49 deaths in Italy alone (2025)
West Nile virus, carried by Culex mosquitoes, thrives in warm, humid conditions. Italy's 2025 season recorded 718 cases and 49 deaths — driven by an unusually warm European summer. The virus is expanding into areas with no historical exposure, and health systems in these regions have had limited preparation time.
🧠
Mental Health — The Underreported Toll
Flagged by PECCH as a growing crisis Rarely addressed in climate policy
Climate anxiety, psychological trauma from repeated disasters, and the stress of displacement are now measurable in clinical settings across Europe. The PECCH identifies mental health as a dimension of climate health impact that current WHO frameworks and most government climate plans largely fail to address. Research also links higher ambient temperatures to increased rates of anxiety, depression, and related presentations.
🌾
Food Security — Climate's Quieter Health Burden
1M+ additional Europeans in food insecurity (2023) Driven by heatwaves and drought
The Lancet Countdown found that over one million more Europeans were in moderate or severe food insecurity in 2023 compared to the 1981–2010 baseline, directly due to climate-driven heatwaves and droughts affecting crop yields. Food insecurity carries its own cascade of health consequences: malnutrition, immune suppression, and increased vulnerability to other diseases.
📅 How the Crisis Escalated to This Point
2016
Lancet Countdown Begins Tracking Climate Health
The Lancet Countdown — now tracking 43 health-climate indicators across 65 researchers and 46 institutions — begins its annual monitoring of how climate change affects human health across Europe and globally.
2023
Warmest Year in 100,000 Years of Recorded History
2023 sets the record for the warmest year in measured history. Heat-related mortality climbs across Europe. Over one million additional Europeans enter food insecurity from heatwaves and droughts. Karmactive covered this record-setting year.
June 2025
PECCH Established by WHO Europe
The Pan-European Commission on Climate and Health is formally established by WHO's European regional office, convening 11 commissioners from across the 53-country region, chaired by former Icelandic Prime Minister Katrín Jakobsdóttir, with Professor Sir Andrew Haines as Chief Scientific Advisor.
Summer 2025
~24,000 Heat Deaths Across 854 European Cities
Nearly 70% of the approximately 24,000 heat-related summer deaths across monitored European cities are directly attributed to climate change. Italy records 718 West Nile virus cases and 49 deaths in that same season. Chikungunya spreads across 40 countries.
April 2026
Lancet Countdown Europe 2026 Report Published
65 researchers from 46 institutions publish the 2026 Lancet Countdown Europe Report, finding extreme heat warnings up 318% from the 1991–2000 baseline, dengue risk in Europe up 297% from the 1980s, and food insecurity climbing.
May 17–18, 2026
PECCH Calls for PHEIC — WHO Launches Advocacy Plan
The PECCH releases its 54-page Call to Action formally asking the WHO to designate climate change a PHEIC. The following day, the WHO launches its own advocacy and communications plan on climate, air pollution, and energy poverty.

Robb Butler, the WHO's special representative for climate and health in the European region, put the scope in concrete terms: approximately 600,000 premature deaths occur annually in the European region from air pollution linked to fossil fuel combustion, and between 60,000 and 65,000 die from heat each year. These are not projections. They are current figures already appearing in clinical and mortality records.

Karmactive has previously covered how air pollution is silently claiming lives and the lifelong health consequences from cradle to grave. The PECCH's call for a PHEIC sits within that broader context — one where the WMO has already sounded the alarm on CO₂ levels and the world is on course for 2.7°C of warming.

The UNRIC also noted the Belgian and broader European expert community's alignment with the commission's position, and the academic community's response to the Lancet Countdown launch underscored the data's weight. The formal PHEIC designation decision rests with the WHO's highest governing bodies. The commission's call, backed by the Lancet Countdown data, is part of an escalating effort to ensure climate's health effects receive the same institutional response as the infectious disease emergencies that triggered the PHEIC framework before.

What This Means in Practice — Right Now

🌡️ Heat Warnings Are Not Routine

Extreme heat health alerts have risen 318% since the early 1990s. They reflect a 30-year mortality trend. When local health authorities issue heat warnings, follow them — especially for children, older adults, and anyone with existing health conditions.

🦟 Mosquito-Borne Disease Is Expanding North

Dengue, chikungunya, and West Nile virus are no longer confined to tropical or Mediterranean regions. Mosquito control around standing water, protective clothing at dawn and dusk, and awareness of symptoms are now relevant across more of Europe than they were a decade ago.

🫁 Air Quality Affects You Today

Around 600,000 premature deaths per year in the European region are linked to fossil fuel air pollution. On high-pollution days, reducing outdoor physical exertion — especially for children and older adults — is a measurable, immediate health protection step.

🧠 Mental Health Is Part of This

Climate anxiety and psychological stress from repeated weather disasters are recognised clinical presentations. Health professionals increasingly treat these as legitimate concerns. Acknowledging the psychological weight of a changing environment is the first step to addressing it.

The Pan-European Commission on Climate and Health published its Call to Action on May 17, 2026, calling on the World Health Organization to formally designate climate change a Public Health Emergency of International Concern. The commission — convened by WHO Europe and chaired by former Icelandic Prime Minister Katrín Jakobsdóttir — presented 17 recommendations covering health security, health system resilience, local urban action, and financial reform. The supporting 2026 Lancet Countdown Europe Report, produced by 65 researchers tracking 43 health-climate indicators, documented increased heat mortality, expanding vector-borne disease ranges, growing food insecurity, and mental health pressure across the European region.
