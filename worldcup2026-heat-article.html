The Heat Scale That Matters

What Is WBGT — and Why Does It Decide Whether Games Are Safe?

Wet Bulb Globe Temperature (WBGT) is the global medical standard for measuring heat stress. Unlike a simple thermometer, it factors in temperature, humidity, sun exposure, and wind — capturing how the human body actually experiences heat during physical exertion.

The Sponsorship Problem Nobody Is Talking About

In 2024, FIFA signed Saudi Aramco — the world's largest oil producer — as a major worldwide partner in a deal reported at approximately $100 million per year, covering the 2026 and 2027 tournaments. Fossil fuel combustion is the primary driver of the climate change that is now making World Cup conditions measurably more dangerous. Researchers estimate the 2026 World Cup will generate over 9 million tonnes of CO₂e — the most polluting tournament in the event's 95-year history. An additional 30 million tonnes CO₂e could be linked to Aramco's promotional activities associated with the deal. Thirty soccer players have formally asked FIFA to end fossil fuel sponsorships. The governing body has not altered its stance.

The Road Here

A Timeline: Climate, Football, and the Heat Warning

1994 — USA World Cup
The Last US-Hosted Tournament — A Cooler Era
Approximately 21 matches played at or near the 26°C WBGT threshold. Heat was a concern, but science had not yet drawn a direct line from climate change to match conditions. Cooling breaks did not exist in tournament rules.
2021 — Qatar Bid & Climate Concerns
Qatar 2022: The First Winter World Cup in History
Qatar moved the tournament from summer to November–December to avoid lethal heat — an unprecedented scheduling shift that underlined how climate was already reshaping sport. Air-conditioned stadiums were built at enormous energy cost.
2023 — FIFPRO Publishes Heat Guidelines
Players' Union Sets the 26°C / 28°C Thresholds
FIFPRO published formal science-based heat safety thresholds for professional football, calling for cooling breaks at 26°C WBGT and match postponement at 28°C WBGT. FIFA's own threshold remained at 32°C — a gap that would grow into a formal dispute by 2026.
2024 — The Aramco Deal
FIFA Partners with the World's Largest Oil Producer
FIFA announced Saudi Aramco as a major worldwide partner in a deal valued at approximately $100 million per year. Over 130 female players had already written to FIFA urging it to reconsider fossil fuel sponsorships. FIFA proceeded regardless.
May 2026 — WWA Report Published
Scientists Formally Warn FIFA: 26 Matches at Risk
The World Weather Attribution group released its peer-reviewed analysis on May 13, 2026, finding 26 of 104 matches face heat strain conditions and 5 face postponement-level heat. Climate change directly attributed. An open letter signed by 20 scientists followed, calling FIFA's 3-minute breaks "impossible to justify."
June 11, 2026 — Tournament Begins
Opening Match: Mexico vs. South Africa at Estadio Azteca
The tournament opens in Mexico City. FIFA's tiered heat mitigation plan — including mandatory 3-minute hydration breaks in both halves — is in effect. Whether it is sufficient remains disputed by the scientific community. 3.5 billion people are expected to watch at least one match.
If You're Going

Protect Yourself at the World Cup — Fan Heat Safety

Players have medical teams, cooling breaks, and climate-controlled benches. Fans outside stadiums and at fan festivals have none of that. Here's what to do.

💧
Hydrate Before You Arrive
Waiting until you're thirsty is too late. Drink 500ml of water in the two hours before entering the stadium. You can bring sealed water bottles into most venues.
🧢
Cover Up — Light Colours, Wide Brim
Light-coloured, loose-fitting clothing and a wide-brimmed hat reduce radiant heat load significantly. Avoid dark synthetic fabrics in outdoor sections.
🌡️
Know the Signs of Heat Illness
Dizziness, nausea, rapid heartbeat, or confusion can indicate heat exhaustion. Move to a cool, shaded area immediately and seek medical help if symptoms worsen.
👴
Higher Risk: Elderly, Chronic Conditions
Older adults, children, and those with cardiovascular, kidney, or metabolic conditions face sharply elevated risk above 28°C WBGT. Plan for indoor breaks and avoid peak afternoon heat.
🏟️
Know Your Venue's Cooling Status
Atlanta, Dallas, Houston, and Vancouver have fully air-conditioned stadiums. Miami, Kansas City, Philadelphia, and MetLife (the final) are open-air — check your city card above before attending.
🌬️
Use Fan Zones With Misting Stations
FIFA has committed to expanded water distribution, shaded areas, and cooling buses at high-risk venues. Locate the nearest cooling zone when you arrive — don't wait until you need it.

What the Study Covered

The World Weather Attribution group's May 2026 study examined all 104 FIFA World Cup 2026 matches across 16 host cities in the United States, Mexico, and Canada, calculating Wet Bulb Globe Temperature risk at each venue based on current climate conditions. The analysis compared findings to the 1994 US World Cup and attributed observed increases in heat stress directly to human-caused climate change. The report found 26 matches at or above FIFPRO's 26°C strain threshold and 5 matches at or above FIFPRO's 28°C postponement threshold — conditions that apply to both players on the pitch and the hundreds of thousands of fans in stadiums and at outdoor fan events. FIFA's response included mandatory 3-minute hydration breaks and tiered stadium cooling measures, while an open letter from 20 scientists described the current protocols as scientifically inadequate. The tournament runs June 11 through July 19, 2026. The final is scheduled at open-air MetLife Stadium in New Jersey.
