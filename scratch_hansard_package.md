- Cause: single-vehicle motorcycle crash on the R109 Lower Road, Strawberry Beds area, between Tinkers Hill and Rugged Lane, Lucan, west Dublin. Emergency services called ~4:30 a.m. He was treated at the scene and pronounced dead shortly after.

- Won the Academy Award for Best Original Song at the 80th Academy Awards (Feb. 24, 2008) with Markéta Irglová for "Falling Slowly." The song later re-charted, peaking at No. 2 in the U.S. after the Oscar win.

- "Once" was adapted into a Broadway musical (opened 2012) that won eight Tony Awards, including Best Musical, at the 66th Tony Awards.

## 2. ARTICLE TEXT

**[INTRO]**

Glen Hansard, the Irish singer-songwriter who fronted the band the Frames and won an Oscar for the song "Falling Slowly," died early Wednesday, July 29, 2026, after a single-vehicle motorcycle crash near the Strawberry Beds in Lucan, on the western edge of Dublin. He was 56.

Emergency services were called to the R109 Lower Road, between Tinkers Hill and Rugged Lane, at around 4:30 a.m. Hansard was treated at the scene but was pronounced dead a short time later. Gardaí said the crash did not involve another vehicle, and a file is being prepared for the coroner.

Hansard and his "Once" co-star Markéta Irglová won the Academy Award for Best Original Song at the 80th Academy Awards, a win the Academy still lists on its [official ceremony record](https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/2008). His death adds to a string of sudden losses in the music business this year, following names such as [Grammy-winning songwriter Brett James, who died in a plane crash](https://cruxbuzz.com/grammy-winning-songwriter-brett-james-dies-plane-crash-north-carolina/).

**[MIDDLE — Career]**

Hansard started out busking on Grafton Street in Dublin as a teenager and formed the Frames in 1990, building the band into one of Ireland's longest-running rock acts. His profile grew worldwide with "Once," a $150,000 film he made with director John Carney in 2006. The movie, in which Hansard played a Dublin busker opposite Irglová, grossed close to $20 million worldwide after premiering at the Sundance Film Festival, where it won the Audience Award.

The film's success carried over to Broadway, where a stage musical based on "Once" opened in 2012 and won [eight Tony Awards](https://www.tonyawards.com/history/year-by-year/2012/), including Best Musical. Hansard's career spanned genres and decades in a way that recalls other songwriters who moved between music and Hollywood, including [Kris Kristofferson, whose six-decade career bridged country music and film](https://cruxbuzz.com/kris-kristofferson-dies-at-88-how-his-60-year-career-redefined-country-music-and-hollywood-but-did-you-know-the-number-of-grammys-he-won/).

**[MIDDLE — The crash]**

The crash happened along a quiet, winding stretch of road that runs beside the River Liffey between Chapelizod and Lucan. Gardaí said the collision did not appear to involve any other road users, so no criminal investigation has been opened, though officers are reviewing CCTV footage and appealing to witnesses, including drivers with dash-cam video, as they prepare a file for the coroner. An early examination of the motorcycle reportedly found little visible damage.

Dublin City Council opened a book of condolence, both in person and online, and mourners lined up within a day of the news breaking. The suddenness of Hansard's death echoed other recent losses in music, including [Grammy-nominated singer Angie Stone, who was killed in a car accident](https://cruxbuzz.com/grammy-nominated-angie-stone-killed-in-car-accident-at-63/).

**[MIDDLE — Tributes]**

Tributes came quickly from collaborators and fellow musicians. Hansard's bandmates wrote on [the Frames' official Instagram account](https://www.instagram.com/theframesofficial/) that "Glen was a force of nature, a troubadour from a different time" who had "that unique ability to bring people together." Bruce Springsteen wrote that "here on E Street, we are heartbroken," calling Hansard "a great musician, a good friend and a generous and gracious man." U2's Bono called him the "voice of the streets" for his years of work supporting homeless outreach in Dublin through the Christmas Eve Busk.

Ireland's president, Catherine Connolly, called Hansard "one of the vital driving influences of the Irish music world." His 2015 solo album, "Didn't He Ramble," had earned him a Grammy nomination for Best Folk Album, an award history recorded on his [official Recording Academy artist page](https://www.grammy.com/artists/glen-hansard/8997).

**[CONCLUSION]**

Glen Hansard died on July 29, 2026, after a single-vehicle motorcycle crash near the Strawberry Beds in Lucan, on the outskirts of Dublin. He was 56. Emergency services were called to the scene shortly before 4:30 a.m., and he was pronounced dead after treatment at the site of the crash. Gardaí said the collision did not involve another vehicle and confirmed that a file is being prepared for the coroner.

Hansard formed the Frames in 1990 and later starred in the 2007 film "Once" with Markéta Irglová. The pair received the Academy Award for Best Original Song for "Falling Slowly" at the 80th Academy Awards in 2008. A Broadway musical based on "Once" opened in 2012 and won eight Tony Awards. His 2015 solo album, "Didn't He Ramble," was nominated for a Grammy Award for Best Folk Album.

Tributes were issued by the Frames, Bruce Springsteen, Bono and Irish President Catherine Connolly, among others. Dublin City Council opened a book of condolence in the days following his death.
