## ARTICLE 1

**Headline:** California Smog Exemption: Governor Newsom Signs "Leno's Law" for Classic Cars

**Meta Description:** California Governor Newsom has signed SB 1392, "Jay Leno's Law," exempting qualifying 1976-1985 classic cars from biennial smog checks under strict mileage caps.

**Schema:** NewsArticle | **Primary Keyword:** California Leno's Law SB 1392

---

California classic car owners who have spent years tracking down discontinued smog parts for late-1970s and early-1980s vehicles now have a legal exemption available for the first time in over 50 years. Governor Gavin Newsom signed Senate Bill 1392 on September 16, 2026, at Jay Leno's garage in Burbank — a law that creates a new smog-check pathway for a specific class of collector vehicles.

SB 1392, known as Jay Leno's Law, exempts qualifying collector vehicles manufactured between model years 1976 and 1985 from California's biennial smog inspection requirement. The exemption is not automatic. To qualify, a vehicle must either be insured under a collector vehicle policy or be driven fewer than 1,000 miles per year, with odometer verification required at registration. Senator Dave Cortese (D-San Jose) authored SB 1392. Newsom signed two companion bills at the same event: AB 2541, which creates a specialized lowrider license plate program, and SB 1069, which fast-tracks approval of aftermarket emissions parts.

For California classic car owners, the signing of SB 1392 eliminates the biennial struggle to locate obsolete smog equipment, provided their vehicle is driven fewer than 1,000 miles per year or registered under a collector policy. Failing to meet these specific mileage or insurance conditions forfeits the exemption immediately, meaning drivers must secure certified collector documentation before canceling their next scheduled inspection.

### Balancing Heritage and Emissions Targets

The law's scope is narrow by design. Collector vehicles represent fewer than 0.1% of California's total vehicle miles traveled, according to the Governor's Office, so the air-quality impact of the exemption is minimal. That matters because California simultaneously announced it has surpassed 2.5 million zero-emission vehicle sales and 216,445 public and shared EV charging ports statewide. The exemption does not conflict with the state's [clean transportation policies](/california-electric-vehicle-adoption-incentives/) because the affected fleet contributes an essentially unmeasurable share of statewide emissions.

SB 1069 removes a separate frustration for restorers — the slow approval process for aftermarket smog components no longer manufactured. AB 2541's lowrider plate carries cultural weight beyond aesthetics, formally recognizing Chicano car culture that has faced decades of discriminatory vehicle profiling. The three bills together adjust [state emissions standards](/national-air-quality-targets/) at the margins without unwinding California's broader clean-air framework.

One enforcement detail most coverage missed: the 1,000-mile default trigger is not a calendar-year grace period. If a qualifying owner exceeds 1,000 annual miles without a collector insurance policy on record, the vehicle immediately reverts to the standard biennial smog requirement at the next registration cycle — not the following year.

**How many miles can you drive a classic car under Leno's Law in California?**
Under California SB 1392, qualifying vehicles manufactured between 1976 and 1985 are exempt from biennial smog checks only if they are insured as collector vehicles or driven strictly under 1,000 miles per year, verified through annual odometer certification during registration.

California's Department of Motor Vehicles is expected to issue administrative guidelines and fee schedules before January 1, when the exemption takes effect. Owners of 1976–1985 vehicles should verify their annual mileage, update insurance to a qualifying collector policy, and monitor for DMV guidance before altering any existing smog inspection or registration plans. Check back for updates as official guidelines are published.

**Headline:** Typhoon Dujuan Nears Tokyo: Flights Cancelled and Heavy Rain Alerts Issued

**Meta Description:** Typhoon Dujuan bears down on Tokyo and the Kanto region, forcing 275+ flight cancellations and train halts during Japan's peak Silver Week holiday travel.

**Schema:** NewsArticle (emergency announcement tags applicable) | **Primary Keyword:** Typhoon Dujuan Tokyo Japan

---

Japan's Silver Week holiday has turned into a travel emergency for millions of domestic and international visitors. Typhoon Dujuan is closing in on Greater Tokyo and the Kanto region, grounding flights, suspending trains, and triggering official landslide and flooding warnings across a stretch of coast where hundreds of thousands of people are mid-journey.

Typhoon Dujuan reached a central atmospheric pressure of 965 hPa as it approached the Japanese coast, with wind gusts exceeding 160 km/h. The Japan Meteorological Agency has issued Level 4 landslide and inundation warnings for the Izu Islands and parts of Kanto, with 200 to 300 millimeters of rainfall forecast across the Tokai and Kanto regions within 24 hours. The northern Izu Islands are under a Level 5 landslide warning. More than 275 domestic flights have been cancelled at Haneda and Narita airports, with international services experiencing delays. JR East and Tokaido Shinkansen services have implemented precautionary slowdowns and selective line suspensions along coastal routes. The storm hit during Silver Week — one of Japan's busiest annual holiday travel periods.

For travelers and residents across Greater Tokyo, Typhoon Dujuan's arrival threatens immediate rail suspensions and airline ground stops during peak Silver Week travel. With over 200 millimeters of rainfall forecast within 24 hours, regional train operators are executing planned precautionary halts. Commuters should verify automated schedule alerts through official transport portals and prepare emergency battery backups before localized suburban drainage systems become overwhelmed.

JMA radar tracking shows Dujuan's outer rainbands generating localized linear precipitation bands — known in Japanese meteorology as "senjo-kousuitai" — over the Izu Peninsula well before the eye reaches central Honshu. This shifts the primary hazard from storm-surge winds to rapid-onset riverbank erosion in suburban watersheds, which can affect areas far from the coast. Warmer-than-average sea surface temperatures in the western Pacific have slowed the storm's dissipation, allowing it to sustain intense rainfall closer to densely populated coastal zones than a typical late-September system. Japan's [extreme weather resilience](/urban-flood-defense-infrastructure/) infrastructure — including Tokyo's Metropolitan Area Outer Underground Discharge Channel — is being put to a severe autumn test.

JR East and coastal rail operators initiated precautionary service throttles before the standard wind-gauge thresholds were reached. That proactive protocol was designed to prevent crowded stations from filling with stranded holiday travelers during an active warning period, rather than waiting for structural triggers that would force emergency stops mid-service.

**Are flights and trains operating normally in Tokyo during Typhoon Dujuan?**
No. Japanese transport authorities and airlines have cancelled over 275 domestic flights at Haneda and Narita airports, with international services experiencing delays. JR East and Tokaido Shinkansen have implemented precautionary slowdowns and selective line suspensions across coastal Kanto and Tokai routes as heavy rain advisories remain active.

The JMA issues updated track and rainfall advisories every three hours. Typhoon Dujuan is expected to clear northeast Honshu into the Pacific within 36 hours. Travelers should check airline and rail operator sites before leaving accommodation and follow local [disaster response protocols](/climate-crisis-readiness/) for shelter locations. Check back as rail networks restore operations.

## ARTICLE 3

**Headline:** Tropical Storm Fay Forms in Atlantic: Path and US Coastal Warnings

**Meta Description:** Tropical Storm Fay has formed in the North Atlantic. The National Hurricane Center confirms the storm will stay offshore but trigger dangerous East Coast surf.

**Schema:** NewsArticle | **Primary Keyword:** Tropical Storm Fay 2026

---

Tropical Storm Fay has formed in the North Atlantic as the sixth named storm of the 2026 hurricane season, and it is not heading toward the US coast. That is the straightforward part. The complication is what the storm is doing to Eastern Seaboard waters regardless of its track.

The National Hurricane Center confirmed Tropical Storm Fay's formation with maximum sustained winds of approximately 45 to 50 mph and a central pressure of 1,002 mb. The system is tracking north-northeast over open water in the central North Atlantic. NHC forecast models show a deep-layer subtropical ridge steering the system toward the open north-central Atlantic and the Azores, keeping it well east of the US mainland. No US territory falls within the current NHC forecast cone. Fay is the sixth named storm of the 2026 Atlantic season, with maximum winds currently below hurricane strength.

Although Tropical Storm Fay remains centered far offshore in the North Atlantic, coastal communities along the eastern seaboard will feel secondary effects. The National Hurricane Center confirms the storm will not make direct mainland landfall, but expanding wind fields are already generating dangerous rip currents and high surf. Beachgoers and commercial maritime operators should heed local marine advisories and avoid unprotected waters.

Fay's ability to maintain organization this far north reflects sea surface temperatures in the North Atlantic running approximately 1.2°C above historical mid-September baselines, according to NHC analysis. Those elevated temperatures have allowed the system to sustain convective activity despite unfavorable upper-level wind shear. The link between [rising sea surface temperatures](/ocean-heating-analysis/) and extended tropical cyclone activity at higher latitudes is part of the documented pattern behind [Atlantic hurricane season trends](/annual-tropical-cyclone-overview/) — though attributing any individual storm to long-term warming requires separate scientific analysis beyond the current advisory.

Fay's interaction with an approaching North American cold front will amplify coastal swell hazards well beyond the storm's geographic position. That swell and rip current exposure hits beachgoers along the Mid-Atlantic and Northeast coast thousands of miles from where the storm center sits — a secondary effect that does not require landfall to be dangerous.

**Is Tropical Storm Fay expected to hit the United States?**
No. According to the National Hurricane Center, Tropical Storm Fay is tracking north-northeast over open waters in the central Atlantic and is forecast to remain offshore without direct impacts to the US mainland, though it may produce hazardous swells and rip currents along East Coast beaches.

The NHC issues updated advisories every six hours until Fay dissipates or transitions out of the tropics. Coastal residents and beachgoers from Florida to the Carolinas should monitor local surf and marine advisories for rip current warnings regardless of the storm's offshore track.
