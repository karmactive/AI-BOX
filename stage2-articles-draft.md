## ATO Is Stopping Credit Card Tax Payments in November — Here's What Small Businesses Need to Know

Australia banned card payment surcharges on October 1. Three days later, the Australian Taxation Office has announced it will stop accepting credit card payments altogether from November 30. For small businesses absorbing one policy change, a second is arriving before they've adjusted to the first.

The ATO's decision affects taxpayers who currently pay BAS, income tax, and other obligations by credit card. From November 30, 2026, the ATO will no longer process credit card payments. Businesses and individuals who rely on credit cards to pay tax bills — particularly during tight cash periods — must shift to BPAY, direct debit, or another accepted payment method before the deadline. The ATO's stated reason is avoiding government absorption of merchant processing fees, which it can no longer pass to the payer under the surcharge ban.

The timing has drawn direct criticism from small business groups. Where previously the ATO could charge a surcharge that offset the card processing cost, it now cannot — and rather than absorb that cost, it is removing the option. The consequence for some businesses is the loss of a short-term financing tool: paying tax on a credit card buys a 30–55 day float on the cash outflow. Without that option, businesses relying on working capital from credit lines to manage BAS cycles will need alternative arrangements in place before November 30.

### What changed on October 1, and why the ATO's move sits on top of it

Karmactive reported on [Australia's card surcharge ban](https://www.karmactive.com/australia-card-surcharge-ban-october-2026/) before it took effect. The Reserve Bank of Australia ban, enforced by the ACCC, prohibits merchants from adding a surcharge for Visa, Mastercard, American Express and eftpos payments. Businesses that previously charged a 0.8–1.5 percent fee at checkout to cover their card processing costs can no longer pass that cost on.

The practical effect is that businesses are now absorbing merchant fees directly. ABC News reports businesses are already adjusting base pricing to account for the lost ability to surcharge. The ATO's separate move adds a different pressure: for tax payments specifically, the government is removing the payment method rather than absorbing the fee.

There is a distinction worth noting for hospitality and service businesses still confused about what the October 1 ban covers. Weekend surcharges, public holiday surcharges, and late-night service surcharges remain entirely legal under Australian Consumer Law — these are service-level charges, not card-processing surcharges. The ban applies only to fees added at payment solely because the customer chose to pay by card. A café charging a 10 percent weekend surcharge is operating lawfully. A retailer adding 1.5 percent at checkout because the customer tapped a card is not.

For tax payments specifically, the November 30 ATO deadline is firm. BPAY is the lowest-friction replacement for most sole traders and small companies — the ATO's own payment reference system supports it and many accounting platforms already auto-populate BPAY details. Structured direct debit arrangements are available for businesses that want to smooth BAS liabilities across a payment schedule rather than meeting them in a single transaction.

For sole traders and small companies, the combined effect of October 1 and November 30 changes requires two immediate actions: update payment terminals and checkout flows to remove card surcharges, and arrange an alternative to credit card payments for any tax liabilities due after November 30. The float that credit card payments provided for cash-strapped businesses during BAS periods disappears in under two months. Setting up BPAY reference codes or a direct debit arrangement with the ATO now avoids a last-minute scramble during what is also a busy Christmas trading period.

**When will the ATO stop accepting credit card payments?**
The ATO will stop processing credit card payments from November 30, 2026. The change affects all credit card tax payments including BAS, income tax, and PAYG instalment obligations. BPAY and direct debit remain accepted. The ATO's reasoning is that it cannot surcharge cardholders under the new RBA rules, and is therefore removing the payment option rather than absorbing processing fees.

The update allows dealers to verify a beneficiary's identity using a camera scan matched against Aadhaar facial data, instead of requiring a fingerprint. NDTV India reported the rollout is active from October 1. UIDAI, which operates the Aadhaar authentication system, confirms that face authentication is a 1:1 biometric match against the Aadhaar database, is consent-based, and is among the alternative authentication mechanisms available to service providers when fingerprint scanning repeatedly fails.

Over 800 million citizens receive food grains under the National Food Security Act. For elderly beneficiaries and manual workers — the groups most likely to have worn or degraded fingerprints — failed biometric scans have historically meant repeated trips to update Aadhaar biometrics at distant enrollment centres, or going without their monthly entitlement. ### How the face authentication process actually works at a ration shop

The face authentication process does not require dealers to purchase new hardware. Existing Android-based e-PoS devices, or the dealer's own smartphone with a compatible camera, can run the Face RD app that connects to UIDAI servers. No separate biometric scanner is needed.

When a beneficiary's fingerprint fails, the dealer can initiate face authentication through the updated e-PoS software. The beneficiary looks at the camera, the system performs a live check, and the result is returned through the UIDAI server. If authentication succeeds, the grain transaction proceeds as normal.

UIDAI's documentation on the process also states that service providers must maintain alternative authentication mechanisms to ensure beneficiaries are not denied their entitlements solely because one biometric method fails. Face authentication is one of those mechanisms — and for beneficiaries who cannot attend the shop due to age or mobility limitations, the mobile camera approach means a dealer can complete authentication at the beneficiary's location rather than requiring physical attendance.

For ration card holders in states where e-PoS software has already been updated, the practical step is straightforward: if your fingerprint is rejected, ask the dealer to use face verification. ## Daylight Saving 2026: Clocks Go Forward This Sunday in NSW, VIC, SA, TAS and ACT

Queensland, Western Australia, and the Northern Territory do not observe daylight saving and make no change.

For the majority of smartphone and computer users, clocks update automatically. Appliances with manual time settings — ovens, microwaves, wall clocks, car dashboards — require a manual adjustment. Daylight saving ends on the first Sunday of April 2027 — April 5. From Sunday, there are five distinct time zones operating across Australia until then. NSW, the ACT, Victoria, and Tasmania run on AEDT (UTC+11). South Australia runs on ACDT (UTC+10:30). Queensland, WA, and NT remain on their standard times.

Clocks go forward. At 2:00 AM on Sunday, October 4, 2026, clocks move to 3:00 AM in NSW, Victoria, South Australia, Tasmania, and the ACT. Queensland, Western Australia, and the Northern Territory do not change.

**Does Queensland have daylight saving in 2026?**
