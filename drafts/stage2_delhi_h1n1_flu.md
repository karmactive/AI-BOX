Delhi is gripped by a flu surge unlike last year's baseline. This season, 1,344 confirmed H1N1 cases have been recorded compared to just 229 for the entire previous year—a sixfold jump in four months.

The spike mirrors monsoon conditions. In 69% of Delhi-NCR households, at least one member reported flu-like symptoms recently, according to ANI data. Health Minister Pankaj Singh confirmed that all 1,344 patients have been treated and discharged. No deaths have been recorded to date.

## Dual Virus Threat

H1N1 is not the only strain circulating. AIIMS Delhi confirmed that H3N2, another influenza subtype, is rising alongside H1N1. The two viruses are co-circulating through the capital.

## Why Now?

Medical experts point to weather. Dr. Sanjeev Sinha at AIIMS said monsoon temperature drops increase virus spread. The cooler, moisture-laden air of the rainy season is classically associated with respiratory infections.

The population most vulnerable includes elderly people, children, and those with chronic illnesses. Dr. Satish Koul of Fortis Healthcare reported seeing 7 to 10 H1N1 patients per day in his outpatient department alone. Dr. Atul Kakar of Sir Ganga Ram Hospital described typical symptoms: high-grade fever, joint and muscle pain, and generalized weakness.

## Healthcare Preparedness

Delhi's health system remains functional. Hospitals are treating cases without reported bottlenecks. Antiviral medications are available. Vaccination status of the outbreak population remains undisclosed.

The DGCA and state health departments have not issued formal travel or gathering restrictions. Standard public health measures—hand hygiene, respiratory etiquette, staying home while ill—remain the advisory baseline.

The monsoon typically lasts through September. If current trends persist, case numbers could rise further before weather patterns stabilize and transmission slows.
