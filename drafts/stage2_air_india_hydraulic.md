An Air India Airbus A320neo dropped 300 feet mid-cruise between Phuket and Delhi on August 4, injuring 17 people. The aircraft, registration VT-EXO, suffered simultaneous failures in all three hydraulic systems—an exceptionally rare event that aviation regulators are now examining.

Flight AI2379 carried 137 passengers and 8 crew members. Among those injured were 4 cabin crew with spinal and neck injuries. The aircraft's automated systems triggered nine technical warnings within one minute of the descent, according to post-flight data.

## What Happened

The A320neo's autopilot disconnected without crew input. Both elevator systems faulted almost simultaneously. All three independent hydraulic systems—designed for redundancy—malfunctioned in the same incident. Stall warnings activated. The pilot applied manual nose-down input to recover the aircraft and regain altitude.

The Directorate General of Civil Aviation classified this as a "Serious Incident" under ICAO Annex 13. The Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau is leading the investigation, with assistance from Airbus and France's Bureau of Enquiry and Analysis.

## Questions Around Pilot Status

The pilot-in-command underwent psychoactive screening. Initial results are described as "requiring further analysis." Both pilots were removed from roster pending further investigation.

Captain Sam Thomas of ALPA India (the Airline Pilots Association's India branch) confirmed that all three hydraulic systems had faulted, calling it an extraordinary failure sequence.

## Corporate Transparency Concerns

Air India initially attributed the incident to turbulence. Subsequent statements from the airline dropped this explanation. The timeline and reasoning behind the shift remain unclear.

The investigation is ongoing. Results may take weeks or months. Regulators will likely examine whether the aircraft's maintenance protocols, design, or other factors contributed to the simultaneous hydraulic failures.

India's aviation safety record and the DGCA's oversight capacity are now in focus. Air India operates over 200 aircraft. The outcome of this investigation could shape how hydraulic systems are monitored across the fleet.
