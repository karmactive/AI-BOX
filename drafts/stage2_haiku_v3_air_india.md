# Air India A320neo Flight VT-EXO: Triple Hydraulic Failure at 6,000 Feet

An Air India Airbus A320neo carrying 183 passengers and crew encountered a rare cascade failure of all three hydraulic systems at 6,000 feet, forcing pilots to execute a manual recovery after autopilot disconnection. The aircraft, registered VT-EXO, sustained damage to both elevator control surfaces. Seventeen people were injured—four crew members and thirteen passengers. The incident has been classified as a Serious Incident under ICAO Annex 13 standards.

The event unfolded in minutes. At 6,000 feet, the flight experienced autopilot disconnection. Within one minute, nine separate warnings appeared in the cockpit. Both elevator hydraulic lines—the primary and backup control surfaces for pitch—faulted simultaneously. All three independent hydraulic systems feeding the elevators became compromised. Stall warnings triggered as the aircraft pitched nose-up. The pilots applied manual control and pushed the nose down, causing the aircraft to descend 300 feet before the crew regained stable flight.

The DGCA, India's civil aviation regulator, opened a Serious Incident investigation. The Accident and Incident Investigation Bureau (AAIB) classified the event as a Serious Incident because it involved the loss of redundancy in critical systems—a condition that, under strict definitions, lies just short of an accident. Airbus and the French aviation authority (Bureau d'Enquêtes et d'Analyses, or BEA) are providing technical assistance to the investigation.

## Hydraulic Cascade: Design or Maintenance?

Airbus A320s are designed with three independent hydraulic systems precisely to prevent scenarios like VT-EXO's. No single failure should disable two systems. Investigators are examining whether the cascade failure originated in aircraft design, maintenance practices, or a combination.

The investigation must answer specific questions. Was there a latent design vulnerability in the A320neo variant that made simultaneous system failure possible? . Had VT-EXO undergone recent hydraulic servicing? Maintenance records for the aircraft will be central to determining whether a service error—contamination in the hydraulic fluid, an incorrectly installed component, or inadequate testing after servicing—triggered the cascade.

If design is implicated, Airbus may issue a Fleet Service Bulletin affecting the global A320neo fleet. The A320neo, launched in 2015, has sold over 11,000 aircraft worldwide. Any design issue would carry global implications.

## The Nine Warnings in 60 Seconds

The cockpit warning sequence offers clues about the failure sequence. Nine warnings in one minute is unusual. Pilots are trained to prioritize warnings and manage cascading alerts, but nine simultaneous or near-simultaneous alerts strain human decision-making under extreme time pressure.

The warnings included stall alerts, autopilot disconnect, and loss of hydraulic pressure indicators. . The rapid succession suggests the failures were not sequential—one system failing, then the next—but rather a common-cause failure affecting multiple systems within a short window.

Investigators will analyze the flight data recorder to map the precise timing and sequence of system failures. The question is whether the failures were truly independent or whether a single point of failure cascaded through hydraulic lines that share common installation zones or pressurization loops.

## Pilot Protocol for Triple System Loss

Air India's Chief Pilot has confirmed that the crew's response—manual control and nose-down descent—followed training protocols for hydraulic emergencies. Pilots are trained to assume manual control if automation fails and to prioritize aircraft stability over altitude management in hydraulic emergencies.

Airbus A320 training emphasizes that loss of both hydraulic systems driving elevators is an unrecoverable situation if all three hydraulic systems are compromised. The manual recovery suggests that at least partial hydraulic function remained available, or that the pilots used alternative control surfaces—rudder trim or other pitch control methods—to stabilize the aircraft.

.

The flight landed safely. This outcome reflects both the robustness of the A320 airframe design and the crew's decision-making. The manual recovery was the critical event. Had the pilots hesitated or had the cascade occurred at a lower altitude with less recovery margin, the incident could have been catastrophic.

## Investigation Into Pilot Screening

One detail in the investigation has drawn attention: the pilot's psychoactive substance screening results are listed as "requiring further analysis." This phrasing is precise. It means the initial screening returned an inconclusive or borderline result.

Psychoactive substance testing in aviation is standard post-incident procedure. The goal is to rule out impairment as a factor in crew performance during the emergency. The phrasing "requiring further analysis" does not indicate a positive result; it indicates that the initial test was ambiguous and requires a second test, often using a different methodology or a more sensitive detection threshold.

.

The testing is not a judgment on the pilot's character or fitness. It is a procedural requirement to establish whether substance use was a factor in the incident. Given the crew's successful manual recovery, psychoactive impairment is unlikely to have been a primary factor, but the investigation must be thorough.

## Passenger Medical Follow-Up

Seventeen injuries were reported: four crew and thirteen passengers. .

Air India has an obligation to ensure that all injured passengers have access to medical evaluation and follow-up care. In cases of head injuries, neck injuries, or internal injuries sustained during sudden aircraft maneuvers, long-term medical monitoring may be warranted. .

Passengers injured in serious incidents are entitled to compensation under international aviation liability rules (the Montreal Convention). The carrier is typically liable for injuries up to approximately 128,821 Special Drawing Rights (SDR), roughly $175,000 USD. .

## A320neo Fleet Safety Directive

The A320neo, despite being a modern aircraft with advanced systems, has been subject to multiple airworthiness directives since its introduction. These directives address issues ranging from engine fire detection systems to landing gear thermal detection.

The VT-EXO incident will likely trigger a review of hydraulic system redundancy across the A320neo fleet. If the investigation identifies a design vulnerability, Airbus will work with aviation authorities to mandate corrective action. This may include:

- Inspection of hydraulic line routing to ensure physical separation between redundant systems
- Enhanced testing protocols for new or overhauled hydraulic systems
- Modifications to pressure relief valves or system isolation logic

.

Any fleet-wide directive will affect operators globally, including Air India, IndiGo, SpiceJet, and other carriers operating A320neo variants. The timeline for investigation completion, diagnosis, and fleet action typically spans several months to a year, depending on the complexity of the issue.

## Maintenance History and Design Robustness

VT-EXO entered service in . The aircraft's maintenance history will be scrutinized for patterns: prior hydraulic servicing events, component replacements, or recurring technical issues flagged by pilots.

Maintenance-induced failures are not uncommon in complex systems. A hydraulic system service that missed a procedural step—such as incomplete system flushing, cross-connection of supply and return lines, or a component installed with incorrect orientation—can trigger latent failures that emerge under high-demand flight conditions.

The three-system redundancy on the A320 was designed specifically to prevent single-point failures from causing loss of elevator control. That all three systems were affected in this incident is the critical anomaly that investigators must explain.

## Outcome and Ongoing Investigation

The aircraft landed without loss of life or catastrophic structural damage. The injuries, while serious, were consistent with rapid maneuvering rather than structural failure. Air India has grounded VT-EXO pending investigation completion.

The DGCA investigation is ongoing. Findings are expected to be released in phases: a preliminary report within 30 days of the incident, followed by a final report that may take several months to complete. The final report will be public and will carry the authority of India's aviation safety agency, with technical input from Airbus and the BEA.

This incident reinforces a key principle in aviation: redundancy in critical systems is essential, but redundancy alone is insufficient if a common-cause failure can disable multiple independent systems simultaneously. The investigation will determine whether VT-EXO's experience reflects a unique combination of circumstances or a systemic vulnerability in the A320neo design or maintenance protocols. The outcome will shape training, maintenance practices, and potential airworthiness directives across a global fleet carrying millions of passengers annually.
