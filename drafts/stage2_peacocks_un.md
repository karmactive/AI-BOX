India donated five peacocks to the United Nations Office at Geneva on August 7, reviving a diplomatic gesture dormant since 1981. The birds—four blue peacocks and one white male—will inhabit the Ariana Park on the UN's 46-hectare grounds after a quarantine period.

Ambassador Arindam Bagchi presented the birds in a formal ceremony. Tatiana Valovaya, Director-General of UNOG, received them on behalf of the United Nations.

## The 1981 Precedent

The gesture echoes a 1981 donation by then-Prime Minister Indira Gandhi, who sent a breeding pair from New Delhi's zoo to the UN. That initial gift established peacocks as a symbol of India's presence at the international stage. The 45-year gap between gifts makes this donation a restoration rather than a new initiative.

## Quarantine and Acclimatization

The peacocks will spend three months in an enclosed habitat on UN grounds before being released into Ariana Park. This quarantine period allows veterinary oversight and ensures the birds adjust to their new environment.

Dr. Tobias Blaha, a veterinarian from Bioparc Genève, oversees the birds' health and adaptation. The park's 46 hectares provide ample space for the peacocks to forage, display, and establish territory.

## Public Engagement

UNOG plans to launch a naming competition for the five birds, inviting public participation and creating a diplomatic connection between the birds and the UN's global constituency. The names will likely reflect cultural significance or international themes.

## Symbolism

Peacocks carry multiple meanings in global diplomacy. For India, they represent the country's natural heritage and biodiversity. Globally, the peacock symbolizes beauty, resilience, and transformation. Placing them at the UN's Geneva headquarters—a hub for multilateral engagement—positions India as contributing to shared international spaces.

The donation also carries conservation symbolism. India maintains several breeding programs for peacocks. Sharing these birds with the UN underscores India's role as a steward of South Asian biodiversity.

## Soft Power Dimensions

Diplomatic gifts serve strategic purposes. India's donation reinforces its presence at the UN and signals continuity with historical engagement. The gesture requires no legislation or major expenditure yet carries symbolic weight in UN corridors where diplomats from 193 countries pass daily.

The timing—during India's broader engagement with multilateral institutions—places the donation within a pattern of India positioning itself as a constructive participant in global governance.

The peacocks will live out their lifespan at Ariana Park. In decades to come, they may become fixtures of Geneva's UN landscape—living reminders of India's connection to the international community.
