The Allahabad High Court declared two women "free to reside at any place and with any person of their own choice" on August 6, ordering their immediate release from unlawful confinement. The women, aged 35 and 20, had been held by their father since 2021 after converting from Hinduism to Islam.

Justice Sandeep Jain heard the habeas corpus petition and found the detention unlawful. The court ruled the confinement constituted "an exceptionally grave violation of constitutional rights."

## Compensation Ordered

The court ordered ₹25 lakh in compensation. Half must be paid by the father, Anil Kumar Bhatia. The remaining ₹12.5 lakh is to be split between a delinquent public servant and the Uttar Pradesh state government.

The order indicts both the father's actions and the state's failure. UP authorities had, despite being notified, permitted the confinement to continue for five years.

## Constitutional Violation

The women's detention violated Article 19 (freedom of movement) and Article 21 (right to life and liberty) of the Indian Constitution. The court did not contest their right to convert. Rather, it centered on bodily autonomy—the principle that no individual can be imprisoned by family for exercising constitutional freedoms.

The judgment addresses a recurring pattern in India: family-led confinement justified by claims of forced conversion or pressure from outside parties. This case establishes that such detention is unconstitutional regardless of the stated rationale.

## Implications

The ruling reaffirms that conversion itself is a protected right. Family members cannot restrain an adult's physical liberty based on religious choice. State inaction in face of reported confinement amounts to state complicity.

The magnitude of compensation signals judicial disapproval of both the family's actions and the state apparatus' failure to intervene earlier. The case is now part of Allahabad HC jurisprudence on bodily autonomy and constitutional rights.
