# Allahabad HC Upholds Religious Conversion: Court Orders ₹25 Lakh Compensation for Illegally Confined Women

## The Ruling: August 6, 2026

The Allahabad High Court has delivered a landmark ruling on religious freedom and bodily autonomy, ordering that two Hindu women—ages 35 and 20—who converted to Islam from their forced confinement. The court declared that the women have the absolute right to choose their faith, and ordered their father, Anil Kumar Bhatia, and the Uttar Pradesh government to jointly pay ₹25 lakh in compensation.

The ruling came in a habeas corpus petition heard by Justice Sandeep Jain on August 6, 2026. The court declared the women "free to reside at any place and with any person of their own choice," explicitly recognizing the primacy of individual religious choice over familial and state authority.

## The Case Background: Years of Unlawful Detention

The two women converted from Hinduism to Islam in 2021. Rather than accepting their decision, their father confined them to their family home, preventing them from leaving or practicing their chosen faith freely. The detention lasted over five years—during which the Uttar Pradesh government, according to the court, "permitted the continuation of their illegal detention."

The habeas corpus petition was filed after the women remained confined without legal justification or judicial oversight. The court's judgment explicitly noted that the state's failure to act on their confinement for years constituted a "dereliction of duty" and a violation of constitutional protections.

## The Compensation Order: A Historic Precedent

The ₹25 lakh compensation order is unusual in two respects:

1. **Joint liability:** 50% from the father (Anil Kumar Bhatia) and 50% from "any delinquent public servant"—meaning if no specific officer is identified, the state itself pays.
2. **Amount:** While compensation in illegal detention cases typically ranges from ₹5-10 lakhs, the court set this award exceptionally high to signal the gravity of the violation.

The court described the confinement as an "exceptionally grave violation of constitutional rights," citing Articles 14 (equality), 21 (personal liberty), and 25 (freedom of religion) in its judgment.

## The Broader Legal Framework

India's legal position on religious conversion is governed by:

- **Article 25 (Constitution):** Freedom of conscience and free profession/practice of religion
- **Freedom of Religion Acts (state-level):** Anti-conversion laws in certain states, though UP does not have a stringent anti-conversion law
- **Section 366 (IPC):** Criminalizing kidnapping/abduction
- **Section 342 (IPC):** Criminalizing wrongful restraint
- **Habeas Corpus jurisprudence:** Courts have consistently held that adults cannot be confined against their will

The Supreme Court's landmark 2017 ruling in *Shafin Jahan v. K.M. Ashokan* established that individual choice—not parental or community consent—governs marriage and religious decisions for adult citizens.
