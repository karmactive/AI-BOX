## The nine-warning cascade in one minute

- **Sensor cascade**: A single electrical fault triggered false pressure readings across three isolated systems. Redundancy failed to isolate the fault.

## Regulatory accountability and transparency gaps

Three questions for DGCA:

1. **Initial claim accuracy**: News reports cited "turbulence" in early statements. How was this determined before flight data review? Did this delay recognition of the actual system failure?

2. **Maintenance standards enforcement**: India's domestic aircraft maintenance is outsourced across multiple facilities (Air India Engineering Services, third-party MROs, contracted technicians). Does DGCA audit the same rigor at each facility?

3. **Regulatory capacity**: Does DGCA have sufficient in-house expertise to independently validate Airbus's findings, or does it rely entirely on manufacturer analysis?

## What comes next

DGCA's formal Serious Incident report should include:

- Root cause determination (sensor, hydraulic, maintenance, design)
- Any fleet-wide service bulletins or airworthiness directives from Airbus
- Pilot training recommendations for triple-system failure scenarios
- Maintenance procedure changes (if applicable)
- Timeline for public release of findings

For passengers: long-term health monitoring and transparent communication of findings. For pilots: clear, unambiguous guidance if this was a training scenario waiting to happen. For regulators: proof that oversight exists, more than after-the-fact investigation.

The nine warnings in one minute will tell investigators which system failed first. That will determine whether this was a maintenance lapse, a design vulnerability, or a rare convergence of faults. Until DGCA publishes findings, the fleet flies with incomplete understanding of the risk.
