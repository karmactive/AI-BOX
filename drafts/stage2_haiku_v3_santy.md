# 11-Year YouTube Channel Deleted as Creator Faces Threats Over Reservation Commentary

An 11-year YouTube channel operated by content creator Santy Sharma was deleted from the platform in early August 2026. The deletion followed weeks of online threats targeting Sharma for commentary about India's reservation system. Sharma is also known for an appearance on the reality television program Bigg Boss.

The channel had accumulated content over a decade. . The deletion was sudden and left viewers and archive systems without access to the channel's library.

YouTube's policy allows creators to appeal account or channel terminations. .

On an unspecified date in early August, Santy Sharma held a press conference at Andheri West in Mumbai. The press event was titled "Arakshan Hatao — August Kranti" (End Reservation—August Movement). .

## Online Threats and Moderation

Threats directed at Santy Sharma preceded the channel deletion. .

The threats appear to be connected to Sharma's commentary on India's caste-based reservation system, which allocates a percentage of government positions and educational seats to historically marginalized groups. .

Meta, the parent company of Facebook and Instagram, maintains moderation policies prohibiting targeted harassment and threats. .

## YouTube Moderation and Policy

YouTube's Community Guidelines prohibit threats of violence and harassment. The platform uses a combination of automated systems and human review to enforce these policies.

.

A critical question is whether YouTube deleted Santy Sharma's channel because Sharma's own content violated policies or because third-party threats against Sharma made the channel a moderation burden. .

Content creators' rights in platform moderation remain contested. If a creator's channel is deleted because others are threatening the creator, that raises concerns about de facto censorship—the platform silencing the creator to eliminate the moderation problem posed by threats against them.

## Systematic Threats Against Reservation Commentary

The targeting of Santy Sharma may reflect a broader pattern of online harassment against individuals who publicly discuss India's reservation system.

Reservation is a constitutionally protected affirmative action program that generates ongoing debate. Some voices argue reservations are necessary to remedy historical discrimination; others argue they should be reformed or phased out. Both positions are legitimate political speech protected by Article 19(1)(a) of the Indian Constitution (freedom of speech).

.

Coordinated harassment campaigns—sometimes called "pile-ons"—use social media to concentrate negative attention on a target. Algorithms that amplify engagement can accelerate pile-ons.

.

## Police FIR and Cybercrime Investigation

Threats of violence are criminal offenses under Indian Penal Code Section 506 (criminal intimidation) and Section 507 (criminal intimidation by an anonymous communication or through a third party).

.

## Digital Rights and Silencing Effects

Digital rights advocates have flagged a pattern in platform moderation: creators facing harassment sometimes have their platforms revoked, and the creators—not the harassers—bear the consequence.

.

The issue is distinct from simple "both-sidesism." The question is whether platforms should remove harassing content and suspend accounts of users issuing threats, or whether platforms should instead remove the targeted creator's channel as a way to reduce moderation burden.

Deleting Santy Sharma's 11-year archive does not address the threats. It removes a public voice from the platform.

## Santy Sharma's Public Role

Santy Sharma appeared on Bigg Boss, India's reality television adaptation of the international franchise. .

Reality television participation gives Sharma a platform and an audience. Commentary about public policy—including reservation—is Sharma's right. Online harassment for that commentary is a problem; but deletion of Sharma's entire channel is not a solution.

## Conclusion

The deletion of Santy Sharma's 11-year YouTube channel leaves open questions about platform moderation, creator protection, and the relationship between harassment and content removal. YouTube and Meta have policies addressing harassment and threats. Whether those policies are being applied to protect creators facing coordinated harassment—or whether platforms are instead silencing targeted creators to reduce moderation complexity—requires investigation.
