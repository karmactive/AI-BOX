India's Parliamentary Standing Committee on Communications and IT issued a 3-day ultimatum to Meta founder Mark Zuckerberg on August 2, demanding an "unqualified apology" after the platform removed Prime Minister Narendra Modi's Facebook post.

The post was offline for 5 to 6 hours. Meta blamed automated moderation. The committee, chaired by BJP MP Nishikant Dubey, rejected this explanation and escalated the matter.

## The Ultimatum

The committee threatened to withdraw Meta's Section 79 safe harbor exemption under India's Information Technology Act if Zuckerberg did not comply. Safe harbor protection shields platforms from liability for user-generated content. Losing it would expose Meta to legal action over every post on its network—a regulatory catastrophe.

The committee signaled that compliance is not optional. The tone and timeline made clear that the government views Meta as accountable for how its systems treat India's highest office.

## Broader Concerns

The committee's demands extended beyond the Modi post removal. Three additional issues frame the standoff.

First: child sexual abuse material (CSAM). The committee flagged Meta's handling of content depicting child exploitation. Second: deepfakes. Third: content demeaning women. All three areas remain under scrutiny.

## Government Engagement

Meta took the threat seriously. The company dispatched its Chief Global Affairs Officer, Joel Kaplan, to India on August 5. Kaplan met with Electronics & IT Secretary S. Krishnan. A meeting with IT Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw was scheduled.

These high-level engagements signal Meta's assessment that India's regulatory stance is hardening. The company cannot afford sustained conflict with the Indian government.

## Legal Limits on Committee Power

The Parliamentary Standing Committee's recommendations are not legally binding. The final decision on Section 79 withdrawal rests with the government under the IT Act. However, the committee's voice carries political weight. Government officials are unlikely to ignore a formal recommendation backed by parliamentary authority.

Meta's next move will determine whether the crisis escalates. An apology acceptable to the committee could restore the relationship. Refusal would likely trigger formal government action.
