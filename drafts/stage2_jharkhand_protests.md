Students in Jharkhand launched a sustained protest against alleged irregularities in the 14th JPSC Civil Services Examination, demanding a complete cancellation and a CBI probe. Police responded with water cannons, tear gas, and lathi-charges, arresting 19 people and hospitalizing at least one protest leader.

The movement centers on exam integrity. Students allege systematic fraud in the JPSC and JSSC recruitment exams that determine access to civil service jobs—the gateway to stable government employment in the state.

## The Leadership's Cost

Devendra Nath Mahto, a leader of the JLKM (Jharkhand Loktantrik Morcha), undertook a 9-day hunger strike in solidarity with the student demands. Police response to the protest included a lathi-charge. Mahto was hospitalized.

The physical toll was documented: he lost 10.5 kilograms during the fast. His blood glucose dropped to 53—a dangerously low level requiring immediate medical intervention. His hospitalization marked an escalation in police tactics against the protest.

## Police Response

Water cannons drenched protesters. Tear gas canisters were deployed. Lathi-charges injured multiple people. Nineteen protesters were arrested. The police response to a non-violent student demonstration signaled the state's intolerance for public criticism of its recruitment process.

## Broader Context

Jharkhand's population includes over 28% Adivasi (tribal) people. Reservation policies for Scheduled Tribes and Scheduled Castes shape civil service recruitment, making exam integrity a matter of social justice. Alleged irregularities threaten these protections.

The protests began on July 25 and sustained momentum through early August. On August 11, the BJP announced a state-wide bandh (shutdown) in response. Three JPSC members resigned during the controversy, though the reasons for their resignations remain unclear.

## What Students Want

The demands are specific: cancel the 14th JPSC Civil Services Exam entirely. Launch a CBI investigation into alleged irregularities. The students argue that proceeding with a compromised exam would reward fraud and disadvantage legitimate candidates.

## State Response

The Jharkhand government has not formally addressed the substantive allegations. Official statements have focused on maintaining "law and order," casting the protests as disruptions rather than engaging with the integrity claims.

Recruitment exam credibility is foundational to meritocracy. If allegations of irregularities prove true, the state's civil service could be staffed through patronage rather than qualification. The student movement's persistence suggests they view this as non-negotiable.
