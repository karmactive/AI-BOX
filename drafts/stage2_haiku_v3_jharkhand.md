# Jharkhand Exam Cancellation Sparks Protest, Hunger Strike, and Police Action

Jharkhand's Public Service Commission (JPSC) and Staff Selection Commission (JSSC) announced the cancellation of the 14th Combined Civil Services Examination (JPSC) following accusations of irregularities in the examination process. The cancellation triggered weeks of sustained protest and escalated to police intervention.

The core issue: Students and job aspirants accused the exam administration of systematic irregularities that compromised test integrity. The precise nature of these irregularities .

## Timeline and Escalation

Protests began on July 25, 2026. By August 11, the conflict had persisted for 18 days with escalating intensity.

One prominent protester, Viral Mahto, became the public face of the movement. On August , Mahto began a hunger strike to demand investigation and remediation. The hunger strike was expected to apply pressure on the government to respond to protester demands.

Mahto's health deteriorated during the strike. After nine days without food, Mahto was hospitalized. Medical records documented:

- **Weight loss of 10.5 kilograms** (approximately 23 pounds)
- **Blood glucose level: 53 mg/dL** (critically low; normal fasting range is 70-100 mg/dL; hypoglycemia below 54 is considered severe)

Hospitalization interrupted the hunger strike but reinforced the protest movement's narrative: the government was sufficiently callous about job aspirants' concerns that a protester had to risk his life to demand investigation.

## Police Response: Water Cannons, Tear Gas, and Lathi Charge

On August , police moved to disperse protesters using force:

- **Water cannons** to separate crowds
- **Tear gas** to disperse assembly
- **Lathi charge** (police wielding batons to strike protesters)

Nineteen people were arrested during this action. .

Lathi charges against student protesters raise questions about proportionality and police discretion. Students protesting job examination irregularities are not engaged in violence or destruction; they are exercising the right to peaceful assembly. A forceful police response may escalate tension rather than resolve the underlying dispute.

.

## Viral Mahto's Role and Medical Status

Viral Mahto's hunger strike made him a focal point for media coverage and public sympathy. A young person willing to endure severe deprivation to fight for transparency in civil service exams resonates emotionally with job-seeker communities.

His hospitalization due to hypoglycemia—a life-threatening condition—elevated the stakes. Mahto became a symbol of government indifference to legitimate grievances.

.

The medical records—weight loss of 10.5 kg, glucose of 53—are precise and verifiable. They create a documentary record of the protest's human cost.

.

## Exam Irregularities: The Core Issue

The government's decision to cancel the 14th JPSC exam acknowledges that irregularities occurred. The unanswered question is: What went wrong, who is responsible, and how will it be corrected?

.

If question papers were leaked before the exam, candidates who obtained leaked papers had an unfair advantage. If answer keys were incorrect or changed after the exam, grading would be invalid. If administrative processes were bypassed, the entire examination's credibility is compromised.

The remedy for exam irregularities typically involves:

1. **Cancellation** (as occurred here)
2. **Investigation** (JPSC announced this)
3. **Disciplinary action** against officials responsible
4. **Re-examination** for all candidates or affected cohorts
5. **Possible compensation** for candidates who invested time and resources in preparation

.

## CBI Investigation

The government announced a Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) probe into the exam irregularities. .

The CBI is India's federal investigative agency, typically involved in high-profile corruption cases. Its involvement shows that the government acknowledges the seriousness of the allegations—that irregularities may constitute criminal corruption rather than administrative error.

.

.

If Jharkhand's commissions have a pattern of problems, it suggests systemic resource constraints, inadequate oversight, or institutionalized corruption. If this is an isolated incident, it may reflect one group of officials' malfeasance in one exam administration cycle.

## BJP Bandh and Political Dimensions

On August 11, the BJP (Bharatiya Janata Party) announced a bandh (general strike) in Jharkhand related to the exam cancellation and police response. .

The entry of political parties into the dispute can either amplify protest or complicate resolution. If political actors use the issue to score points against the current government, resolution becomes entangled with electoral politics.

## Impact on Job Market

The cancellation and re-examination delay impose costs on job aspirants. .

Young people preparing for civil service exams often have limited income and face family pressure to secure government employment—a path to stable, pensioned work. Exam delays and cancellations extend the limbo period and impose financial stress on aspirants' families.

Labor market impact depends on how many positions are affected and how long the re-examination delay extends. .

## Adivasi-Majority Context

Jharkhand is an Adivasi-majority state (Adivasi means "original inhabitants," referring to India's tribal communities). Civil service recruitment in Jharkhand carries significance for Adivasi communities seeking equitable access to government employment.

.

Allegations of exam irregularities in an Adivasi-majority state's civil service commission carry heightened political salience if Adivasi candidates are excluded or disadvantaged through corruption.

## Government's Broader Response

.

Silence or delay in government response tends to prolong protest. Clear communication of investigation progress and remedial steps can help de-escalate.

## Conclusion

The cancellation of Jharkhand's 14th civil service exam, the attendant student protests, and the police intervention reflect a crisis of confidence in public institutions. A young protester's hunger strike and hospitalization for severe hypoglycemia symbolize the stakes for job aspirants. The CBI investigation shows that the government acknowledges seriousness, but the absence of clear remedial steps—new exam timeline, accountability for officials, compensation—leaves underlying grievances unresolved. The BJP bandh indicates political polarization. Jharkhand's exam crisis will not be fully resolved until investigation findings are released and a transparent remedial pathway is established.
