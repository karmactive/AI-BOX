Parliament passed legislation amending India's payment systems taxation framework, modifying Section 10A of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007. The Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Bill will enable taxing certain UPI transactions.

Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman made two commitments in her floor statement: "UPI will remain free for consumers" and "the vast majority of merchant transactions would remain free." These assurances frame the tax as targeted rather than universal.

## Opposition and Walkout

Opposition parties rejected the justification. CPI(M) and AAP lawmakers staged a walkout from the Rajya Sabha, accusing the government of subservience to US directives. They argued the tax represents capitulation to foreign pressure on India's digital payment system—a nationalist critique questioning whether India's financial sovereignty is being compromised.

The specific allegation was that the tax reflects pressure from the United States. The opposition offered no evidence but signaled deep mistrust of the government's rationale.

## Merchant and Small-Trader Impact

Small merchants rely heavily on UPI for low-cost transactions. The government's promise that "the vast majority" would remain untaxed leaves open the question of which transactions would be taxed. The definition of "vast majority" was not clarified in floor statements.

UPI's explosive growth—from marginal adoption in 2016 to over 100 billion annual transactions by 2024—rests partly on its zero cost to consumers and near-zero cost to merchants. Any tax that applies to certain transactions could alter adoption patterns, particularly for small traders operating on thin margins.

## Implementation Timeline

The bill passed without amendments. Implementation details remain pending. The government will likely issue rules clarifying which transactions attract tax and at what rate.

Digital payment adoption is critical to India's financial inclusion agenda. The tax raises a fundamental tension: whether taxation should apply uniformly or be calibrated to preserve system accessibility. Small traders and informal-economy participants will feel the impact most acutely.

## Concurrent Legislation

The Rajya Sabha also passed the Bankers' Books Evidence Bill during the same session, though amid the broader opposition walkout over UPI taxation.

The outcome signals that the government prioritizes revenue collection over framing UPI as a purely free public good. How the rules implement this taxation will determine whether the system remains accessible to India's poorest users.
