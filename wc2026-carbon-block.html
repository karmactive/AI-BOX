Three host nations spanning thousands of miles mean that fans following multiple matches face some of the longest internal travel distances of any World Cup. Unlike Europe or a compact Gulf state, North America's geography makes low-carbon travel between cities extremely difficult.

Beyond the stadium footprint, analysts point to FIFA's commercial relationship with Saudi Aramco as a significant multiplier on the tournament's total climate impact.

FIFA signed Saudi Arabia's state oil company Aramco as a top-tier global sponsor. Critics estimate the promotional association adds approximately 30 million additional tonnes of CO₂e when accounting for continued fossil fuel production enabled by the revenue and visibility — more than three times the tournament's own direct footprint.

What Fans Can Do

Your Choices Shape the Final Score

Individual action won't fix a systemic problem — but 3.5 million attending fans make collective choices that add up. Tap each card.

🚂 Choose Train Over Plane Tap to learn more

Rail between cities like New York, Philadelphia, Boston and even Chicago is viable. A return train trip emits roughly 90% less CO₂ than the equivalent flight.

Up to 0.5 tonnes saved per trip
🚗 Group Travel & Share Tap to learn more

Sharing a vehicle between four fans cuts each person's road travel emissions by 75%. Shared accommodation in one apartment vs four hotel rooms reduces the hospitality footprint significantly.

75% cut when sharing a car of 4
📍 Pick Clustered Cities Tap to learn more

Northeast clusters (New York, Philadelphia, Boston) or West Coast cities (LA, San Francisco, Seattle) keep inter-match travel short. Avoid itineraries that zigzag the continent.

Avoid 3,000+ mile cross-country hops
🌱 Verified Carbon Removal Tap to learn more

Skip cheap offset credits. Verified carbon removal programmes (biochar, enhanced weathering, direct air capture) actually remove CO₂ permanently — not just avoid emissions elsewhere.

Removal > offsets. Check certification.
