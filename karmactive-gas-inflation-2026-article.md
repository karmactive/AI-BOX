**The Strait of Hormuz and Your Gas Bill**

The current energy spike traces directly to the 2026 Iran war. In late February, U.S. and Israeli military strikes against Iran triggered the conflict. By March 4, the Strait of Hormuz — a 33-kilometre-wide channel through which roughly 20% of all global oil trade moves — was effectively closed. The International Energy Agency described the resulting shortfall as "the largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market." By May 2026, production shortfalls were averaging 11.3 million barrels per day.

The national average for regular unleaded gasoline hit approximately $4.48–$4.60 per gallon in May 2026, more than $1.50 above pre-war levels. For a typical American household, that translates to roughly $857 more spent on gasoline over the course of 2026. Americans are on track to spend approximately $213 billion on gasoline between May and August — around $59 billion more than the same period in 2025.

**Who Is Absorbing the Cost**

The burden does not fall evenly. Research shows households earning less than $40,000 cut their gasoline consumption by 7% — and still paid 12% more on fuel because driving, for most people, is not optional. Higher-income households earning over $125,000 saw their spending rise 19%, but their budget flexibility is significantly greater.

The behavioral shift is visible beyond the fuel gauge. At more than $4 per gallon, consumers begin to change habits: Walmart and Sam's Club members have been purchasing fewer than 10 gallons per fill-up — the lowest per-trip volumes since 2022. Fast-food chains have reported losing customers in the under-$45,000 income bracket, a sign that $4+ gas is functioning as a financial tipping point for lower-income households. These patterns echo what occurred during <a href="https://www.karmactive.com/global-energy-on-edge-unpacking-the-2021-2023-crisis-and-its-unprecedented-impact/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">the 2021–2023 energy crisis</a>, when fossil fuel price volatility first exposed how exposed gasoline-dependent households remain to geopolitical events thousands of miles away.

**An Unusual Domestic Split**

While American consumers pay more at the pump, the United States is simultaneously one of the few countries benefiting from elevated global oil prices. U.S. crude and petroleum product exports rose to nearly 12.9 million barrels per day in late April 2026 — a record. America's position as the world's largest oil producer means its energy sector is realising substantial revenue from the same price surge that is straining household budgets, creating a domestic divide between energy producers and everyday consumers that rarely features in coverage of this inflation report.

**The Federal Reserve at a Turning Point**

The June 16–17 Federal Open Market Committee meeting will be the first under new <a href="https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260429a.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh</a>, who was sworn in on May 22. It will also include an updated Summary of Economic Projections — markets will be reading it closely for early signals on monetary policy direction. The Fed held rates at 3.5%–3.75% at its April meeting, citing the energy-driven nature of the spike and a softening labour market. Core CPI at 2.9% gives the Fed room to hold steady, at least for now.

**Partial Relief Already in Place**

Congress authorised a Federal Gas Tax Holiday from June 1 through October 1, 2026, which removes 18.4 cents per gallon from the federal fuel tax during peak summer driving season. The <a href="https://budgetmodel.wharton.upenn.edu/blog/BE2026-3/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Penn Wharton Budget Model</a> has analysed the measure's fiscal impact.

**What the EIA Expects**

The <a href="https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">EIA's June 2026 Short-Term Energy Outlook</a> projects the Strait of Hormuz will remain effectively closed into early summer, with a gradual resumption of flows in the third quarter of 2026. A return to pre-conflict trade patterns is not expected until early 2027. Brent crude is forecast to average around $105/barrel in June and July before easing. Gas prices have already declined slightly in early June, suggesting May was the inflation peak for this cycle.

**The Electric Vehicle Difference**

One detail largely absent from coverage of this CPI report: electric vehicle drivers are mostly shielded from this inflation spike. Based on national average residential electricity costs and fleet efficiency data, fuelling an EV carries the equivalent cost of approximately $1.67 per gallon — compared to $4.48+ at the pump for gasoline vehicles. The <a href="https://www.karmactive.com/evs-vs-gas-guzzlers-epa-report-exposes-stark-auto-industry-reality/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">cost advantage over gasoline-powered vehicles</a> becomes most measurable in precisely these moments. Data from Edmunds shows EV consideration climbing among car shoppers as gas prices rise, though broader economic pressures are limiting immediate purchasing shifts.

**The Global Dimension**

The inflation story extends well beyond U.S. borders. The <a href="https://www.imf.org/en/blogs/articles/2026/04/14/war-darkens-global-economic-outlook-and-reshapes-policy-priorities" target="_blank" rel="noopener">IMF's April 2026 World Economic Outlook</a> downgraded global growth to 3.1% for 2026, with headline global inflation rising to 4.4%. Commodity-importing developing nations with limited foreign currency reserves face impacts nearly twice as severe as advanced economies. For those countries, the Strait of Hormuz disruption is not a line item on a monthly budget — it is a macroeconomic stress event affecting food, transport, and essential services.
